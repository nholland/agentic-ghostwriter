## Coherence Checks

### Voice Consistency

All five chapters hold the voice spec well: short declarative landings after
longer build sentences, no corporate/therapy-speak, Stoic terms explained on
first use (*phantasia*, *prohairesis*, *hegemonikon*, equanimity, *parrhesia*,
*praemeditatio malorum*), and the author's own mistakes staged in real time
rather than narrated from a place of having already figured it out.

**[RESOLVED] Ending structure.** Ch1 closed with a Marcus Aurelius quote plus
a one-sentence echo ("Use it."), per the voice spec's original "end with
someone else's line and a one-sentence echo" rule. Ch2–5 instead each close
on the author's own image, with no terminal quote. This was a live
inconsistency between the voice spec and the chapters as actually written.
**Resolution (2026-06-12):** the author chose the Ch2–5 pattern as the house
standard. `01-voice.md`'s "Never wrap up with a moral" rule was rewritten:
chapters now close on the author's own image; Stoic quotes do their climactic
work mid-chapter, not in the final lines. Ch1 remains on the old pattern — it
was refined earliest and is the Part I opener; revisiting it is optional and
non-blocking. The rule change is forward-looking, not a mandate to re-edit
Ch1.

**Em-dashes inside direct quotations.** Three confirmed-genuine translator
em-dashes exist in quoted Stoic passages: Ch3's Marcus 11.18, Ch5's Marcus
9.28, and Ch5's Seneca Ep. 75 (Gummere). **Resolution (2026-06-12):**
`01-voice.md`'s no-em-dash rule now carries an explicit exception for direct
quotations from published Stoic translations, gated on confirmation via
`sources/citation-manifest.md`. Em-dashes in the author's own prose remain
prohibited.

### Arc Integrity

Part I delivers on its structural promise. Ch1 establishes the gap and the
four-virtues frame (logical, kind, self-controlled, brave enough to stay
engaged). Ch2–4 each take one piece of "what goes in the gap" (inner weather,
listening, anger), and Ch5 brings all of it together under real conflict,
closing with an explicit Part II handoff ("Part II asks what happens when the
weight isn't distributed evenly..."). The four-virtues frame from Ch1
resurfaces naturally in Ch3 ("The fourth virtue from Chapter 1 shows up
here...") without over-signposting — consistent with parking-lot #1's
resolution (background architecture, not explicit signposting).

**Framework-lens check (Oak/River/Sun × Four Virtues, `05-framework.md`):**
the author asked whether Ch1–5 still rhyme with the framework now that it's
locked in. They do, strongly. Each chapter sits cleanly in its assigned
cell — Ch1 = River×Wisdom ("The Script"), Ch2 = River×Temperance ("The Mood
Mirror"), Ch3 = River×Justice ("The Closed Door"), Ch4 and Ch5 =
River×Courage ("Stonewalling / Combustion") — without naming any of it. Two
chapters resonate especially strongly: Ch1's "What Got Installed" section
dramatizes "The Script" failure mode almost exactly (a man running an
inherited pattern he never examined), and Ch3's literal closed-door image
("the door is either open or it isn't") makes "The Closed Door" cell
concrete without ever naming it. The matrix is doing its job as invisible
architecture: present in each chapter's bones, absent from its language. No
drift, and no chapter feels assigned-by-formula. This gives good grounds for
continuing to use the framework as Part II begins.

## Strengths to Protect

1. **"Stoicism isn't armor" as a recurring thread, not a single statement.**
   Ch1's "The Mistake I Made First" ("My wife thought I'd gone somewhere she
   couldn't reach... That's not equanimity... That's a philosophical excuse
   to disappear") and Ch2's "corrective" section both dramatize this in
   scene, setting up the book's central argument early and concretely. Don't
   let later chapters restate this abstractly — keep paying it off in scenes,
   the way Ch1 and Ch2 do, through to its full statement in Ch14.

2. **Ch5's capstone recap.** "This is the last chapter of Part I because it's
   where all five chapters apply at once" — naming the integration
   explicitly, once, at the start of the chapter that does the integrating.
   This is the right amount of signposting. Don't add more of it elsewhere.

3. **"The Four D's" naming (Ch3).** Concrete, memorable, and reused correctly
   in Ch4 and Ch5 without re-explanation. This is the model for how named
   concepts should travel through the book: defined once, referenced freely
   afterward.

## Framework Visibility (author's question)

The author asked whether the book over-ties back to Oak/River/Sun, and
proposed handling it in the Introduction, plus a possible italicized epigraph
under each chapter title tying back to the concept.

**Recommendation:** the current practice already matches the goal — the
matrix is fully internal, and Ch1–5 never name it (confirmed above). Adding
an italicized framework-epigraph under every chapter title would move toward
*more* reader-facing framework content, not less, and a half-explained
recurring element across 27 chapters would raise more questions than it
answers (what is "River"? why does this chapter say "Oak"?) without the
matrix ever being explained. The Introduction is the better home for this:
it's the one place a light, one-time gesture toward "what a marriage needs"
can land without becoming a recurring tic the reader has to track for the
rest of the book. Suggest deferring any epigraph-style treatment until
`/book-intro` (or a `/book-feedback introduction` pass), and keeping it as a
single framing image there rather than a per-chapter device.

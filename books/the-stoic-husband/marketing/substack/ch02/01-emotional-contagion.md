Moods are contagious. Not metaphorically. Literally. The man who doesn't know this is walking through his marriage undefended.

When someone walks into a room carrying something heavy, the people around them start mirroring it before anyone has said a word. Facial expressions, posture, the energy they bring through the door. You're reading your wife constantly, and most of that reading happens below conscious thought. Researchers put the non-verbal share of communication at around 70%. The signal is always running. For most men, so is the mirror.

Her bad day arrives and you absorb it before you've noticed it happening. Now there are two of you carrying it. She walks in tense and within an hour you're both short with everyone. She comes in low and by evening the whole house has taken on her weight. Nobody passed it deliberately. It just ran.

The Stoic practice is the inoculation. Epictetus was a former slave who became one of the most important Stoic teachers. He opens his field manual with a single cut: some things are yours to govern, some aren't. Her mood isn't. It's external, outside what you control. But you can only work with that if you notice what's arriving. Most men don't. Her mood walks in and they're already mirroring before they've registered it happened.

When she walks in and something's off, you name it, quietly, to yourself, before it has time to take hold. *Her day is in the room.* That naming is the gap between the signal and the mirror. The Stoics called the governing part of a man the hegemonikon: the part that observes what's arriving and decides what to do about it. Most men have never used it because nobody told them the moment existed. Name it before it locks in. That's the inoculation.

And you're not exempt going the other way. You've had a hard day and you can feel it in your chest at the door. That's the moment. Name it before you carry it inside. The man who knows what's happening isn't the one who never feels the signal. He's the one who doesn't let it make the decision.

That's not cold. That's the only way to be useful in a room.

# The Replay Question

There's a question most men never ask themselves after a fight, and it isn't "who was right." It's not even close to that question, and that's exactly why most men never find it.

Lying awake after the house has gone quiet, running the night back, the easy question is whether she was being fair, whether she deserved what she got, whether you were justified. Those questions feel productive. They aren't, because they all point outward, at her, at the situation, at anyone but you. There's a different question that does something the others don't: it stops grading her and starts grading you. Was I being the best husband I could be tonight? Not a feeling. Four specific things, asked as if you're still standing in the moment, the same way you'd review a play after the game instead of arguing about the score.

Logical: did you actually think it through, or had you already decided and were just executing it, the outcome settled before the conversation even started? Was the care behind it real, for her and the marriage, instead of ego, a running score, or a fight you were already inside before she said a word? Self-control showed up, or it didn't: you either governed the impulse before it fired, or let whatever you felt become automatic, the way it usually does. And were you actually there, in the room, reachable, rather than leaving through words or silence or the line you used to end it?

Ask all four honestly and you usually already know the answers. That's the uncomfortable part. The replay question doesn't uncover some hidden truth. It just makes you look at what you already knew and would rather not have looked at directly. The four answers, taken together, tell you more about the marriage than the fight itself ever could.

Tonight, after whatever happens tonight, the question isn't who's right. Lying there in the dark, the four questions come one at a time, slowly enough to actually answer each one instead of skimming past it. You're not judging the fight. You're grading the man who showed up to it.

**The question doesn't fix tonight. It trains the version of you that shows up tomorrow.**

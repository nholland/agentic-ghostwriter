## Full List (as captured)

1. Have sex often. Not just for pleasure — but as a reminder: you chose each other.
2. Your partner comes first. Not your friends, not your family — them.
3. Ego ruins more marriages than cheating ever will.
4. Flirting shouldn't stop after the wedding. You wanted them once — keep showing them why you still do.
5. Love isn't always fireworks. Sometimes it's just showing up... again and again... like keeping a fire alive.
6. Pray for your partner. Even when you're mad — especially when you're mad.
7. Stop inviting your mom into your marriage. This is a two-person build, not a group project.
8. Quit zooming in on their flaws. You're their partner, not their critic.
9. Love them hardest when they're hardest to love.
10. Learn when to stop fighting. Not every argument needs a winner.
11. They can't read your mind. Say what you need. Hints aren't communication.
12. You can feel lonely in a relationship. It doesn't mean it's wrong — it means something needs attention.
13. At the end of the day, choose to be teammates. Not opponents. Don't go to bed as enemies — reset and try again tomorrow.

## Thematic overlap with existing book material

Most of this list already has a home in the book's existing frameworks or
author beliefs — which is itself useful signal: it confirms the book's
arguments track what resonates broadly, not just what one author believes.

- **#3 (ego ruins more marriages than cheating) and #8 (quit zooming in on
  their flaws)** — directly restate `author-notes.md` Core Belief #4
  ("Anger is failed leadership") and the **Role Frame** framework
  (`okf/frameworks/`) — ego asks "am I winning," role asks "what does love
  require." Confirms the ego-vs-role frame is on the right track.
- **#9 (love them hardest when hardest to love)** and **#10 (not every
  argument needs a winner)** — align with the **Dichotomy of Control in
  Marriage** and **Marriage as Anti-Narcissism** frameworks, and with
  Pillemer's "learn how to fight" / "don't keep score" findings already in
  `sources/articles/pillemer-cornell-marriage-advice.md`.
- **#11 (they can't read your mind, hints aren't communication)** — matches
  Pillemer's "communication is the paramount lesson" finding. No new
  framework needed; reinforces existing Ch7/communication material.
- **#13 (choose to be teammates, not opponents)** — echoes **Marriage as
  Community of Life** and the jurisdiction-over-fault argument in Part II.

## Possible genuine gaps (checked against 03-outline.md and okf/ — updated 2026-07-06 during /book-source-prep)

Cross-referencing against the actual outline and existing OKF concepts
narrowed this from 5 candidates to 3 real gaps. Two turned out to already be
covered:

- **#4 (flirting/pursuit shouldn't stop after the wedding) — NOT a gap.**
  Ch11 (Romance Is a Discipline) and Ch12 (Pursue Her After You Have Her)
  already cover this ground directly and in depth — Ch12's entire premise is
  "a man who worked relentlessly to win his wife and then stopped."
- **#6 (pray for your partner, especially when angry) — NOT a gap.** The
  existing framework `okf/frameworks/the-evening-review-stoic-night-practice.md`
  is explicitly described by the author as "the Stoic prayer" — a secular
  nightly self-examination practice already serving this exact function.

Three items remain genuine, narrower gaps than first flagged — now logged as
`status: unverified` citation stubs in `../../okf/citations/`:

- **#1 (sex as a recurring ritual affirmation of choice, not just pleasure)**
  — distinct from the existing love-vs-desire (Perel) framework. See
  [`sex-as-ritual-choosing-each-other.md`](../../okf/citations/sex-as-ritual-choosing-each-other.md).
- **#7 (boundaries with extended family / in-laws)** — Pillemer's "Five Major
  Stressors" names in-laws as a top conflict category, but no current
  chapter addresses it directly (Ch9 covers a related-but-different
  discipline: saying no to one's spouse, not to one's family of origin).
  Flagged as a possible **outline gap**, not just a research gap — see
  [`in-law-family-of-origin-boundaries.md`](../../okf/citations/in-law-family-of-origin-boundaries.md).
- **#12 (loneliness inside a relationship is a signal, not proof the
  relationship is wrong)** — narrower than it first looked; Ch17 and Ch3
  cover adjacent ground from the outside, but not this direct, reader-facing
  reframe. See
  [`loneliness-as-signal-not-verdict.md`](../../okf/citations/loneliness-as-signal-not-verdict.md).

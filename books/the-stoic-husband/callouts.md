## Signature Frameworks

*Ranked with the author's call front and center: **The River, the Oak, and the Sun is the concept this book builds its fame around.** Everything below reflects that decision — it's listed first, and later marketing/launch work (`/book-marketing`, `/book-substack`) should treat it as the anchor concept the way a book like *Atomic Habits* anchors on "atomic habits" itself, with other frameworks (the Three-Second Window included) as strong supporting ideas rather than co-leads.*

### ⭐ The River, the Oak, and the Sun — THE SIGNATURE IDEA
**What it describes:** The book's master metaphor — a husband needs the river's calm adaptability, the oak's dependable strength, and the sun's deliberate warmth, together. Missing any one produces a named failure mode (passive, rigid, cold, unreliable, volatile).
**Where it appears:** Introduction (full treatment); referenced structurally as the organizing frame for the book's five Parts (River = Part I, Oak = Part II, Sun = Part III).
**Current state:** Already named, and already load-bearing at the structural level, not just the conceptual one — this is the rare framework doing double duty as both a standalone idea and the book's table of contents.
**Visual potential:** Very high — the "Danger of Becoming Only One" section is already a five-row table in prose form (river without oak = passive, oak without river = rigid, etc.). A simple triangle or Venn diagram with the five failure modes labeled at the edges would visualize the whole Introduction in one image. **Recommend this diagram becomes the book's primary marketing visual** — cover treatment, social header image, and the first thing `/book-marketing` builds around.
**Standalone potential:** Very high, and now the book's chosen center of gravity — memorable, alliterative-adjacent, and immediately teachable independent of the Stoic/marriage frame (it would work for leadership, parenting, or any domain needing calm + strength + warmth). Every chapter-level Substack/social push (`/book-substack`) should look for a natural, non-forced tie-back to River/Oak/Sun the way Part structure already implies (Part I chapters reinforce River, Part II reinforces Oak, Part III reinforces Sun) — without inventing a connection where a chapter doesn't actually have one.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-river-the-oak-and-the-sun.md`

### The Three-Second Window (a.k.a. The Gap)
**What it describes:** Between any triggering moment and a man's response sits a gap — real, even when it feels like zero seconds — where an inherited "operating system" (trigger → loaded meaning → automatic response) either fires unexamined or gets interrupted by a deliberate choice.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 1, and referenced as the load-bearing concept for the rest of Part I (Ch2–5 each build on "the gap").
**Current state:** Already named — the book's title concept and its most fully developed single-chapter framework. Strongest supporting idea to River/Oak/Sun, not a co-lead — Part I's own internal engine, not the whole book's frame.
**Visual potential:** High. A simple horizontal timeline: Trigger → Loaded Meaning (baggage) → **[THE GAP — 3 SECONDS]** → Response, with the gap segment visually widened and labeled "prohairesis: your faculty of choice."
**Standalone potential:** High — strong enough to anchor a single essay, talk, or social motif of its own, but positioned in this book's marketing as the reader's first practical tool inside the River/Oak/Sun frame (it's the River chapter's core mechanism), not as competing top billing.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-operating-system-trigger-meaning-autopilot.md`

### The Four D's (Defense, Deny, Downplay, Deflect)
**What it describes:** The four automatic, ego-protective responses to criticism — each feels like honesty in the moment, and each teaches a partner that bringing something up costs more than it's worth.
**Where it appears:** Named in Chapter 3; recurs explicitly in Chapter 5 ("the Four D's are already running") and Chapter 6 (applied to household-labor criticism specifically).
**Current state:** Already named, and already doing real cross-chapter work — a rare case in this manuscript of a framework introduced once and then actively reused, which is exactly what a signature framework should do.
**Visual potential:** High. A simple 2x2 or four-box diagram, one box per D, each with its one-line tell ("Defense: lists evidence. Deny: 'that's not true.' Downplay: 'it's not that bad.' Deflect: 'you're not perfect either.'").
**Standalone potential:** High — recognizable enough (most readers have caught themselves doing at least two) to work as a standalone diagnostic quiz or a single shareable graphic.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-four-ds-defensive-response-taxonomy.md`

### The Nail Parable
**What it describes:** A father has his son drive a nail into a fence post for every angry outburst, then pull one out for every day of self-control. The nails come out. The holes stay. Damage from anger outlives the apology that follows it.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 4.
**Current state:** Already named — a well-known parable (origin unattributed in the text) put to specific, well-earned use here.
**Visual potential:** Very high, and almost literal — a fence full of holes is already the image. A simple before/after illustration (nails driven / nails pulled, holes remaining) needs no adaptation.
**Standalone potential:** High — this parable circulates independently of any book already; this manuscript's contribution is the sharp one-line reframe ("An apology pulls the nail. It doesn't fill the hole.") that could travel on its own.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-nail-parable.md`

### Rocks and Sand (Two Honest Ledgers)
**What it describes:** A marriage's actual labor is mostly sand — hundreds of small, uncounted acts — with a few "rocks" (visible, nameable, citable-in-an-argument contributions) on top. Any private tally is built entirely from rocks and will always undercount the sand, which is why it always tells the person keeping it that they're ahead.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 7, building directly on Chapter 6's "bucket" concept (the unnamed, unscheduled tasks).
**Current state:** Already named as a concept in the knowledge bundle (Small Rocks, Big Rocks); the chapter itself doesn't use the word "sand" explicitly but the jar image ("What fills most of the space is sand") is right there in the prose.
**Visual potential:** Very high — a literal jar illustration: a few large labeled rocks (visible contributions) with sand filling the gaps (the uncountable rest). This is one of the most naturally diagram-able ideas in the manuscript so far.
**Standalone potential:** High — "your ledger only has access to your own column" is a generalizable insight beyond marriage (roommates, business partnerships, family labor), which is often the sign of a signature idea that outgrows its book.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/small-rocks-big-rocks.md` (the "give 60, expect 40" corollary is folded into this same concept file)

### The Mood Mirror (Emotional Contagion)
**What it describes:** A man's inner state automatically absorbs his wife's — lifting when she lifts, sinking when she sinks — and left ungoverned, this "mirroring" means he's outsourced his peace to someone who never agreed to carry it.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 2.
**Current state:** Already named (both in-chapter as "the human mirror" and in the knowledge bundle as "Emotional Contagion").
**Visual potential:** Moderate — a simple two-state diagram (her mood → his mood, mirrored, vs. her mood → his steady state, ungoverned) could work but is less visually distinct than the other candidates above.
**Standalone potential:** Moderate-high — "emotional contagion" is an established research term the book is translating for a lay reader, so the standalone version would need to lead with the book's own reframe ("outsourcing your peace") rather than the academic name to travel well.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/emotional-contagion-the-human-mirror.md`

### The Tipping Scale
**What it describes:** Felt unfairness almost never arrives as one clean grievance. It accumulates as dozens of things too small to name individually, each adding invisible weight to a scale neither person is watching, until one ordinary moment tips it and the feeling surfaces as if from nowhere.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 8 — named as the chapter's own distillation mechanism.
**Current state:** Now captured — was flagged as a gap in this document's first pass (the mechanism and prose were already finished in `refined.md`, just not yet its own concept). Author confirmed capturing it; done.
**Visual potential:** High — a scale accumulating small, unlabeled weights on one side until it tips.
**Standalone potential:** High — the third accumulation-based metaphor in the book (after the Nail Parable's holes and Rocks-and-Sand's jar), which is itself worth a `/book-sweep` note later: three structurally identical metaphors is either a deliberate through-line or a repetition to vary further out.
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-tipping-scale.md`

### The Bucket (Unscheduled Labor)
**What it describes:** Some household labor has a name and a schedule (trash on Tuesday) and gets seen. Most of it doesn't — it lives in an unnamed bucket of one-off, non-recurring tasks (the rattling ceiling fan, the errand). Because nothing repeats often enough to earn its own calendar slot, nobody notices when the bucket gets emptied, which is why blindness to a partner's labor gets worse the longer a marriage runs.
**Where it appears:** Chapter 6, "The split" section.
**Current state:** Now captured — was flagged as a gap in this document's first pass (existed only as supporting prose for the split/hero-of-your-own-story idea). Author confirmed capturing it; done.
**Visual potential:** Moderate — pairs well with Ch6's existing "what you see / what she sees" two-column visual as the specific reason one column stays invisible.
**Standalone potential:** Moderate-high — reusable beyond marriage (any invisible, non-recurring labor: parenting, caregiving, freelance admin).
**Knowledge bundle:** Captured as: `/okf/frameworks/the-bucket-unscheduled-labor.md`

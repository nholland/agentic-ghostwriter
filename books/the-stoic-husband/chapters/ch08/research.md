## Chapter Mechanism (final)

**The mechanism in one sentence:** Unfairness rarely arrives as one clean grievance. It accrues, small uncounted injustice by small uncounted injustice, a critique that stung, effort that went unnoticed, taking a different shape depending on what kind of imbalance a man is prone to, until it tips into a felt wave that seems to come from nowhere but has been building the whole time. Once you've noticed the wave, there are two different situations to tell apart: when it's her expressed unfairness landing on you, and when it's your own.

## Structure (final — follow this, not the old beats below)

### Opening: return to Chapter 7, no new scene
Callback directly to Ch7's ending: the private tally was the wrong tool because it can only read one side of the ledger. This chapter is about what actually accrues underneath that tally, and what to do once it tips.

### Beat 1: Name the real mechanism — it's grievance, not "unfairness," that accrues
The felt sense of "this is unfair" isn't usually one moment. It's built from many small things: a critique that stung, a contribution that went unnoticed, over and over, until it tips into something that feels sudden but wasn't. This is the chapter's central image (replacing the "diagnosis/doctor" metaphor from the previous drafts, which the author didn't ask for and doesn't need to survive) — consider a scale or accumulating weight image, but keep it to one image, capped per the voice spec's metaphor rules.

### Beat 2: The shape varies by marriage — brief, patterned, no elaborated scenes
Explicitly do NOT build one long composite scene the way Ch1–7 typically do. Instead, use light, patterned, direct-address configurations so a reader finds his own without the chapter assuming a script: "some men find themselves in this situation," "a common scenario for many men is X," etc. Cover 2–3 configurations briefly, not one elaborated per paragraph:
- The traditional split (not earning, doing more of the visible home tasks, feeling that goes uncredited) — brief acknowledgment this exists, explicitly not asserted as the default or the only shape.
- The reversed or dual-income split (the primary earner, or the more demanding job, carries weight that's invisible because it happens offstage, and its output — a mortgage payment, insurance, retirement savings — doesn't visually compete with a clean kitchen).
- A same-domain-different-labor split (both partners working, one doing more physical household tasks, the other owning a different domain entirely, e.g. kids' schooling/activities) where the unfairness is about task load AND lack of recognition simultaneously, not a single clean binary.
Do not use real named individuals (a real friend, "Chris," was explicitly ruled out by the author). Keep each configuration to 1–2 sentences. This section functions as the taxonomy's presence in the chapter: task imbalance, invisible/offstage contribution, and inconsistent recognition are all folded in here as light texture, not as separately headed, individually-scened beats the way earlier drafts built them.

**Optional grounding, if useful:** `six-ledgers-of-scorekeeping.md`'s Impact/Value ledger ("whose work counts for more, often money vs. care work") and `gillespie-peterson-lever-2019-fairness-housework-expenses.md` (fairness over shared expenses predicts relationship quality more strongly than fairness over housework) can back the money/career configuration briefly, conversationally, not as a stat-dump.

### Beat 3: The two-directions framework — this is the chapter's real payload
Two different situations, two different Stoic tools:

1. **When it's her expressed unfairness landing on you.** The diagnostic is Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 5.28 (already used for a different purpose in an earlier rejected draft — this drafting should use it for THIS purpose, not the double-standard framing from before): is this true or not true? If true, there's real work ahead, a construction project, not a performance of apology. If not true, she's likely not being malicious, just unreflective, hasn't examined what she's not seeing — gentle correction, not counterattack. `Backed by: /okf/citations/marcus-aurelius-on-correction-and-tolerance.md`.
2. **When it's your own felt unfairness.** The tool is Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 81: virtue's reward is in the act itself, "the wages of a good deed is to have done it." You don't need her active gratitude to validate work you've already done. **This must be introduced as an explicit callback to Ch7's "don't reach for the third thing" (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, already used there)** — name directly that the reader already has this tool from last chapter, and this is a new philosopher making the same case from a different angle, not a fresh unrelated concept. Do not present it as if new/unconnected. `Backed by: /okf/citations/seneca-letter-81-wages-of-a-good-deed.md`. Explicitly ruled out for this: reusing Marcus Aurelius's exact "third thing" passage/phrase verbatim (repetitive back-to-back with Ch7), and a Musonius Rufus quote ("the greatest reward for a good deed is to have done it") that only has quote-aggregator sourcing, not a traceable primary passage — do not use the Musonius line.

### Closing beat: gratitude as the standing inoculation
Tie back to `scorekeeping-is-contagious.md`'s "The Missing Competition" addition (grievance is contagious, gratitude never is) and Ch7's 60/40 line (give 60, expect 40 — expecting less is what keeps the scale from tipping as often in the first place). Land the chapter's own idea in one final sentence, author's voice, nothing after it that restates or qualifies it.

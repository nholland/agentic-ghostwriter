# Scorekeeping Is Contagious

Scorekeeping doesn't stay contained to one issue or one person. The moment
one spouse feels an injustice — "I'm carrying more" — and says so, or shows
it, the other spouse's instinct is rarely to concede the point. It's to reach
for their own ledger: "Well, *I*..."

This is escalation by ledger. Each person, feeling attacked on their own
account, digs into a different account to defend themselves — and now two
real grievances are colliding instead of either one being heard.

**The key insight: two things can be true at once.** A man can be genuinely
exhausted from a hard day and need rest. His wife can be genuinely at the end
of her capacity and need relief. Neither is wrong. But once both retreat to
"I'm the one who's overworked here," the conversation stops being about the
evening and becomes a referendum on the entire marriage's fairness — fought
on two different scoreboards at once.

# Schema

1. Spouse A feels an injustice (their ledger shows them under-credited).
2. Spouse A names it — directly, or through behavior (a comment, a sigh, a
   withdrawal).
3. Spouse B receives this as an attack on *their* ledger, not as information
   about A's.
4. Spouse B responds by opening their own ledger — often in a different
   category than A's (see [Small Rocks, Big Rocks](/frameworks/small-rocks-big-rocks.md)).
5. The conversation is now two parallel audits, neither of which can resolve,
   because neither person is auditing the other's actual book.

# The Missing Competition

*(Added from the Ch8 research check-in, 2026-07-19.)* Grievance is
contagious in exactly the way gratitude never is. The moment one spouse
names a felt injustice, the other's instinct is to open a competing
ledger — who's more disappointed, who's given up more, who's been let
down worse. Nobody has ever responded to a partner's grievance by opening
a *counter-grievance of gratitude* — racing to prove they're more thankful,
more served, more fortunate. The asymmetry is the tell: contagion runs one
direction because the ledger format itself only has room for debts, never
credits held in someone else's favor on purpose.

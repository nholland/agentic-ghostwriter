# Six Ledgers of Scorekeeping

Most people who recognize themselves as "keeping score" picture one thing:
who did the dishes, who handled bedtime, who's ahead on chores. That's real,
and it's the most visible version. But it's one ledger out of at least six,
and a reader who doesn't run a chore tally can still be a heavy
scorekeeper, just on a different book.

# Schema

| Ledger | What's being tallied | Example tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | Who performed the visible labor | "I did the dishes again." |
| **Impact / value** | Whose work counts for more (often money vs. care work) | "I'm the one who actually provides." |
| **Sacrifice** | Who gave the bigger, more permanent thing | "I went through childbirth. You don't get to complain." |
| **Competence** | Who's *better* at a shared domain, not who does more of it | "You're just not as good with the kids as I am." |
| **Grievance / harm** | Who has hurt the other more often or worse | "You did this to me five times. I never did that to you." |
| **Intimacy** | Who initiated, who was rejected, who's "more loving" | "I'm always the one who tries." |

**Task** is the easiest to spot because it's countable: a dish either got
washed or it didn't. The other five are harder to see precisely because
they're judgment calls, not events. **Impact/value** and **competence**
aren't disputes about what happened, they're disputes about what something
is *worth* or who's *good at it*, and judgment calls have no agreed
scoreboard, which is what makes them so much more corrosive than the task
ledger. **Sacrifice** is the most dangerous of the six because it's
designed to end the argument rather than continue it: "I gave the ultimate
thing" forecloses debate instead of opening one. **Grievance** is a harm
ledger, not a labor ledger, and it's the version most likely to calcify into
contempt over years. **Intimacy** cuts closest to identity because it reads
less like "who does more" and more like "who is more wanted."

# The shared root

All six ledgers run on the same engine: the self cast as the protagonist of
its own story, auditing the marriage from inside that story instead of a
shared one. This is [The Role Frame (Ego vs. Role)](/frameworks/the-role-frame-ego-vs-role.md)
applied across six different domains instead of one. Ego asks "am I getting
what I'm owed," in whichever currency that ledger uses. Role asks what the
shared mission actually needs, which has no ledger at all.

# Why a reader might not see himself in "scorekeeping"

A man who's never once tallied chores can still be running the impact
ledger ("my income is the real contribution"), the competence ledger
("she's just better at the emotional stuff, I'm better at the practical
stuff, so we're each in our lane") or the intimacy ledger, without ever
naming any of it scorekeeping. Naming only the task ledger risks losing
these readers entirely; naming the full range, even briefly, gives more
readers a way in.

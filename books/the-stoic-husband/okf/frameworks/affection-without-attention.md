# Author material

"My personal frame is the number of times you say "Love you" with no true thoughts behind it. The number of times you ask how her day was and are content with "fine". The times you and her start watching different shows, listening to different podcasts, and neither has interest on the others choices."

"When a child brings you a hand drawn picture, you express joy and wonderment, but can barely look away from your phone when your wife shares about her day. You don't give her your full attention. You don't remember things. You aren't interested, and she mirrors to be less interested in you."

# Scope

These are the author's illustrative observations, not established autobiographical scenes or a universal causal law. The child's picture contrasts attention he already knows how to give; it does not prescribe treating his wife as a child. The reciprocal loss of interest is a proposed mechanism, not a guaranteed response. Repeated words can retain meaning; repetition alone is not evidence of neglect.

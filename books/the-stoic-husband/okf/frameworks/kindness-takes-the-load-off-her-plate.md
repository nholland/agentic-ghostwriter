# What the author says kindness is

- **A spectrum** (Round 3). At one end, choosing kindness "when you're under
  assault." At the other, no conflict at all: being understanding, accepting someone's
  flaws, "just be a nice person."
- **Not the same as being there** (Round 3): "Being kind is when you need to show
  patience. You need to show understanding." (Round 4, on listening: "just listening and
  being present is really a kindness" — presence is a form of kindness when it is aimed
  at her; the distinction is not yet settled.)
- **Trying to make someone's day better** (Round 3). Kindness "shows up through the four
  love languages," but it is "a certain framing of it."
- **Compassion, not empathy** (Round 3): "I don't like empathy because many of the Stoics
  would tell you that empathy isn't the right muscle but compassion is the right muscle."
  It takes "a pretty well-adjusted person who is strong and stable in their own state."
  See [Compassion vs. Empathy](/frameworks/compassion-vs-empathy.md).
- **Being the sun** (Round 3): "a source of strength, a source of warmth, a source of
  kindness. When you are the sun people come to you to feel good."
- **Unconditional** (Round 2): "She deserves kindness every day, always, even when she's at
  her lowest."

# His examples (Round 4)

A foot rub after a long day. A glass of wine when she comes to the door. Making coffee in
the morning and bringing it to her. Making breakfast on the weekend. And, in the zing
moment: rolling up his sleeves, a hug, "I see you," asking what would lighten her day.

**His reason these are kind:** "not that there are so many acts of service." It is
"because these are normally things that she does and I'm taking chores and work off of her
plate. I'm doing something that shows that I want to make her life better."

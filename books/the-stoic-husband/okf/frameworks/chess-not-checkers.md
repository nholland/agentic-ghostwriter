# Chess, Not Checkers

The author's words for how he navigates his wife: "playing checkers, not chess"
(Round 1: "I have to remain calm, sturdy, strong, patient.")

# Two definitions, both the author's

| Where | Checkers | Chess |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier intake (`sources/interview-author-stories.md`) | "winning every exchange" | "the long game, winning the marriage, not the argument"; he teaches it to his sons |
| Ch15 interview, Round 2 | "you don't have to meet every single zing in a checkers match with hers" | answering the one question, "Am I being the best husband possible right now at this moment, irrespective of what the other person is doing?" |

The two read as compatible: answering every zing is winning every exchange; the
chess move is the [Virtue Question](/frameworks/the-virtue-question.md) plus patience.
Not yet confirmed by the author as one idea.

# Round 5 update (2026-10-23): chess is the long game

The author: "I think the chess and checkers part is more like the long game. If we've already done
the winning every exchange in a previous chapter, I think chess is more like winning the long
game. If I ignore the zings and pour kindness into my wife, it has an incredible impact on changing
the tone and the mood of the entire house. It's like playing the long game, which means I would
rather have a good night of happiness with my wife than try to answer each exchange."

- Chess = the long game; checkers (winning every exchange) he treats as possibly covered earlier.
  A grep of landed chapters finds "winning the argument while losing the relationship" in Ch5 and no
  "checkers" or "chess" anywhere, so this is not yet in the book in those words.
- One dictated sentence was unclear: "It's not like, 'I'll take your advice on which way to do it.'"
  Resolved by the author 2026-10-24: reading 1, not surrender (ignoring the zings does not mean doing
  whatever she says), and "it's my own advice to myself and other me, learned through experience."
  The authority is lived experience, not borrowed advice.
- This reading (the long game, the mood of the house) is the working definition for Chapter 15. The
  Round 2 wording (asking only the Virtue Question) is kept above and is compatible.

# Four Types of Unfairness

Most "this isn't fair" moments in a marriage aren't one thing wearing
different clothes. They're one of at least four different mechanisms, and
each calls for a different response. Treating a double-standard problem as
an input/output problem, or an input/output problem as an old debt coming
due, is a common reason the same argument keeps returning without ever
actually resolving.

# Schema

1. **Input/Output Imbalance** — a genuine gap between what one person puts
   in (time, effort, emotional investment, specific tasks completed) and
   what comes back. The root feeling underneath it is often not really
   about the task itself. It's closer to "I can't personally do any
   more" — which is why being asked for more, again, lands as an
   accusation of being a disappointment rather than a logistics request.
2. **The Double Standard** — being held to a rule the other person doesn't
   hold themselves to. This isn't about volume of contribution; it's about
   being judged by a standard the judge is exempt from. Naming it usually
   triggers defensiveness, because pointing out a double standard reads as
   a personal attack even when the observation is accurate. See
   [The 4Ds (Defensive Response Taxonomy)](/frameworks/the-four-ds-defensive-response-taxonomy.md).
3. **The Wrong Yardstick — Sideways** — measuring your marriage against
   another couple's, or a hypothetical different partner's, without
   pricing in the trade-off baked into whatever you're envying. The
   version of "better" being compared against always has a cost that
   doesn't show from outside it.
4. **The Wrong Yardstick — Backward** — treating a past imbalance (a
   season where the roles were reversed, or one person carried more for
   years) as a debt the present is obligated to repay. The complaint isn't
   really about today. It's an old ledger that never got formally closed.

# Why the distinction matters

The same sentence, "this isn't fair," can be any of these four, and they
don't respond to the same fix:

- An **input/output gap** is addressed by a direct, specific conversation
  about what's actually being asked and actually being given — not a
  referendum on the whole marriage.
- A **double standard** is addressed by naming the inconsistency itself,
  not re-litigating the specific incident that surfaced it.
- A **sideways or backward yardstick** usually isn't addressed by any
  conversation with your spouse at all. The argument is with a comparison,
  not with her — and no amount of her explaining herself resolves an
  argument she was never actually part of.

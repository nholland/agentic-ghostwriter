# The Discipline of Enough

A man doesn't usually lose interest in his wife because a better option
appeared. He loses it because his own brain quietly runs a rigged
comparison — her whole, known, real self against a single spiking trait
in someone else — and nobody told him the comparison was rigged in the
first place.

# Schema

1. **The treadmill, applied to one specific marriage.** Courtship-era
   intensity was never purely "her" — it ran substantially on novelty and
   uncertainty (see [The Hedonic Treadmill](/frameworks/the-hedonic-treadmill.md)).
   That intensity fading is not a signal about her, or about
   compatibility, or about the marriage — it's what happens to every
   pairing, because it's what the brain does to every reward it adapts
   to. A man who doesn't know this mistakes a universal mechanism for a
   personal verdict.
2. **Miswanting and the comparison trap.** Attraction to one striking
   trait in someone else — a laugh, a body, a sharp comment, an easy
   rapport at a conference — is a comparison between one spiking quality
   and his wife's *entire, known, real person*, complaints, bad days,
   and all. No whole person survives being measured against another
   person's highlight reel. That includes her, if she ran the same
   comparison on him.
3. **"Enough" as jurisdiction, aimed at desire itself.** The same
   principle that governs mood (Chapter 2) and scorekeeping (Chapter 7)
   — govern what's actually yours to govern — applies to wanting. He
   doesn't control whether a stray attraction crosses his mind. He does
   control whether he feeds it, names it, or lets it quietly redefine
   what "enough" means in his own marriage.
4. **Proactive vs. reactive — most advice only offers the second half.**
   - *Reactive* (after the itch, the boredom, or the comparison has
     already fired): naming it and journaling gratitude in response;
     telling himself "this is normal" once he's noticed the pull;
     redirecting attention deliberately after catching himself staring.
     Useful, but all of it is cleanup.
   - *Proactive* (before it fires): naming the coming lull out loud, in
     advance — telling himself the high *will* fade, on purpose, so its
     arrival isn't mistaken for a verdict when it does; scheduling
     novelty deliberately (new shared experiences, real dates) before
     boredom goes looking for novelty on its own terms; savoring as a
     standing daily habit, not a rescue — see
     [The Hedonic Treadmill](/frameworks/the-hedonic-treadmill.md)'s
     savoring practice, repurposed here from general joy to specifically
     keeping the comparison trap starved of material.
   The chapter's real argument: a man who only knows the reactive tools
   is always managing symptoms. Contentment as a discipline means most of
   the real work happens before the wandering eye ever shows up.

# Distinct from neighboring concepts

- **Not** [The Hedonic Treadmill (and Miswanting)](/frameworks/the-hedonic-treadmill.md)
  — that framework is the general mechanism (why achievement and
  circumstance fail to produce lasting joy; lives in Chapter 27). This
  framework is the same mechanism aimed at a specific application:
  contentment with a spouse, and the comparison trap that follows when
  it's not managed. Chapter 14 should cite the mechanism briefly and
  spend its own word count on the marriage-specific argument, not
  re-derive the general science.
- **Not** [The New Partner Is Not Better (The River Comparison)](/frameworks/the-new-partner-is-not-better-river-comparison.md)
  — that framework addresses the *post-divorce* version of this same
  comparison fallacy (a divorced person romanticizing a new partner
  against the real weight of the old marriage). This framework is the
  *in-marriage* version, before any decision has been made — the
  earlier, preventable stage of the same error.

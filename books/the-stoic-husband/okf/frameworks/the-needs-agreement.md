# The Needs Agreement

The foundation, in the author's words: "you explain to your partner that you
have needs, they have needs, and that we love each other and we want to serve
those needs." Everything below is built on that sentence. It is mutual: both
partners have needs, and both serve.

# Schema

## The premise, as the author states it

Sex is an important part of many people's need profile, "particularly men."
It is "less important for women, often for a variety of reasons, including
some of it being medical or health-related." These are the author's
generalisations, recorded as his; they are not established by research here.

## The three rules (author's own list)

1. **Set a frequency.** "At the end of the day, you set a time period: you go
   no longer than that before you have sex." The author's examples of what
   couples choose: "once a week" or "every 3 days." The number is the
   couple's to set.
2. **Say honestly that you are not into it.** The author's stated condition:
   "People should be able to say no anytime they want to. It's their body,
   their right." The other half of the same rule: "constant rejection leads
   to resentment and bitterness." A no is therefore always allowed, and it
   comes with an obligation (rule 2a).
   - **2a. The re-initiation clause.** If one person initiates and the other
     says no, "that person who said no has 24 hours to reinitiate." In the
     author's words: "sometimes you don't feel like it and that's okay but
     you do need to put in the work to basically express love in one of the
     many ways, with physicality being one of those."
3. **The initiator raises the bar.** When the other person "doesn't really
   feel like it," they can say, "Hey I'm going to need you to really put in
   the work to make me an enthusiastic participant." The author's point: the
   person "is willing to do it. It just might not feel very sexy at the
   moment," so it asks "the initiator to raise the bar to make sure that it
   is romantic and enjoyable."

## Why it works, per the author

- "Rejection then puts the IOU on the other person." Putting the next
  initiation on the person who declined "removes the rejection": the one who
  asked is not left holding it, and the one who declined keeps the right to
  decline.
- The rules of engagement "make it fair so that nobody feels rejected."
- It is the mechanism that keeps a stretch of nos from curdling into
  resentment on one side and shame on the other.

## Open questions for the interview (raised by the Publisher, not the author)

These are for him to rule on, at the Chapter 17 interview or sooner. None is
decided.

1. **Where it goes.** Chapter 17, "The Sexless Marriage," is the best fit: it
   already holds the honest conversation, rejection, and "direct and patient"
   (the folded-in former Chapter 15). Whether this is the chapter's practice,
   its central conversation, or a section is his call.
2. **Obligation versus willingness.** The agreement lets either partner say
   no at any time, and also sets a frequency ceiling and a 24-hour
   re-initiation. A reader may hear a quota. The author's own wording keeps
   the no ("their body, their right") and moves the obligation to *showing
   love*, not to *saying yes*. Whether the prose must say that explicitly,
   and how, is open.
3. **Medical and health reasons.** He names them as one reason desire is
   lower. Whether the agreement flexes, pauses or is renegotiated when the
   cause is medical (pain, illness, medication, postpartum) is not addressed
   in what he said.
4. **The second chair.** Rule 3 asks the initiator to raise the bar. What the
   person who is "not into it" owes besides being honest is covered by 2a
   only. Whether rule 3 needs a mirror sentence is open.
5. **His own use.** He describes it as something he has seen couples agree
   to. Whether it is part of his own marriage, and whether it may be told as
   such, is not stated. Do not write it as his story.

## Stoic fit (proposal, not accepted)

- **Desire without need.** The Chapter 17 frame already separates wanting
  from needing the yes to feel okay. This agreement is a structure for the
  same thing: the request is open, the no is safe, and neither is a verdict
  on the person.
- **Serving each other.** [Marriage as Community of Life](/frameworks/marriage-as-community-of-life.md)
  (Musonius Rufus): a shared life in which each serves the other's good.
- **Bounded, not unconditional.** [Bounded Generosity, Not Obligation](/frameworks/bounded-generosity-not-obligation.md)
  addresses the same line between giving and owing.

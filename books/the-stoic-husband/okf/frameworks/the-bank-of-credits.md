# The Bank of Credits

In the author's words: "people want to feel like their relationship is an investment not a burden." "You are really looking to put what we call credits in the bank."

"I teach my boys that relationships of all types, not just marriage, are ones where you have to invest in the relationship over a long period of time. That means constantly you have to put in deposits and those deposits begin to build up because there is inevitably a time when you will need to make a withdrawal. I tell my boys that this is with their teachers, with their coaches, with their girlfriends and I believe that it is critical for husbands to understand the same thing."

**The sun goes down:** "sometimes the sun goes down. It could just be for a short night. It could be because it's a long winter but at the end of the day you have to have built up credits."

**The audit question:** "if you think back over the last whatever period, the last few weeks, the last semester, the last year, what credits have you put into the bank of the relationship?" His examples: saying good morning to his wife when traveling, making coffee, rubbing her back or feet, getting her a gift while out. "It is not about the act of service or the gift. It's just about the credits."

**Green everywhere:** with kids, parents, siblings, work relationships and his wife; "you should have the most credits with your wife because she is your number one." (Round 2 confirmed: "I did mean sun.")

**Second image he offered:** the relationship as a plant, thriving or starving for nutrients, water or sunlight. He is unsure about nutrients ("We could just leave that out"). The book's voice rule allows one image per chapter; Ch14 owns grass-and-water.

# Why it is not a tally (author, Round 2)

"the bank doesn't ever owe you money. You put your own money into your own bank account and then you pull it out. When you're at zero the bank account either closes down or, if you go negative, the bank account starts charging you interest, which could be a metaphor for divorce if we were trying to be funny." "It's more like a bank ledger and I don't know that the bank has ever paid me anything."

Chapter 7's tally counts what she owes him; this counts only what he has put in. On 2026-10-02 the outline removed deposits/account/debt from Chapter 24 as scorekeeping; that ruling predates this distinction, and Ch16's use of the bank is put to the author in runs/ch16/outline-proposal.md.

# How the joint account works (proposed by gw-researcher 2026-11-05; accepted by the author 2026-11-05: "I will go with your recommendations to all six")

1. **One account, nobody counting.** Both put in. Nobody keeps track of whose deposit was whose,
   and nobody checks it to see who is ahead. The check is whether the account is growing, not who
   filled it. (Answers Ch7: a tally is one page of a two-page ledger; this has one page.)
2. **Reciprocation is what a working account looks like, not a condition.** The author wants the
   chapter to be about "investing in the marriage and the reciprocation of attention and effort"
   (Round 6). His side never waits on hers: the Prologue already says to invest "regardless of what
   she's doing with her half". Her deposits are named in one plain sentence and never listed or
   compared.
3. **What it is for.** Hard stretches, his or hers or both, are carried by what is already in the
   account. It never buys her patience for his mistakes. "the bank doesn't ever owe you money"
   (Round 2): nothing comes back to him as a return; the account is the marriage's.
4. **"Most" compares his relationships, not the two of them.** His line "you should have the most
   credits with your wife because she is your number one" (Round 2a) follows his list: "with your
   kids, with your parents, with your siblings, with your work relationships, and with your wife". "Most"
   means more than with anyone else in his life, never more than she has.

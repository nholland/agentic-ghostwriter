# The Courage to Correct Without Needing to Win

The opposite error from defensiveness is total capitulation — accepting
everything in the moment to avoid conflict while the record goes uncorrected.
The Stoic answer is neither.

# Schema

It requires two things working together:

- **Courage** (from the virtue question's fourth pillar — brave enough to stay
  engaged): the willingness to state plainly what he believes, what didn't go
  well, and his perspective on the marriage — in a calm moment, with the intent
  of exiting in a better place.
- **Dichotomy of control**: he states his perspective and releases the outcome.
  He does not need her to validate it, agree with it, or declare him right.
  Things said take time to sink in; things have to be repeated before they
  stick. The Stoic husband can say the true thing once, clearly, without making
  her response the measure of whether it was worth saying.

The critical distinction: in-the-moment correction (defensive, escalating,
ego-driven) vs. calm-moment honesty (courageous, clean, outcome-independent).
The man who has learned the difference can correct the record — just not in the
heat of the moment, and not in a way that requires a verdict.

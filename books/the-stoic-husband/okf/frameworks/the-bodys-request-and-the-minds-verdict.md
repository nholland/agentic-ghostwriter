# The Body's Request and the Mind's Verdict

Two things sit under "I'm angry that we aren't having sex," and they are not
the same thing.

1. **The body's request.** A drive. Not chosen, not a character flaw, not
   his wife's fault. The friend's point in the conversation: it can be real
   and physical.
2. **The mind's verdict.** The thought laid on top of it: *I am owed this; her
   no is a wrong done to me; I cannot be at peace until it is fixed.* This is
   what the author called attachment. This is where the anger comes from.

The Stoic move is not to deny the first or to be cold about it. It is to take
the request seriously and refuse to sign the verdict.

# Schema

## The structure (Publisher's synthesis, pending the Researcher's check)

| Layer | What it is | Chosen? | Stoic handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| The request | A bodily drive; the pull toward her | No | A "first movement" (Seneca, *De Ira* II): involuntary, not itself a fault |
| The verdict | Assent to *I'm owed this / this is a wrong* | Yes | Epictetus: what is up to us is opinion and desire; "attachment" is a preference promoted to a demand |
| The aversion | Dread of her no; withdrawal, silence, not asking | Yes (in what he does with it) | Epictetus pairs desire with aversion; both are judgments about things not up to him |

## What each layer calls for

- **The request:** acknowledge it; do not shame it; do not make her the
  place it is discharged. Name it to her as a need, in the Needs Agreement's
  terms ("we have needs, and we serve each other").
- **The verdict:** ask what claim he is making. Is the want a *preference*
  (strong, valid, to be pursued) or a *demand* (she must, or I am wronged)?
  The REBT version is the same distinction, preferences versus demands.
- **The aversion:** notice the quiet withdrawal that dread of rejection
  produces, and which looks like patience or maturity but is avoidance. This
  is the same machine as [The Deferral Ratchet](/frameworks/the-deferral-ratchet.md)
  ("an aversion to pain and an aversion to fighting"), applied to intimacy.
- **Her side:** she has requests and verdicts and aversions too. Being
  touched out, pain, or dread of being asked are hers, and they are not an
  attack on him.

## Open questions

1. **A word for it.** "Biological attachment" is the author's phrase.
   "Attachment" also means something else in psychology (attachment theory:
   Bowlby, adult attachment). The prose will need to say which it means.
2. **What the evidence says about sexual frustration** (hormones, mood,
   irritability) is unresearched here. The book should not assert a mechanism
   without a source; the Researcher would look.
3. **The hard text.** Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 3 (kiss your wife "as a human
   being" so her death will not disturb you) is the famous attachment passage
   about a spouse. A Chapter 14 researcher marked it "do not use": out of
   context it reads as the emotional armor the book argues against. If this
   framework reaches the page, the book should decide whether to meet that
   text or leave it, with the surrounding Stoic account of loving.
4. **The Stoics on marital sex.** Not a developed topic for them, and Musonius
   Rufus (as remembered, lecture 12) is stricter than the author. This is the
   author's application, not Stoic doctrine, and the prose should say so.
5. **Which chapter.** Chapter 17 is the natural home (its spec already holds
   "desire without need" and rejection). No outline change has been made.

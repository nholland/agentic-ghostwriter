# The Cup — A Two-Way Boundary With Friends

Picture a friend's opinion of your wife as a cup. Every complaint you make
about her pours something in. A cup that's been filled with negativity
doesn't empty on its own — it sits there, coloring how that friend sees
her, long after the specific complaint is forgotten. The author's practice,
held clearly: he does not fill his friends' cups with complaints about his
wife.

The second half runs the other direction. He doesn't let friends pour
contempt into *his* cup about her, either. Advice is welcome — a friend can
say something hard and true. Disrespect isn't — there's a line between a
friend offering a real perspective and a friend running her down, and he
holds that line on both sides of the conversation.

# Why this matters for Chapter 10

The chapter's existing discernment test (key point 1, Wisdom, in `03-outline.md`)
asks whether a friendship makes *him* more contemptuous, more willing to
lie, more likely to treat the marriage as a burden — but that's only the
receiving half of the equation. This framework adds the giving half: he's
not just responsible for filtering what he absorbs, he's responsible for
what he puts into circulation about her in the first place. A man who
polices what friends say about his wife while freely complaining about her
himself hasn't actually solved the problem — he's just moved it.

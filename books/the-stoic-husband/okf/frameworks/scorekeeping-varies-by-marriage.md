# Scorekeeping Varies by Marriage

Scorekeeping isn't universal — it's a dial, and different marriages sit at
different points on it. Some couples (the author's included) run a fairly
active ledger much of the time. Others — a friend's marriage was offered as a
contrast — barely keep score at all; "who's ahead" doesn't organize their
conflicts the way it organizes some couples'.

# Schema

| Marriage type | What scorekeeping looks like |
|---|---|
| High-scorekeeping | Frequent, specific tallying ("I always do X, you never do Y"); resentment accumulates around named categories |
| Low-scorekeeping | Contributions aren't tracked against each other; disagreements don't reach for "who's done more" as ammunition |

# Why this matters for the chapter

If Ch7 assumes every reader recognizes himself as a scorekeeper, a
low-scorekeeping reader may feel the chapter doesn't apply to him — or worse,
that the book is teaching him to *start* scorekeeping by naming a pattern he
doesn't have. Naming the range up front lets the high-scorekeeping reader
feel accurately diagnosed without telling the low-scorekeeping reader that
something is wrong with his marriage.

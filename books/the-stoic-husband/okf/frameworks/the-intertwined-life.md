# The Intertwined Life (Author Personal Frame)

The author and his wife became best friends in sixth grade in 1991, started dating
in tenth grade in 1995, and married in 2003. Their emotional lives are
genuinely intertwined — shared context, shared history, mutual care that runs
deep. When she is having a hard day, he feels it. Not because he is a weather
vane, but because he loves her and they are genuinely connected.

This is the frame that prevents the chapter from reading as cold advice to be
less emotionally present. The book is not about disconnecting from your wife's
emotional life. It is about being so grounded in yourself that the connection
can be real — felt, present, responsive — without becoming uncontrolled.

# Schema

| Frame | Implication |
|---|---|
| Deep connection (best friends since 1991, dating since 1995, married since 2003) | He genuinely feels what she feels — not weakness, but love |
| The misread to prevent | "Stoicism means not caring" / be less emotionally present |
| The antidote | Not less love — a more governed man at the center |

# Placement

- **Ch 2** — the governing claim; prevents the "Stoicism means not caring"
  misread.
- Potentially **Ch 14** and the **Conclusion** — the book's personal testimony

**Use carefully:** This is author-specific context, not a template for all
readers. But it models the principle — deep connection makes uncontrolled
mirroring more likely, not less. The antidote is not less love; it's a more
governed man at the center.

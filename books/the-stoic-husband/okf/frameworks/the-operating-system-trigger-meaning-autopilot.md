# The Operating System: Trigger, Meaning, Autopilot

A marriage runs on an "operating system" built from three parts, repeated
constantly:

1. **A trigger** — something she says, doesn't say, or a look/tone.
2. **Loaded meaning** — the trigger lands on existing baggage (a sensitivity, a
   story, an old wound) and instantly produces a feeling that already has
   meaning attached, before any conscious thought ("she's disrespecting me,"
   "here we go again").
3. **An automatic response** — the inherited behavior pattern (yelling, sulking,
   the sharp comment) named in Ch 1's "What Got Installed."

The gap Chapter 1 is about sits between (2) and (3): the loaded impression has
already arrived, but the response hasn't fired yet.

# Schema

| Step | What it is | Stoic mapping |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Trigger + (2) Loaded meaning | An impression (*phantasia*) — already loaded with meaning, arriving before conscious thought | Epictetus's *phantasia* |
| The gap before (3) | The faculty that can pause before the response fires | *prohairesis* |
| (3) Automatic response | The inherited behavior pattern that fires when the impression runs straight through | — |

Most men never use *prohairesis*; the impression runs straight through to the
automatic response.

# Placement

- **Ch 1 (The Three-Second Window)** — introduces the full pipeline in plain
  language.
- **Ch 3** — the Four D's are automatic responses firing from this same
  pipeline.
- **Ch 4 (anger)** — "you are not accounting for me correctly" is the
  loaded-meaning step at full activation.

Practical hook for the reader: in the moment, one breath plus one question —
"What's this actually about for me right now, and how do I want to respond?" —
is the in-gap version. The full four-virtue review ("Am I being the best husband
I can be?") is the replay version, done afterward, that trains what fires
automatically next time.

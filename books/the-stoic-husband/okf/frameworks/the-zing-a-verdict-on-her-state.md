# The Zing

The author's definition (Round 2): "a little slight, a cutting remark ... like a
critique but it's small." "We call them zings in this family and there are so many
in a day it's hard to keep count." "Every little critique from her was met with an
equal or escalated response." (Round 1; he adds that she was not kind either, and
that the question is who has the strength to de-escalate.)

# What he came to see

- **The offense is the lever.** "That's what it comes down to: how do you get
  offended? That's what a zing is." The zing "doesn't deserve an opinion"; he does
  not have to meet it with a cutting remark, and he does not have to be offended.
- **It is often a verdict on her state, not on him** (Round 4). When she says things
  like "We don't have time to just lay around and not do stuff" or "There's an infinite
  amount of work to get done. Just look around. I shouldn't have to tell you what to
  do," he used to hear "lazy" and defend. He came to hear "a verdict on how stressed
  and overworked she is."
- **Two moves in place of retaliation** (Round 4): (1) roll up his sleeves and help;
  (2) give her a hug, tell her he sees her, and spend a little time seeing whether he can
  make her day lighter. "That's kindness."
- **Being kind while you work through the issue is what de-escalates** and "brings
  warmth, trust, and love into the relationship" (Round 3).

# Was open; resolved by the author 2026-10-23 (Round 5)

An earlier raw intake records a different first response to being called lazy: he
"genuinely reflects: *Am I lazy?*" and, if not, says so plainly. These may be sequential
(reflect, then respond with kindness) or one may have replaced the other. Asked of the
author 2026-10-23. ANSWERED 2026-10-24 (Round 5, below): two different scenarios, not
one sequence, and not to be used twice. Do not merge them.

## Resolution (Round 5, 2026-10-23)

The author: "I think it's different and maybe we don't need to use it twice." Two scenarios, not
one sequence:

- **A. Feedback about you: ask whether it is true.** "When you get feedback from your partner, when
  she calls you lazy, you have to ask yourself, 'Is that truly the case?' If it is you need to fix
  it. If it's not then you can ignore it." This is the earlier "Am I lazy?" answer, kept.
- **B. A remark that is an outward sign of how someone feels inside.** "There are many times, even
  with your children (it's not just your wife, it's the whole household), when people say things
  that may or may not be a reflection of you. It's more an outward manifestation of how they're
  feeling on the inside." His example: "When a child is having a temper tantrum because they are
  hungry or tired, that's not a reflection of their love for the parent or how the parent is even
  parenting." "One drives a reflection into my own self and the other one is that I see it as just
  that the person is struggling."

Chapter 15 is about B, across the whole household. A is not used twice. Source: Author interview, Chapter 15 (runs/ch15/interview.md), Round 5, 2026-10-23 15:14

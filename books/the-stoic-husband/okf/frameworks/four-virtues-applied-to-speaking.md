# The Four Virtues Applied to Speaking

Communication is a throughline of this book, not a topic confined to one
chapter — Ch3 (defensiveness vs. honesty), Ch5 (direct speech in a fight),
Ch16 (real repair vs. apology theater), and Ch19 (honest conversation
across divergence) all depend on it. Rather than invent new terminology for
each instance, this framework reuses [The Virtue Question](/frameworks/the-virtue-question.md)'s
existing four practical virtues, applied specifically to the decision of
whether to speak — not the broader "am I being the best husband" use of
the same four virtues.

# Schema

| Practical name | Applied to speaking |
|---|---|
| Logical (wisdom) | Is this actually worth saying, and is now the moment — not mid-conflict, not stored for months? |
| Brave (courage) | Saying it despite being nervous about her reaction or the consequences |
| Self-controlled (temperance) | The filter: knowing what's genuinely worth raising versus what should be let go — this isn't permission to litigate everything |
| Kind (justice) | Delivering it without it turning into an attack |

# Why this exists

The author's coaching pattern: with men working through real marital
trouble, the diagnostic question that keeps mattering most is "have you
actually had a conversation with her?" Men avoid this for two overlapping
reasons — they lack practiced communication skill, and they're afraid of
the consequences of saying the true thing. Both are addressed by the same
four-part filter: wisdom and temperance handle the skill question (what's
worth saying, and when); courage and justice handle the fear question
(saying it anyway, without it becoming cruelty).

# Placement

- **Chapter 9 (Silence Is Not Peace)** — anchor chapter. This is the first
  chapter about speaking *before* there's a conflict to manage — not
  defense (Ch3), not mid-fight (Ch5), just deciding to say the true thing
  while it's still small.
- **Cross-link candidates for future reuse** (not retrofitted this
  session): Ch3, Ch5, Ch16, Ch19 all already touch honest communication
  and could reference this same four-part filter rather than re-deriving
  the logic each time.

# Internal architecture note

`05-framework.md`'s Layer 2 (the 3×4 Wisdom/Justice/Temperance/Courage
matrix) is internal-only — no chapter should reveal the grid or label a
virtue explicitly by its cardinal name. This framework does not conflict
with that rule: it reuses the Virtue Question's reader-facing practical
labels (Logical/Kind/Self-controlled/Brave), which Ch1 already established
as a repeatable, named, reader-facing tool — not the hidden matrix. Ch9's
own matrix cell (Oak × Temperance, "The Bottomless Yes") already defines
its positive expression as "strength that knows its sustainable limit and
says so" — this framework is that "says so" made concrete, not a drift
into a different cell.

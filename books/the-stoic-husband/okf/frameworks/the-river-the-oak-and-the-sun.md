# Schema

Three elements. A husband is not measured by any one alone — all three,
together, are the standard.

**River — Calm and Adaptable.** Self-command that moves. The river doesn't
fight every stone in its path; it accepts the landscape and keeps flowing in
the same direction. Marriage will not follow the course a man imagined on
his wedding day — careers change, children reshape the household, bodies
age, plans fail. To possess the river is to govern yourself before
attempting to govern the moment: resisting the urge to turn every
inconvenience into a crisis, every disagreement into a battle, every
emotional reaction into a verdict. Calm does not mean feeling less — it
means feeling deeply without letting every feeling take command.

**Oak — Strong and Dependable.** The oak's strength isn't announced; it's
revealed when weight is placed on it. To possess the oak is to become
load-bearing: telling the truth when deception would be easier, keeping
your word after your feelings have changed, carrying responsibility without
demanding applause. Strength doesn't mean pretending you never hurt — it
means refusing to use your own pain as an excuse to become cruel, dishonest,
absent, or irresponsible.

**Sun — Warm and Life-Giving.** A house can be structurally sound and still
feel cold. Strength and calm aren't enough — a husband must bring warmth
*deliberately*: praise without being prompted, touch in passing, curiosity,
flirtation after years of marriage, moments of play and desire. Love that is
never expressed can start to feel like indifference. Warmth isn't weakness;
it's what gives strength a purpose. Make your affection unmistakable.

**The Danger of Becoming Only One.** The incomplete husband embraces one
quality while neglecting the others — six named failure modes:
- River without Oak → **passive** (adapts to everything because he stands
  for nothing)
- Oak without River → **rigid** (mistakes stubbornness for conviction,
  control for strength)
- Oak without Sun → **cold** (provides shelter; no one feels warmth beneath
  his branches)
- Sun without Oak → **unreliable** (affection is bright when life is easy,
  disappears when love requires sacrifice)
- Sun without River → **volatile** (passion is powerful, but emotions
  govern the home)
- River without Sun → **detached** (calm because he has stopped being
  invested; the peace is real and so is the absence underneath it)

**The Standard (closing formula).** When life changes, flow. When life
becomes heavy, stand. When life becomes ordinary, bring warmth. Ordinary
days — not crises — are where most marriages are nourished or neglected.

# The Negative Rumination Loop

The evening review builds the reflection muscle — but reflection can curdle into
rumination. Once a man has developed the habit of examining his inner life, a
separate risk emerges: chronic negative thoughts about the wife or the marriage
that compound on themselves. "She doesn't appreciate me." "She's not meeting my
needs." "She always does this." Left uninterrupted, this is not reflection — it
is rumination, and it has the same psychological effects as ruminating about life
stressors generally: depleted mood, narrowed perception, eventually depression.
Marital-specific: a man who ruminates about his wife's failures builds disdain;
disdain is contempt's precursor; contempt is Gottman's #1 divorce predictor. The
reflection practice must be governed *toward what is mine to correct*, not toward
cataloging her failures.

# Schema

- **The pathway (author's map):** React defensively → stonewall → retreat to
  corner → ruminate → develop negative internal dialogue about wife → disdain →
  contempt (end of marriage).
- **Key distinction:** Healthy reflection (what did I do? what is mine to
  correct?) vs. unhealthy rumination (what has she done to me? how has she let me
  down?). The same evening-review practice can produce either, depending on
  direction.

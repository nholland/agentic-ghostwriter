# Emotional Weather — Shared Categories, Not "Her Mood"

# Schema

When Ch2's mechanisms (emotional contagion, the cascade, sea legs, care
without contagion) need a concrete example of negative affect entering a
household, the easy reach is "she walks in low" or "her mood arrives before
she speaks." Used once, that's a fine illustration. Used as the default
example across every post drawn from this chapter, it drifts into casting
the wife as the source of moodiness and the husband as the neutral governing
party — which is not the book's argument and is unfair to the woman in the
example.

The corrective category set, established during Ch2 Substack drafting:
- **Anxiety** — building over months, not tied to one bad day
- **Real fear** — a child gets sick, a genuine emergency
- **Dread** — a parent's health declining, background weight on an ordinary day
- **Grief** — loss, anticipated or realized
- **Exhaustion** — the ordinary accumulation of a hard stretch

These hit both partners. A sick child is both parents' fear. A parent's
decline is something both spouses watch. Marriage is shared exposure to all
of it — not one partner's weather system that the other learns to weather.

# Examples

- Concept 03 (Sea Legs) reframes "what arrives" as this shared category set
  instead of "her mood," and explicitly notes both partners carry it.
- Concept 04 (Care Without Contagion) uses "someone you love is genuinely
  struggling" rather than gendering the struggling party, reserving "she"
  only for the closing line's direct address to the reader's actual
  relationship.

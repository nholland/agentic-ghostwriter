# The Slide Starts Before the Line

Nobody instantly starts an affair. It's a series of small boundary shifts,
each one individually easy to defend in the moment: joking around, then
lunch together, then after-work drinks — and by the time any single step
looks like a real problem, several lines have already moved. The author
and his wife's actual practice cuts through this with one simple,
non-negotiable rule, decided once and in advance rather than re-litigated
case by case: **don't put yourself in a bad situation.** The decision is
made before any specific situation arises, when judgment is clear — not
in the moment, when it's already compromised.

# Why this is a Chapter 22 mechanism, not Chapter 10's

This is a preventive boundary against a private, internal temptation, not
a boundary against an external person or influence trying to get a vote in
the marriage — Chapter 10's actual territory. The mechanism is close
cousin to Chapter 10's argument (a boundary decided in advance is easier to
hold than one negotiated under pressure) but the application belongs where
temptation and self-command are the direct subject.

# The Evening Review (Stoic Night Practice)

What the author calls "the Stoic prayer" — the nightly self-examination practice
described most explicitly by Seneca in *De Ira* and the *Moral Letters*. Marcus
Aurelius used morning forethought; Seneca used the evening review; both are
designed reflection practices, not accidental self-awareness. The evening review
is the training ground for the in-the-moment pause — you cannot govern the
three-second gap reliably if you never reflect on your reactions after the fact.
Reflection is how the autopilot gets reprogrammed.

# Schema

Three questions:

1. What did I do today?
2. What did I do poorly?
3. What did I leave undone?

**Practical value:** One of the most actionable daily practices in the book.
Works as a nighttime routine anchor — not meditation, not journaling, not a
45-minute process. Three questions, every night.

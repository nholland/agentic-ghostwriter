# The Cascade

The husband comes home and yells at the wife. The wife yells at the kids. The
kids yell at each other. The youngest kicks the dog. A complete chain of
uncontrolled emotional contagion — from one man's ungoverned arrival to an entire
household in distress, ending with an animal who did nothing wrong.

The author has three kids; this pattern is observed from real experience, not
hypothetical.

# Why It Works

The cascade is the purest illustration of what uncontrolled downhill mood
mirroring does. Nobody in the chain made a deliberate decision to pass the
distress along — it just moved, the way weather moves. The dog at the end is the
detail that makes it human and slightly absurd: the final recipient of a chain
that started with one man's bad day. The humor in the image prevents it from
reading as heavy or moralistic.

# Tonal Note

Do not use this as an accusation. The husband in the cascade is everyman — he
doesn't know he's doing it. The chapter is about seeing the pattern, not blaming
the man who enacts it.

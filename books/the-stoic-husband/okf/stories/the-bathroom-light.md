# The Bathroom Light

One night, a husband leaves the bathroom light on — something he'd never
think twice about, something his wife could flip off in passing without a
thought. Instead, she's in there, already carrying frustration from
somewhere else, and it comes out as a sharp, outsized comment about the
light.

He didn't care about the light. He still doesn't. But the response attached
itself to the moment — and now, every time he sees that light left on, some
small charge fires. Not because the light matters. Because of what the
reaction once meant: an injustice, landing on something that had nothing to
do with the actual source of the frustration.

# Best Use

Ch 7 — illustrates how a scoreboard entry gets *installed*. The ledger isn't
built only from genuine grievances; it's also built from moments where an
unrelated frustration lands on an innocent target, and the target becomes
permanently associated with the feeling. The entry doesn't come off just
because the "issue" was never real.

# Schema

1. A genuine frustration exists (its source is unrelated to the trigger).
2. It's expressed disproportionately, attached to a trivial, innocuous act.
3. The person on the receiving end didn't do anything wrong by any normal
   standard — but absorbs the disproportionate reaction.
4. The trivial act becomes permanently loaded. Years later, the charge is
   still there, detached from its original cause.

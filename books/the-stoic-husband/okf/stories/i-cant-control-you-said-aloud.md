# "I Can't Control You"

In the author's words: "When I first got into Stoicism, one of the first arguments I had with my wife led to her being angry at me and me sitting there quiet. After she was done engaging, I simply looked at her and said, 'I can't control you. I can only control me and I don't see things the way you do.'"

"I was using a lot of the Stoic phrases in the actual argument and it created a problem where my wife misinterpreted that I did not care about her. That is one of the ways that sometimes Stoicism can come across as if you are unfeeling. I think that in the hands of someone who is unskilled or uneducated about Stoicism, that is a reasonable conclusion."

**Overlap:** Ch1 printed the internal version and "My wife thought I'd gone somewhere she couldn't reach." Ch16 may use the spoken line as new detail; do not retell Ch1's scene.

**Her answer (Round 2, 2026-11-04):** "If I have a man who doesn't care how I feel, if I have a man who believes that he's in a relationship by himself, then he will find himself in a relationship by himself." In his words: "She was very quick to communicate. She did not want to be married to a stone." ("Married to a stone" is his summary of what she meant, not her words.)

**What he realized (Round 3, 2026-11-04):** "I don't remember what the argument was about, likely something with kids. But I do remember that when she started getting upset about the things I was saying, I had a moment of reflection. I realized that I had to be doing something wrong because at the end of the day philosophy is about living a set of truths that should yield a good life." "When I thought about her reaction (I can't remember what we were arguing about) it wasn't something that was incredibly principled. I realized that my behavior and interactions actually made the situation worse and that's why I realized I needed to update how I was thinking about it" The topic is not remembered; never name one in prose.

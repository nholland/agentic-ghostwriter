# The One-Time Loan

The author's family has a standing rule for financial crises: you can
borrow money once. If you pay it back, you can always come back if you
need help again — the door stays open. If you don't pay it back, that was
your one time. A surprising number of people haven't paid it back. The
rule holds anyway, no matter how much they need it later or how hard the
ask is.

# Why this fits Chapter 10

A clean, real, already-resolved illustration of a boundary enforced
against genuine pressure and genuine need — not a hypothetical. It's a
concrete instance of [Cloud & Townsend's Law of Sowing and
Reaping](/citations/cloud-townsend-boundaries.md): the consequence of not
repaying belongs to the person who didn't repay, not to the family that
would otherwise keep absorbing it indefinitely. Candidate supporting
example for Claim 2 (distance isn't cruelty) or Claim 4 (consistent,
undramatic enforcement) — smaller and more resolved than the chapter's
central story, useful as a secondary beat rather than the opening.

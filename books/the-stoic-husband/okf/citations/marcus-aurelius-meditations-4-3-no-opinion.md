# Marcus Aurelius — "You Always Own the Option of Having No Opinion"

> "You always own the option of having no opinion. There is never any need to
> get worked up or to trouble your soul about things you can't control. These
> things are not asking to be judged by you. Leave them alone."
> — *Meditations* 4.3, Hays trans. (wording and section both to be confirmed)

# Why This Fits Ch11

The tolerating row is the chapter's one relieving beat, and it needs a Stoic
grounding or it reads as permission to disengage. This supplies it. "These
things are not asking to be judged by you" is the whole idea: a hobby he finds
tedious, a brother-in-law he would not have picked, a friend of hers he
tolerates. None of it is asking for a verdict, and supplying one anyway is
work he chose.

It also carries the chapter's argument about **cost**: a man who fights the
friction he could have tolerated has spent courage he will need for the thing
that actually had a door.

# Handling Note

Do not let this bleed into the door row. "Leave them alone" is right for
friction and catastrophically wrong for the thing he has been silent about for
months — and a reader skimming could take it as blanket permission. The
tolerating beat should be clearly fenced from the courage beat, ideally with
the fence stated: this is what you do with the things that were never trouble,
and the previous beat was about what you owe the things that are.

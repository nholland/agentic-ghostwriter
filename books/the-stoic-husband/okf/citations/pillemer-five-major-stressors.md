# Pillemer — The Five Major Stressors in Long Marriages

From interviews with 700+ people in marriages of thirty to fifty-plus years,
Pillemer's project names five stressors that recur across most long marriages:
**in-laws, finances, household labor delegation, communication failures, and
loss of intimacy.**

# Why this fits Chapter 10

The chapter organizes boundaries into three tiers — how the couple treats each
other, how they act (money), and how they run their family (in-laws,
holidays, children). Pillemer's five, arrived at independently and from a very
different method, cover the same ground:

| Pillemer's stressor | Where this book handles it |
|---|---|
| In-laws | **Ch10, Tier 3** |
| Finances | **Ch10, Tier 2** |
| Communication failures | **Ch10, Tier 1** (and Ch9) |
| Household labor delegation | Ch6–Ch7 |
| Loss of intimacy | Part III |

The useful claim for the chapter is small and should stay small: a man might
reasonably suspect these three domains are just one author's list of house
rules. They aren't. When researchers asked seven hundred people who had stayed
married for decades what actually strains a marriage, this is the territory
they named.

**Use as one line, not a section.** The chapter's evidence hierarchy
(`04-archetype.md`) puts recognizable scenarios first and keeps citation
density low. This is a corroborating aside, not a beat.

# Backfill Note — 2026-09-10

**The count was NOT changed to four, deliberately.** Two reasons.

First, **nothing in the book depends on it.** No chapter and no compiled prose
asserts a number of stressors — the claim lives only in this concept file. So
there is no reader-facing error to fix, and the cheapest correct action is to
say so rather than edit a figure nobody prints.

Second, **the two lists are not the same list minus one.** This concept names
in-laws, finances, household labor delegation, communication failures and loss of
intimacy. Publisher copy for *30 Lessons for Loving* names child-rearing, work,
money and in-laws. Only in-laws is common to both, and money/finances is arguably
a second. Changing five to four would have meant silently picking one framing over
the other on no evidence — which is the failure this whole ledger exists to
prevent.

**What would settle it:** the book's own text. Until then, a drafter should use
the individual stressors this concept names, each on its own merits, and should
NOT write "Pillemer identifies five major stressors" or any other counted claim.
The count is the unverified part; the items are ordinary and unremarkable.

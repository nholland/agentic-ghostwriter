# Diogenes Laertius VII.87-90 (Hicks): eudaimonia and arete

**What it says, plainly:** For the Stoics the goal of life is to live in agreement with nature,
which is the same as living by virtue. Happiness is a life that runs smoothly because it is lived
well. Virtue is excellence: in general, the perfection of anything at what it is for.

# What was read (page-text, OCR, Hicks 1925)

- VII.87: Zeno named the end "life in agreement with nature", "the same as a virtuous life".
- VII.88: "the virtue of the happy man and the smooth current of life" (Greek *eudaimonos aretēn
  kai euroian biou*).
- VII.89: "it is in virtue that happiness consists"; virtue is "a harmonious disposition".
- VII.90: "Virtue, in the first place, is in one sense the perfection of anything in general, say
  of a statue".

(Short phrases above are recorded so a later desk can find them; they are not cleared for print.)

# Glosses the Researcher can stand behind (plain English, no quotation marks)

- **Arete** (Greek, usually translated "virtue" or "excellence"): being excellent at what you are
  for. For the Stoics, a person's arete is good character: wisdom, justice, courage and
  self-control.
- **Eudaimonia** (Greek, usually translated "happiness" or "flourishing"): a life going well as a
  whole, not a passing good mood. The Stoics held that it comes from living by virtue.

# Cautions for Ch16

- **The author's extension.** In Stoic texts both words describe a person, not a couple. Applying
  them to the relationship ("the relationship ... at its highest potential, to be at peace, and to
  be in a state of flourishing") is the author's own step; the prose should present it as his,
  not as Stoic doctrine.
- **"Maximizing all of your potential."** The author's gloss of arete leans toward potential and
  performance. The Stoic sense is excellence of character. `02-audience.md` uses the same
  potential framing ("living up to one's highest potential"), so the book's own definition is
  consistent with his; the brief flags the nuance and does not overrule it.
- **Image risk.** "Smooth current of life" is a river image, and VII.90's example is a statue
  (Ch16 already has "married to a stone"). Do not import either into a chapter whose one image is
  the bank.
- **Spelling.** `02-audience.md` and the outline for Ch18 write *Arête*; the Ch16 outline writes
  *arete*. A house spelling is not ruled. Arete has not appeared in any refined chapter as of
  2026-11-04; Ch16 would be its first printed use.

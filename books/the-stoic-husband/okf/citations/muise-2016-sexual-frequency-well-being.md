# Muise, Schimmack & Impett (2016) — Sexual Frequency and Well-Being

Across three studies (N=30,645 combined), the association between sexual
frequency and well-being is **curvilinear**, not linear: well-being
increases with frequency up to about once a week, then plateaus — more
frequent sex beyond that point isn't associated with additional
well-being. In the two studies restricted to people in relationships,
relationship satisfaction mediated the link between frequency and
well-being. One notable comparison from the study: the well-being
difference between having sex once a week versus less than once a month
was larger than the well-being difference associated with a $50–75K vs.
$15–25K income gap.

# Jack & Dill (1992) — The Silencing the Self Scale

A validated psychological construct: the habitual suppression of one's own
feelings, needs, and positions in order to create and preserve a sense of
safety and intimacy in a relationship. Four subscales, including "care as
self-sacrifice" and "silencing the self" directly. Three decades of
follow-on research (126+ studies per a 2025 review) link higher
self-silencing to worse individual and relational outcomes.

# Why this fits Chapter 9

This gives "happy wife, happy life" treated as total strategy a real name
and a real research trail — not just a folk observation, but a documented
pattern with documented costs. It's the academic backbone for the chapter's
opening claim: chronic accommodation isn't peace, it's a known risk factor.

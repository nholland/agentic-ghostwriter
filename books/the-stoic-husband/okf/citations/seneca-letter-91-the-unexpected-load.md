# Seneca — Letter 91: The Unexpected Puts the Heaviest Load

> "It is the unexpected that puts the heaviest load upon us. Our minds should be sent forward in advance to meet all problems, and we should consider, not what is wont to happen, but what can happen."
> — *Epistulae Morales*, Letter 91, Gummere trans.

# Why This Is Used

Ch3's grounding for premeditatio malorum -- pre-living the hard conversation so
the reaction isn't an ambush. Seneca states the mechanism directly: it is the
unexpectedness, not the difficulty, that does the damage.

> Section 3: "it is the unexpected that puts the heaviest load upon us." Section 4: "Our minds should be sent forward in advance to meet all problems"
**Surrounding context:** In section 3 Seneca explains why Liberalis has been shaken by the destruction of Lyons and then discusses how surprise increases the weight of calamity. Section 4 begins with the principle that nothing ought to be unexpected, after which Seneca says the mind should be sent ahead to anticipate possible problems.
**Notes:** SILENT SPLICE. Both fragments are genuinely Gummere but we join them into a continuous two-sentence quotation when the source does not. In section 3 the first fragment begins lowercase 'it' after a semicolon, not capitalized as a new sentence. Two further sentences about strangeness and surprise follow it. Section 4 then adds a transitional statement that nothing should be unexpected before our second sentence begins. A publishable quotation must either quote the portions separately or signal the omission with an ellipsis.

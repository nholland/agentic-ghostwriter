# Brickman & Campbell (1971) / Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman (1978) — The Hedonic Treadmill

The term "hedonic treadmill" describes how large life changes — winning
money, losing physical ability — produce only a temporary shift in
wellbeing before adaptation pulls a person back toward a stable baseline.
The 1978 study is the field's classic empirical anchor for this claim,
and its actual numbers are more precise (and more honest) than the "full
bounce-back either direction" version that circulates in pop-science
retellings: winners essentially matched controls (no lasting boost, and
notably reduced pleasure from small daily things); accident victims
landed meaningfully below controls, not equal to them, while still
retaining real hope for the future and real capacity for daily pleasure.

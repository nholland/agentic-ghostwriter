# Christensen & Heavey (1990) — Demand/Withdraw Pattern

Foundational study establishing the demand-withdraw pattern in marital
conflict: one partner pushes for change (demands, criticizes, pursues) while
the other withdraws (avoids, defends, disengages). The study found gender
interacted with who wanted the change under discussion — the pattern isn't
simply "women always demand, men always withdraw" as a fixed trait, it
tracks with who has more invested in a given change happening. Later
research (see PMC review, link below) links higher frequency of this
pattern to greater relationship distress.

# Important distinction for this book's use

This study is about conflict-engagement *style* and its cost to the
relationship, not a direct measurement of whether a more emotionally
reactive/expressive partner's preferences get acted on more often across
many small, non-conflict decisions. The book's
[Temperament Asymmetry](/frameworks/temperament-asymmetry-who-cares-more-wins.md)
framework's specific "who cares more wins" claim is a reasonable
extrapolation adjacent to this literature, not a literal finding of it —
keep that distinction explicit in prose rather than citing this as direct
proof.

**Source's own wording:**

> 31 couples assessed in two conflict situations: one in which the husband wanted a change in the wife, and one in which the wife wanted a change in the husband.
**Surrounding context:** Husbands, wives and observers all showed a main effect of gender (wife-demand/husband-withdraw more likely than the reverse) AND an interaction with conflict structure: wife-demand/husband-withdraw exceeded the reverse only when discussing a change the wife wanted.
**Notes:** TWO CAVEATS. n=31 couples — small, and worth not leaning on as though it were a large study. And the interaction matters: the gendered pattern held reliably only when the topic was a change the WIFE wanted, which is a narrower claim than 'men withdraw'. Heavey, Layne & Christensen (1993) is the replication and extension if a stronger cite is wanted.

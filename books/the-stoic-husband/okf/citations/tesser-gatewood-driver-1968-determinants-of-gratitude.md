# Source finding

Later reviews credit this study with showing that how grateful a person feels for help depends on how valuable the help is to them, how costly it was to the giver, and whether the giver seemed to mean well. It is the root of the 'benefit appraisal' line of gratitude research.

# Limits

Not about marriage. Primary text unread; design and sample unknown. Supports at most: people feel more grateful when they see what help cost the giver, that it was meant for their good, and that it was worth something to them. Do not claim cost alone produces gratitude.

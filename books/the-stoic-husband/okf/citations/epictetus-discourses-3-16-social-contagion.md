# Epictetus, Discourses III.16 — The Soot and the Charcoal

Epictetus warns that regular company — talk, meals, ordinary sociability —
is never neutral. Either you bring the other man over to your way of living
or he brings you over to his, the way a burning coal and a quenched one
cannot sit together and both stay as they were. He closes with the blunt
version: you cannot keep company with a man covered in soot and stay clean.

# Why this fits Chapter 10

The rebuilt chapter treats friends not as a domain of their own but as one of
three pressures testing Tier 1 — the standing agreement that the couple does
not run each other down to friends or family. This passage carries that
pressure in a sentence, without the chapter having to argue for social
contagion empirically.

That matters practically. The chapter's original spec leaned on Christakis &
Fowler's divorce-clustering study for the same point, and that source carries
a live methodological challenge (see
[the concept](/citations/christakis-fowler-divorce-clustering.md)). Epictetus
makes the claim the chapter actually needs — proximity transfers — as a moral
observation rather than a contested effect size, and does it in an image a
reader remembers.

It also fits `04-archetype.md`'s research guidance to prefer the
less-quoted passage: this is not one of the Ryan Holiday standards.

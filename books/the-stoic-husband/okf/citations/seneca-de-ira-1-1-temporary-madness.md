# Seneca, De Ira I.1.2 (Basore): anger as temporary madness

**What it says, plainly:** Wise men before Seneca called anger a short spell of insanity. A man
in anger has lost command of himself: he forgets decency, forgets the people he is bound to, and
cannot hear reason.

# Wording (as read at page-text, OCR of Basore 1928, p. 107)

Recommended span (one sentence; no dash; marginal-note OCR noise between "Certain" and "wise" removed, see verification_note):

> Certain wise men, therefore, have claimed that anger is temporary madness.

Optional continuation, same paragraph, clean in the OCR except that "self-control" is split across a line break as "self- / control" (the hyphen is read as the word's own) (use only if the chapter needs the
"ties" point; quote it as a separate fragment, never spliced with a bare ellipsis):

> For it is equally devoid of self-control, forgetful of decency, unmindful of ties, persistent and diligent in whatever it begins, closed to reason and counsel, excited by trifling causes, unfit to discern the right and true

Latin, same scan p. 106: *Quidam itaque e sapientibus viris iram dixerunt brevem insaniam*.
Latin *necessitudinum immemor* is Basore's "unmindful of ties" (ties of kinship and close
relationship).

# Why it fits Ch16

The author's own phrase was "a state of temporary madness"; Basore's English matches it word for
word, so the chapter can give the author's realization and then show it is the old text's own
wording. "Unmindful of ties" is the marriage-relevant clause: anger forgets the people a man is
bound to.

# Sprecher (2001) — Equity and Social Exchange in Dating Couples

Part of the equity theory research tradition (Walster, Hatfield, Traupmann
and colleagues). This study found that feeling *underbenefited* — that you
give more than you get — predicts lower satisfaction, lower commitment, and
a higher likelihood of breakup over time. Notably, feeling *overbenefited*
(getting more than you give) did not carry a symmetrical cost — the
asymmetry itself is part of the finding.

**Surrounding context:** Longitudinal study of romantic couples testing equity against other social exchange variables (rewards, investments, alternatives). **Notes:** TWO CAVEATS. The sample is DATING couples, not married ones — do not cite it as a finding about marriages. And the asymmetry is the actual result: feeling under-benefited predicts dissatisfaction, feeling over-benefited does not.

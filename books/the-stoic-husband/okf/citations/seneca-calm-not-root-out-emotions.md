# Seneca — "Reason wishes to give calm to our emotions, not to root them out"

Seneca, *De Ira* (On Anger), Book II, §3:

> Latin: "Ratio affectibus tranquillitatem dare vult, non tollere."
> English: "Reason wishes to give calm to our emotions, not to root them out."

Also rendered, more modernly: "Reason seeks to calm our emotions, not to destroy
them."

**Why this matters:** It directly refutes the Stoicism-as-suppression misread
that Part I must counter in every chapter — *apatheia* is tranquility within
feeling, not the absence of feeling. The best single sentence for that purpose.

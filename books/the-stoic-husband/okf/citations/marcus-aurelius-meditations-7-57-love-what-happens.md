# Marcus Aurelius — "Love Only That Which Happens to Thee" (Meditations 7.57)

> "Love that only which happens to thee and is spun with the thread of thy
> destiny. For what is more suitable?"
> — *Meditations* 7.57, Long trans. (translation choice and exact wording to
> be confirmed)

# Why This Fits Ch11

Ch11's third claim is that a man can call a hard season what it is without
surrendering to it. The pitfall is that this reads as a demand for gratitude,
which is an insult to a man in a bad year and which the chapter must not make.
Marcus is not asking for gratitude. He is refusing to spend the year at war
with the fact of the year, which is a different and much smaller ask.

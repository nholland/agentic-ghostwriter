# Gillespie, Peterson & Lever (2019) — Fairness in Housework and Shared Expenses

A large study (N = 10,236) of different-gender partners examining how
perceived fairness over housework and shared expenses relates to
relationship satisfaction and sex frequency. Key findings:

- Evaluations of unfairness **to oneself** are a stronger predictor of
  relationship quality than perceived unfairness **to one's partner** — i.e.,
  feeling shortchanged yourself matters more to the relationship's health
  than worrying your partner is being shortchanged.
- Fairness evaluations over **shared expenses** are a stronger predictor of
  relationship quality than perceived equity in **housework** — money
  fairness carries more weight than chore fairness.

This directly resolves the open gap in
[Household Labor Distribution and Caretaker Burden Research](/citations/household-labor-and-caretaker-burden.md):
it gives a specific, citable, large-N finding on the fairness side of the
ledger problem, and it does so in plain "fairness" language rather than
"emotional labor" or "mental load" — both terms `02-audience.md` flags as
off-register for this book.

# Source finding

As the abstract is reported in search: elementary classrooms of 8-11-year-olds were randomly assigned to a curriculum teaching the appraisal of benefit exchanges (intent, cost, benefit) or a control. The intervention increased awareness of those appraisals, gratitude and positive affect. A daily version raised gratitude two days later; a weekly version showed gratitude effects up to five months later. Thank-you-card behavior was also reported (figure search-level only).

# Limits

Children in schools, not spouses. The nearest research parallel to the author's account of his children learning what dinner costs, but the curriculum taught noticing, not cooking. Do not print the thank-you-card percentage or the five-month duration without the primary.

# "Emotional bank account": attribution

**What can be said in print:** Stephen Covey made the "emotional bank account" famous in *The 7
Habits of Highly Effective People* (1989). That is safe: it credits without claiming he invented
it. "Covey coined" is NOT safe; nothing read establishes first use.

**Gottman:** uses the same image for turning toward a partner's small bids. Under the outline's
device cap (inbox 111: Gottman's one remaining headline is Ch26), Ch16 may give Gottman a clause
at most, or leave him out.

# How the author's version differs (his words, from the interview; for the brief)

- He teaches it to his sons for every relationship: teachers, coaches, girlfriends, wife.
- "Green everywhere", greenest with her.
- "The bank doesn't ever owe you money" (Round 2): it counts only his own deposits, never hers,
  which is how it stays clear of Ch7's tally.
- The sun goes down: deposits are what the marriage lives on in the dark stretch.

The credit line in prose should come before or right after the first use of "bank", so the reader
never takes the frame as the author's invention.

# Cautions

- Do not attribute any specific wording to Covey or Gottman; neither text was read.
- Do not reuse Gottman's bid numbers here (86/33 are Ch11's; see

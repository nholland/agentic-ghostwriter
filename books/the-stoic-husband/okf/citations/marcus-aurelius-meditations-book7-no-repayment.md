# Marcus Aurelius — Meditations Book 7: Against Seeking Repayment

A passage from Book 7 of the *Meditations*, commonly rendered along the
lines of:

> "When you have done a good act and another has received it, why do you
> still look for a third thing besides — credit for having done good, or a
> return for it?"

Marcus's point: a good act is complete in itself. Seeking a third thing
after it — recognition, gratitude, or reciprocal benefit — is what "fools"
do. This is a concise, single-line Stoic anchor for the exact payoff Ch7
needs after walking through the small-rocks/big-rocks ledger problem: the
husband who has stopped expecting his contributions to be tallied and
repaid is doing what Marcus describes here.

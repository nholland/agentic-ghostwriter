# Basson (2000) — Responsive vs. Spontaneous Sexual Desire

Basson's circular model of sexual response splits desire into two kinds:

- **Spontaneous desire** — wanting sex out of nowhere, before arousal
  begins. Common early in a relationship or after a long separation.
- **Responsive desire** — desire that emerges *after* arousal or erotic
  context has already started. The norm, not the exception, in long-term
  relationships.

Basson's key implication: for a partner who rarely experiences spontaneous
desire, an overture, closeness, or initiation from the other partner can
be what predisposes desire to show up — rather than desire being a
prerequisite that has to arrive first. This is the legitimate research
anchor for "initiation can precede the feeling," distinct from and more
credible than pop-self-help framings of "scheduled sex."

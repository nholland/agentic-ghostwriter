# Muise, Impett, Kogan & Desmarais (2013) — Sexual Communal Strength

Defines **sexual communal strength**: the extent to which a person is
motivated to be non-contingently responsive to a partner's sexual needs —
engaging even when their own desire is low, but out of care for the
partner's wellbeing rather than to avoid conflict. People high in this
trait maintained sexual desire over a 4-month follow-up period; people low
in it saw desire decline over the same window. A related study (PLOS ONE,
2019) extended the same protective pattern specifically to couples where
one partner has chronic low sexual interest/arousal — high sexual communal
strength in that partner still predicted better sexual well-being for both
people.

**This is distinct from avoidance-motivated compliance**
([Impett & Peplau, 2005](/citations/impett-peplau-2005-approach-avoidance-sexual-motives.md)):
sexual communal strength operates through an *approach* motive (caring
for the partner, wanting to sustain intimacy), not an *avoidance* motive
(preventing a fight). Both involve engaging without one's own desire
present, but they predict opposite outcomes.

**The critical caveat**: "unmitigated sexual communion" — prioritizing the
partner's sexual needs to the total exclusion of one's own — predicts
*worse* sexual well-being and *more* sexual distress. This is bounded
generosity, not self-erasure or martyrdom.

# Gordon et al. (2012) — Feeling Appreciated

The paper proposes a reciprocal appreciation loop. Felt appreciation and
appreciation of a partner were associated in its measures; appreciation also
traveled with responsiveness and commitment. The designs do not prove that
one partner's gratitude causes the other's or that the relationship will last.

# Why It Matters for Ch12

It is the evidence under the author's first link. "If a partner doesn't feel
wanted, valued, loved, and cherished, then this is where cracks begin" is a
strong claim, and this is the paper that lets a version of it be made honestly:
felt appreciation is bound up with responsiveness and commitment. The
observational study does not establish the chapter's full proposed sequence
or show that downplaying effort causes infidelity.

# Source access update — 2026-09-29T20:44:31-05:00

Original article now retrieved and read at https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/To_Have_and_to_Hold-_Gratitude_Promotes_Relationship_Maintenance_in_Intimate_Bonds.pdf . Earlier access restrictions above are historical, not the current evidence ceiling. Abstract and Study 2 methods/results inspected. Study 2 began with 99 undergraduates, only 4% married; daily measures covered seven nights and the follow-up was nine months. Appreciation predicted later commitment and relationship persistence, but the persistence association became marginal after controlling baseline satisfaction. Felt appreciation did not predict change in own appreciation across nine months. These are observational associations, not a randomized gratitude intervention. Do not turn the paper's causal-sounding model into demonstrated causality or guaranteed reciprocity. This update responds to Ch14 interview round 4; Ch12's existing use remains relevant. No status advancement or verbatim quotation.

# Randall & Bodenmann (2009) — How Chronic External Stress Erodes a Relationship

Bodenmann's model holds that chronic *minor* external stress is more corrosive
to a marriage than acute major stress, because it works gradually and below
the couple's notice. The damage runs through mediating mechanisms rather than
through the stressor itself:

- less time spent together, and therefore fewer shared experiences
- weakened feelings of togetherness ("we-ness")
- decreased self-disclosure between partners
- degraded dyadic coping (the couple's capacity to handle stress as a unit)
- more negative and hostile interaction, and more withdrawal

# Why This Fits Ch11

This is the chapter's mechanism, arrived at independently. Ch11 argues that a
man in a long hard season economizes on himself, cuts the parts of him that
cost energy and pay nothing back today, and never notices because the only
metric he grades himself on ("did I keep going") returns yes the whole time.
Bodenmann's model says the same thing from the couple's side and adds the
finding the chapter most needs: the invisibility is not incidental, it is the
characteristic feature of this kind of stress.

It also independently supports the chapter's "the season has no edges"
observation — that a bad stretch, unlike a fight, has no date on it, which is
why a man can be years into one and still call it a rough patch.

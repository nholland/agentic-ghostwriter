# Gross & John (2003) — Expressive Suppression and Interpersonal Functioning

Habitual suppressors — people who default to holding emotion in rather than
reappraising or expressing it — show worse interpersonal functioning and
*more* felt negative emotion internally than people who use other
regulation strategies, despite outwardly appearing calmer. Suppression
doesn't neutralize the feeling; it just relocates the cost.

# Why this fits Chapter 9

This is the direct evidence for the chapter's "the debt accrues even in
silence" claim: choosing not to say something doesn't make the want or the
frustration disappear, it just moves the cost inward, where it compounds
until something eventually forces it out — disproportionately, per the
gunnysacking pattern this chapter also names.

# Marcus Aurelius — Meditations 12.4: Concealing Our Thoughts

"If a god appeared to us — or a wise human being, even — and prohibited us
from concealing our thoughts or imagining anything without immediately
shouting it out, we wouldn't make it through a single day... That's how much
we value other people's opinions — instead of our own." (Gregory Hays
translation, *Meditations* 12.4, wording as corroborated across secondary
sources — not yet confirmed against primary text.)

# Why this fits Chapter 9

Marcus isn't arguing for radical transparency about every passing thought —
he's naming, almost self-deprecatingly, how much fear of other people's
reactions shapes what we're willing to say out loud. That's the exact
mechanism behind "happy wife, happy life" treated as total strategy: not
malice, not laziness, just the ordinary human weight of not wanting to
disturb someone whose opinion of us matters enormously.

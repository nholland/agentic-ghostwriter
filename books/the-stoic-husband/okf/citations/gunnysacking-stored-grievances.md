# Gunnysacking — Stored Grievances

Storing up unaddressed grievances — small compliances, unstated wants,
irritations let go without comment — until the accumulated weight bursts,
producing a reaction that looks wildly out of proportion to whatever small
thing actually triggered it. Named for a gunny sack, filled past its
capacity.

# Why this fits Chapter 9

This is the direct name for the chapter's central cost: not that his wife
stops trusting him, but that the backlog he's been keeping in silence
eventually discharges — disproportionately, confusingly, and unfairly to
her, since she never had visibility into what was accumulating.

# Related

- Plausibly explained (not proven) by [Gross & John (2003) — Expressive Suppression](/citations/gross-john-2003-expressive-suppression.md) —
  suppression's accumulating internal cost as the likely mechanism behind
  the eventual burst.
- Distinct from but adjacent to [The Nail Parable](/frameworks/the-nail-parable.md)
  (Ch4) — the nail parable is about repeated *anger incidents* accumulating
  into disdain; gunnysacking is about *unspoken grievances* accumulating
  into a single disproportionate discharge. Related mechanisms, different
  chapters, worth keeping the distinction explicit if both are ever
  referenced near each other.

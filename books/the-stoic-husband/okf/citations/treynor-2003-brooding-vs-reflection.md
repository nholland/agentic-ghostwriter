# Treynor, Gonzalez & Nolen-Hoeksema (2003) — Brooding vs. Reflective Pondering

Rumination is not one thing. It splits cleanly into two:

- **Brooding** — dwelling on how bad the situation is and what it says about
  you, with no solution in view. Maladaptive.
- **Reflective pondering** — turning the same situation over deliberately,
  aimed at working out what to do. Adaptive.

Same posture from outside. Opposite outcomes.

# Why This Is Load-Bearing for Ch11

The chapter's failure mode is a man who goes inward. But this book asks a man
to go inward constantly — the three-second window, the ruling faculty, the
virtue question. "Don't go inward" would contradict everything before it.

This finding supplies the precise line instead. Interiority aimed at a
decision is the work. Interiority that circles the same ground without ever
arriving at one is the poison. The man in Ch11 is not thinking too much. He is
brooding, which is a different activity that happens to look identical from
the outside and even to him.

It also pairs with the chapter's taxonomy: brooding is what a man does with a
door he refuses to walk through. Reflective pondering is what finds the door,
or establishes there isn't one.

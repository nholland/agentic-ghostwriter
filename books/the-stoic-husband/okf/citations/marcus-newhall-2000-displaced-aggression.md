# Marcus-Newhall et al. (2000) — Displaced Aggression Is Alive and Well

Displaced aggression: provoked by one source a person cannot or will not
confront, someone aggresses against a different, innocent target. The
meta-analysis found it robust, mean effect size +0.54.

**Triggered displaced aggression** is the variant that matters here. A minor
or trivial offense — the triggering event — sets off a response sized for
everything the person was already carrying, not for the trigger.

One moderator finding is directly relevant: the more negative the setting in
which the person and the target interact, the larger the displaced
aggression. A house already under strain amplifies it.

# Why This Fits Ch11

The author's own framing: the man goes inward, self-poisons, and eventually
lets the poison out on his wife. This names how that happens and, crucially,
*what it looks like when it does* — not a confrontation about the real thing,
which he still has not raised, but a response two sizes too big to something
that did not deserve it. She cannot source it, because the source was never
in the room.

That is also why the chapter's silent man is not neutral. He is not sparing
her. He is choosing the delivery method.

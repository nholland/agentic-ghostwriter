# Marcus Aurelius, Meditations IV.4 and VI.44 (Long): the world as one city

**What it says, plainly:** Because people share reason, they share a law, and so they are
fellow-citizens of one community. A Stoic belongs to his own town and also to the human race.

# Wording (as read at page-text, Long, Gutenberg #15877)

IV.4 (recommended span):

> if this is so, we are fellow-citizens; if this is so, we are members of some political community; if this is so, the world is in a manner a state.

VI.44 (recommended span):

> But my nature is rational and social; and my city and country, so far as I am Antoninus, is Rome, but so far as I am a man, it is the world.

# Gloss the Researcher can stand behind (paraphrase, no quotation marks)

*Cosmopolis* is Greek for "world-city": the Stoic idea that every person is a citizen of one
human community, bound to the others by shared reason, and that justice is what a member of that
community owes the rest. The two passages above are Marcus's own statement of it. The author's
step, from the world-city to his marriage ("we are all here for each other"), is his application:
if he owes justice to strangers, he owes it first to her.

# Cautions

- Long's IV.4 is a chain of "if this is so" clauses; quote from "we are fellow-citizens" only, not
  the whole chain (reading level).
- VI.44 is the plainer of the two for a sixth-grade target.
- Ch15 spent Marcus I.9; this is a different passage. Marcus 11.18 is capped (outline, inbox 111).

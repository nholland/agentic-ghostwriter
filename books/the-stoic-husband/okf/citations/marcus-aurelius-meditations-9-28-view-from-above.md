# Marcus Aurelius — Meditations 9.28: The View From Above

> "Take a view from above — look at the thousands of flocks and herds, the thousands of human ceremonies, every sort of voyage in storm or calm, the range of creation, combination, and extinction."
> — *Meditations* 9.28

# Why This Is Used

Ch5's scale-shift. The view from above is what makes a fight smaller without
making it unimportant -- the move the chapter needs so that not-winning stops
feeling like losing.

**Verdict:** `WRONG_LOCATOR`
**Edition:** Martin Hammond, Penguin Classics, 2006; compared with George Long, Little Brown, 1889
**Locator:** Meditations IX.30 — NOT IX.28.
**URL:** https://fliphtml5.com/wtlgs/nacu/Marcus_Aurelius_%28Emperor_of_Rome%29%2C_Martin_Hammond%2C_Diskin_Clay_-Meditations-Penguin_Books_%282006%29/
**Confidence:** medium
**Source's own wording:**

> Hammond IX.30: "Take a view from above - look at the thousands of flocks and herds, the thousands of human ceremonies, every sort of voyage" Long IX.30: "Look down from above on the countless herds of men and their countless solemnities, and the infinitely varied voyagings in storms and calms"
**Surrounding context:** Hammond introduces the passage immediately after warning against being seduced into pompous pride. The numbered reflection then surveys animal herds, human ceremonies, voyages, and the processes of coming into existence and passing away. Hammond's own notes cross-reference the conceit explicitly as 9.30.
**Notes:** Wording is substantially Hammond 2006 but our section number is wrong. Hammond's own commentary identifies it as 9.30, and Long has the view-from-above passage at IX.30. Hays 9.28 is an unrelated reflection on the world's cycles, which rules out an edition-numbering explanation. IMPORTANT: the facsimile extraction renders the punctuation after 'above' as a hyphen-minus, which does NOT prove the printed dash glyph, so our claim that the em dash is physically confirmed should not stand. A visual check of the printed Penguin page is still required.

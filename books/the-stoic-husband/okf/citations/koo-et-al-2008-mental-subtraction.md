# Source finding

Koo, M., Algoe, S. B., Wilson, T. D., & Gilbert, D. T. (2008). It's a wonderful life: Mentally subtracting positive events improves people's affective states, contrary to their affective forecasts. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 95(5), 1217–1224.

# Findings and limits

Earlier experiments found greater immediate positive affect after considering how a good event might not have happened. Study 4 randomly assigned 88 people in relationships of at least five years to writing conditions. Only respondents at or above the sample's average baseline relationship satisfaction were included. Writing for 15–20 minutes about how they might never have met or ended up with their partner produced a greater immediate satisfaction increase than the comparison conditions in the planned contrast (p=.049). This is a small, selected, mainly female sample; it is not evidence for distressed marriages or lasting effects. The exercise concerns the contingency of meeting, not imagining a spouse's death. Original PDF methods/results inspected. Do not claim the causal mechanism was settled: mediation evidence in Study 2 was tentative. No quotation transcribed.

# Marcus Aurelius — "It Is Fortunate That This Has Happened" (Meditations 4.49)

> "It's unfortunate that this has happened. No. It is fortunate that this has
> happened and I've remained unharmed by it, not shattered by the present or
> frightened of the future."
> — *Meditations* 4.49, Hays trans. (wording to be confirmed against a
> physical copy)

# Why This Fits Ch11

The chapter's second claim is that a steady man is not an immovable one. He
bends, he adapts, he gets tired; what makes him steady is that he keeps
returning to himself rather than drifting into whoever the circumstances are
making him. Marcus is doing exactly that move here, in his own voice, mid-
argument with himself. The thing he claims as undamaged is not his comfort or
his circumstances. It is the part of him that decides.

It also guards against the misread the whole book has to keep countering
(`02-audience.md`, reader belief #1: Stoicism means not being affected).
Marcus is not saying the event did not land. He is saying it did not remake
him.

# Sprecher, Schmeeckle & Felmlee (2006) — The Principle of Least Interest

Willard Waller's 1938 "principle of least interest" holds that the partner
who is less invested in a relationship holds more relative power over it.
This 2006 study tested the idea empirically on a longitudinal sample of
dating couples: 58% of couples reported unequal emotional involvement
between partners, the imbalance was stable over time (correlation of .62
across waves), and the less-involved partner reported more perceived
control over whether the relationship continued. Couples with unequal
involvement were less satisfied overall and more likely to break up.

# Important distinction for this book's use

This is the classic version of "whoever cares less has more power" — and
it's about investment in the *relationship as a whole* (an exit-power,
bargaining-power frame), not about a specific partner having stronger
opinions about more individual day-to-day decisions. The book's own
"who cares more wins" idea (see
[Temperament Asymmetry](/frameworks/temperament-asymmetry-who-cares-more-wins.md))
is adjacent to this concept, not identical to it — cite this as the
intellectual ancestor of the idea, with the distinction made explicit in
prose rather than implying the study proves the chapter's specific claim.

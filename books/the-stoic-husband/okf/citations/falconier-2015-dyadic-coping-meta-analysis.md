# Falconier et al. (2015) — Dyadic Coping and Relationship Satisfaction

Dyadic coping is what a couple does with stress *as a unit*: one partner
communicates that they are under strain, the other responds. It is a two-sided
behavior, and it is the thing that stops existing when a man in a hard season
goes quiet.

Across 72 independent samples and 17,856 participants, total dyadic coping
correlated with relationship satisfaction at r = .45 — a large effect by the
standards of this literature — and the association held across gender, age,
relationship length, education level, and nationality.

# Why This Fits Ch11

The chapter argues that a man under sustained load cuts the wrong things
first: he protects the tasks and spends the contact. This meta-analysis names
what the contact actually was. A man who stops saying he is struggling has
removed his half of dyadic coping; a man who stops responding when she says
she is struggling has removed the other half. He has not merely gone quiet, he
has deleted the couple's mechanism for handling the very season he is trying
to carry them through.

# Source finding and limits

Direct Gummere online text inspected, Letter 2.5–6. Seneca uses sufficient possessions as the measure rather than continuing acquisitiveness. The house wording is “first, to have what is necessary, and, second, to have what is enough.” Bertolotti shortens this into a paraphrase. The narrow screenshot was initially misread as loving; the full article actually says having. No loving-wording defect exists. The preceding thought in 2.5 is expressly borrowed from Epicurus.

Use as a brief philosophical basis for enough; it does not itself teach gratitude training or marital appreciation. A spouse is a person, not property. Gummere online transcription, not a physical house-copy check.

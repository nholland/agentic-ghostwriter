# The Answer To "Is A Date A Week Actually A Thing"

Partly. There is a real report behind it. The report is cross-sectional survey
work from a marriage-promotion research centre, and the weekly threshold is
the strongest-sounding line in it rather than an experimentally derived dose.

The peer-reviewed literature does not prescribe a frequency at all. What it
supports is a different claim: that shared activity does something when both
partners are engaged in it (Girme et al. 2014), and that what the time is
spent on matters more than that it happened (Aron et al. 2000; Harasymchuk et
al. 2021).

# What This Should Do To The Chapter

The chapter currently prints: "We've heard the same advice you've heard, that
you should get out on a date once a week." That sentence is SAFE AS WRITTEN,
because it reports the advice as advice rather than as research, and then
immediately admits to failing it. No repair needed.

What is now available, and was not in round 1, is the option of a single clause
that makes the chapter smarter than the advice: the frequency is the part
nobody has actually established, and the thing that has been tested is what
you do with the time.

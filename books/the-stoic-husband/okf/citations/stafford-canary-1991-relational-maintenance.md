# The Five

Positivity (being pleasant and uncritical, making time cheerful), openness
(talking about the relationship itself), assurances (saying and showing that
this is a future, not an arrangement), social networks (shared friends and
family), sharing tasks (carrying your half of the work of a life).

# Why It Earns A Place In Ch12 — And Why It Might Not

**For:** it is the direct, peer-reviewed answer to "is there research on
romance," and the author asked that question. It is also the honest cousin of
the love-languages taxonomy: a list of channels care travels down, derived
from data rather than from a counselling practice.

**Against:** the chapter is full at 1,202 words and a five-item list is
expensive. A list the reader has to hold is a different thing from an image he
can act on. If anything from this literature goes in, it should be the one
finding in Ogolsky & Bowers that the chapter's mechanism needs — that
maintenance effort falls off as the relationship gets older — not the taxonomy.

# Kahneman & Deaton (2010) vs. Killingsworth (2021) vs. Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers (2023) — Income and Happiness

This is a genuinely contested area of the science, resolved more recently
and more narrowly than most popular accounts (including the source
material behind this concept) suggest. The honest version: money keeps
producing real gains in emotional wellbeing for most people well past any
"modest" threshold — the plateau effect is real, but only for a
minority of unhappier people, and it sits closer to $100,000 than
$75,000. This makes money a weaker example of the hedonic-treadmill
mechanism than lottery winnings or a promotion — worth flagging as the
complicating case rather than using it as a clean confirming example.

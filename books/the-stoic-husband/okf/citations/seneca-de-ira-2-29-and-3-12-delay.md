# Seneca, De Ira II.29 and III.12 (Stewart): delay is the best remedy for anger

**What it says, plainly:** Wait before answering. And judge what she meant, not only what she did.

# Wording (as read; copied verbatim from the research file)

> II.29.1: The greatest remedy for anger is delay

> Latin, as given in the research file: *Maximum remedium irae mora est*

> III.12.4: No one will postpone his anger: yet delay is the best remedy for it, because it allows its first glow to subside

> Adjacent, III.12.2: No one considers the intention of the doer, but merely the thing done

> Adjacent, III.12 (subsection not given in the research file): A large part of mankind manufacture their own grievances either by entertaining unfounded suspicions or by exaggerating trifles.

**Evidence label:** PRIMARY READ at page-text, per sections 2 and 4 of the research file. This
establishes wording only. Not `verified`.

# Basore's wording, read 2026-10-24 (page-text, II.29.1 only)

From the archive.org scan of Heinemann 1928 (moralessayswithe01seneuoft), OCR with noise (some letters in the scan are garbled and are read here as the evident words):

> The best corrective of anger lies in delay. Beg this concession from anger at the first, not in order that it may pardon, but in order that it may judge. Its first assaults are heavy; it will leave off if it waits.

(De Ira II.29, Basore's page headed "ON ANGER, II. XXVIII. 8-XXIX. 3".) Stewart's version of the first sentence is "The greatest remedy for anger is delay". III.12 has not been re-read in Basore.

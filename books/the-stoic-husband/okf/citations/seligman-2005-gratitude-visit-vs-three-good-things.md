# Seligman, Steen, Park & Peterson (2005) — Gratitude Visit vs. Three Good Things

Two different gratitude practices from the same study, with two different
payoffs. The gratitude visit — naming a specific person, a specific
thing they did, and its specific impact, delivered directly to them — is
the single biggest happiness bump this line of research has produced.
It's also one of the exercises that fades fastest, gone within months.
Three Good Things — a nightly list of three good things and what caused
them, kept for a week — produces a smaller lift up front but is the one
that's still measurable six months later. A book making a durability
claim about gratitude should name Three Good Things, not the gratitude
visit; a book making an intensity/meaning claim should name the
gratitude visit, not durability.

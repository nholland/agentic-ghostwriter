# Seneca — Letter 75: Letters Should Be Just What Conversation Would Be

> "I prefer that my letters should be just what my conversation would be if you and I were sitting in one another's company or taking walks together — spontaneous and easy; for my letters have nothing strained or artificial about them."
> — *Epistulae Morales*, Letter 75, Gummere trans.

# Why This Is Used

Ch5's model for direct speech: say the thing the way you would say it, without
strain or performance. Seneca is describing his own prose, which is exactly the
register the chapter asks a man to bring to conflict.

**Surrounding context:** Letter 75 opens with Lucilius's complaint that Seneca's letters seem carelessly written. Seneca answers by comparing the desired style of his letters with ordinary conversation between the two men, then explains that even when arguing he does not want the theatrical gestures of an orator.

# Elie Wiesel — The Opposite of Love Is Indifference

"The opposite of love is not hate, it's indifference." Elie Wiesel, in a
1986 interview, speaking about atrocity and moral indifference generally,
not marriage specifically. This book borrows the line and applies it
narrowly: a man who goes quiet to protect the peace at home isn't
practicing love, he's practicing indifference, and indifference is the
actual opposite of what he's trying to protect.

# Why this fits Chapter 9

The author's framing during the Chapter 9 revision check-in (2026-08-03):
many men don't stay silent from fear of disappointing their wife
specifically. They stay silent because they're already stretched thin by
everything else in life, and risking the one peace they still have feels
too expensive. That's optimizing to avoid unhappiness, not to build
happiness. Wiesel's line names where that trade actually leads: not peace,
apathy. A man who'd rather be quietly unhappy than risk being actively
unhappy is choosing the very thing Wiesel warns is worse than hate.

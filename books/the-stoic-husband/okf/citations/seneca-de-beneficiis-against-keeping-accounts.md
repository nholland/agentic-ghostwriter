# Seneca — De Beneficiis: Against Keeping Accounts of Benefits Given

Seneca's *De Beneficiis* ("On Benefits") is the only complete surviving
ancient work on gift exchange, and its central teaching maps directly onto
Ch7's theme: a true benefactor does not keep a ledger of what he has given.
Per secondary summaries of the text:

- "Donors must be taught not to keep accounts; recipients must be taught
  that they owe even more than they have received."
- A benefit should not be given "out at interest" — the moment a gift is
  given with an expectation of return, it stops functioning as a gift and
  becomes a transaction.
- True generosity is to give, receive, and return benefits *freely* — Seneca
  goes further, urging the recipient to try to outdo the original giver in
  generosity rather than simply settle the account.

This is a more specific and more directly on-theme classical anchor for Ch7
than anything currently in the OKF bundle — the existing Stoic sources for
this chapter (dichotomy of control, the role frame) describe the *structure*
of jurisdiction; Seneca describes the specific *vice* (transactional giving)
that scorekeeping is.

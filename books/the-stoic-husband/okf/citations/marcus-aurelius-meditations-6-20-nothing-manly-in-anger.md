# Marcus Aurelius — Meditations 6.20: Nothing Manly in Being Angry

> "there is nothing manly in being angry, but a gentle calm is both more human and therefore more virile."
> — *Meditations* 6.20

# Why This Is Used

Ch4's climax. Marcus answers the chapter's objection directly -- that refusing
anger is softness -- by asserting the opposite, and he is a man with legions and
no particular incentive toward gentleness.

**Source's own wording:**

> Hammond XI.18: "there is nothing manly in being angry, but a gentle calm is both more human and therefore more virile." Long XI.18: "to be moved by passion is not manly, but that mildness and gentleness"
**Surrounding context:** In Hammond the line occurs during Marcus's extended set of reminders for responding to other people's wrongdoing and anger. He immediately contrasts the angry person with the gentle person who possesses strength and courage. Long VI.20 instead discusses how to react when another person accidentally scratches or wounds you during gymnastic exercise.
**Notes:** The words are genuine Hammond when excerpted mid-sentence, but the citation 6.20 is wrong; correct locus is XI.18. Because no translator was named in the manuscript the line misleadingly appears edition-neutral. It is not: Long's public-domain wording differs. Penguin's official page confirms Hammond 2006; exact text inspected in a third-party facsimile.

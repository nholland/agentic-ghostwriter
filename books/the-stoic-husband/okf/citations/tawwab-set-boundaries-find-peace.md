# Tawwab — Set Boundaries, Find Peace (Boundary as What You Will Do)

Tawwab's central reframe: a boundary is not a request that someone else
change — it's a statement of what you need, and what you yourself will do
if the need isn't respected. The practical sequence: notice the signal
(resentment, dread, exhaustion), clarify the boundary for yourself, state
it specifically and calmly, then enforce it through your own action rather
than repeated explanation.

# Why this fits Chapter 10

This directly backs Claim 3's "he takes the call himself" beat with
concrete, secular script language: naming the specific behavior (a
father-in-law relitigating a joint decision), the feeling and need
("I need you to bring this to both of us, not just her"), and the action
that follows if it continues. It also backs Claim 4's "small, repeated
discipline" framing — Tawwab's own model treats boundary-setting as a
practiced skill enforced through consistent action, not a single dramatic
confrontation, which matches this chapter's existing "not one dramatic
break with a friend group" argument almost exactly.

# Source finding

As the abstract is reported in search: two daily-experience studies of romantic couples (total N reported as 426). Missed sacrifices were about as common as detected ones; people also perceived sacrifices the partner did not report. Perceiving a sacrifice, accurate or not, predicted gratitude; missed sacrifices did not. A companion paper, Visserman, Righetti, Impett, Keltner & Van Lange (2018), Emotion 18(5), 625-637 ('It's the motive that counts'), reported that sacrifices perceived as made for the partner's own good evoked gratitude, partly via perceived responsiveness (also search-level).

# Limits

Romantic couples; married share unknown. Daily diary, so associations. Do not print the 'about half' or '40-50 percent' figures (secondary coverage). The claim that missed sacrifices lower the sacrificer's satisfaction is search-level only. Strongest couple-level support for 'you cannot be grateful for what you do not see.'

# Park, Johnson, Gordon & Impett (2025) — "Pay Me Back"

A 13-year longitudinal study tracking exchange orientation — the tendency to
expect direct reciprocation from a partner for what you give them — across a
national sample of 7,293 German couples. Key findings, per secondary coverage
of the study:

- Most people naturally become *less* exchange-oriented over time as the
  relationship deepens — they relax what one co-author calls their "inner
  love accountant."
- People who stay transactional — continuing to expect a return on what they
  give — show steeper declines in relationship satisfaction over subsequent
  years.
- When a person becomes *more* score-focused than their own baseline in a
  given period, their satisfaction tends to drop in the years that follow.

This is a stronger external anchor for Ch7's central claim than the existing
Pillemer citation: it is a large-N, longitudinal, peer-reviewed empirical
study of the specific mechanism (exchange orientation / scorekeeping) rather
than an attributed summary of elder advice.

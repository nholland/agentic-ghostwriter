# Gottman — Bids for Connection and the Turning-Toward Rate

A "bid" is the smallest unit of contact in a marriage: a remark about
something out the window, a question, a hand on a shoulder, a look. Turning
toward is any response that meets it. Turning away is not hostility, it is
absence — the bid lands on nothing.

The reported figures: couples still married at six-year follow-up had turned
toward bids about 86% of the time; couples who divorced averaged about 33%.

# Why This Fits Ch11

Two jobs, and the second is the more important one.

**It supplies Claim 1's observable test.** The chapter needs a way for a
reader to check whether he has gone passive that is a behavior rather than a
feeling. Bid response is that behavior. A man who has disappeared into a hard
season is still answering questions and has stopped asking any; he responds
and never initiates. That is a turning-away rate he can notice tonight.

**It sets the scale for Claim 4.** The chapter's answer to "there's nothing
left of me to give" is not a fifteen-minute conversation or a date night. It
is that the unit of connection was always small, which is why it survives a
season that has taken everything else. That claim is much stronger if the
research says the small unit is the one that predicts the outcome.

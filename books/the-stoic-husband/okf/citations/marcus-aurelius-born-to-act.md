# Marcus Aurelius — "You Weren't Born to Stay Under the Covers" (Meditations 5.1)

> "At dawn, when you have trouble getting out of bed, tell yourself: I have
> to go to work — as a human being. What do I have to complain about, if
> I'm going to do what I was born for, the things I came into the world to
> do?" — *Meditations* 5.1 (translation and exact wording to be confirmed
> against a physical copy)

# Why This Fits Ch6

Marcus isn't scolding himself for ambition. He's reminding himself that
doing his proper work isn't an imposition; it's simply what a human being
does, the way a horse runs and a bee makes honey. No one thanks the bee.

This is the duty side of Ch6's argument: your job is your job, the way it
is for every other person who shows up and does the unglamorous, repeated
thing without expecting applause for it. It also supplies the chapter's
self-check moment: when a wife's critique stings, the question isn't "what
have I done for credit lately" — it's "was I actually avoiding my proper
work just now, or not?" If the honest answer is yes, this passage is the
voice that gets a man out from under the covers without resentment. If the
answer is no, it's the standard against which the critique can be measured
and, if unfair, set down without anger.

# Distinct From

/citations/marcus-aurelius-on-correction-and-tolerance.md — that citation
(Meditations 5.28, paired with Donald Robertson's modern reframe) is about
*receiving* criticism gracefully once it's been leveled. This citation is
about the prior question: whether you're doing your proper work in the
first place. Ch6's redraft uses this one as its single anchor quote rather
than stacking both — the bad-breath passage remains available for a future
chapter where receiving criticism is the dedicated focus.

# Seneca — Letters to Lucilius, Letter 81: The Wages of a Good Deed

Seneca, writing on the theme of benefits and gratitude, argues that virtue
is not practiced for a reward beyond itself: "The reward for all the
virtues lies in the virtues themselves... the wages of a good deed is to
have done it." A man who does something good, in this frame, has already
received the only payment that was ever actually owed to him: the act
meeting the need it was meant to meet.

# Why this instead of Ch7's Marcus Aurelius passage

Chapter 7 already closed on Marcus Aurelius's "don't reach for the third
thing" (Meditations, on not seeking credit or repayment for good deeds).
Chapter 8 needed the same idea, applied to a man's own felt unfairness
(he doesn't need his wife's active gratitude to validate work he's already
done), but reusing the identical passage and phrase back to back risked
reading as repetition rather than reinforcement. This Seneca letter
carries the same weight from a different philosopher and a different
specific articulation, keeping the two chapters' Stoic anchors distinct
while making the same underlying argument.

### What's landing

### Where readers are getting lost

Not a comprehension gap — the reader accurately restates the chapter's own
mechanism back (unfairness building from small uncounted things, needing to be
processed before it drives behavior). The gap is practical, not conceptual:

Chapter 8's "When it's yours" section — the beat that addresses *your own* felt
unfairness — resolves it with a single belief-level reframe (Seneca, Letter 81:
the deed already paid itself the moment it was done). That's a true and useful
reframe, but it's not an in-the-moment behavioral step. Compare this to the
adjacent beat, "When it lands on you" (her unfairness landing on you), which
*does* give an operational diagnostic: "run one question at the moment before
you speak... is what she's saying true, or not true?"

The reader explicitly asked for something "similar to the 3 second rule" —
i.e., he already knows the book's core tool (the Three-Second Window / Virtue
Question, established in Chapter 1) and is asking why it isn't showing up here,
in the one beat where the emotional stakes are highest for him personally.

This is a real structural finding, not just a reader preference:
`okf/frameworks/the-virtue-question.md` states the Three-Second Window is meant
to "return throughout as the book's reflective anchor for every scenario."
Chapter 8's "When it's yours" beat is currently the one scenario in this
chapter where that anchor is absent — the chapter gives a *belief* to hold but
not a *pause* to take.

## Revision Recommendations for Chapter 8

### Immediate (high-confidence fix)

**"When it's yours" section is reframe-only; needs an in-moment step before the
reframe.** Suggested fix: before the Seneca material, add one or two sentences
that put the Three-Second Window / Virtue Question to work at the moment the
felt unfairness spikes — the same "pause, then ask the question" shape used in
"When it lands on you," pointed inward instead of outward. Something in the
register of: *catch the feeling before it becomes a tone of voice — three
seconds, then the same question: am I being the best husband I can be right
now, even uncredited?* — then proceed into the existing Seneca payoff, which
still does the belief-level work the pause alone can't. This isn't a new tool;
it's applying the book's existing anchor where this chapter currently skips it.

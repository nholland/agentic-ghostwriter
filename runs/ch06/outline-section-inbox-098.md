## Chapter 6: Duty Without Resentment
**Premise:** The man who does what the marriage needs but keeps a running tab of what it's costing him has not yet understood what duty actually means.
**The reader's takeaway:** He can distinguish between genuine service and martyrdom wearing the mask of generosity — and can choose the former deliberately, understanding why the distinction matters.
**Key points:**
1. Musonius Rufus on shared life and marital obligation — introduced here with brief context; his writing on partnership is direct and immediately applicable, and he's the Stoic most focused on domestic life
2. Resentment is the signal that he was performing the duty for the wrong reason — not a sign that the duty was wrong to do; the resentment lives in the intention, not the act
3. Duty chosen freely doesn't accumulate into debt; duty performed for credit always does — the scoreboard runs only when he's performing for an audience, even a private one
**Central story/example:** The man who does the dishes every night, loves his wife, and has somehow spent three years angry about it — and the moment he finally understands that the dishes were never the problem
**Stoic lesson / principle:** Voluntary duty — obligation chosen, not endured (Musonius Rufus)
**Reader ah-ha:** "My resentment isn't about the dishes. It's about why I've been doing them."
**Research burden:** Medium — Musonius Rufus *Discourse* XIV (On Marriage) and *Discourse* XIII (on equal virtue in men and women)
**Word count target:** 1,200–1,500 words
**Transition to Chapter 7:** Resentment comes from scorekeeping. Chapter 7 examines the ledger directly.

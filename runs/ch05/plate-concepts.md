# Chapter 5 plate concepts — 2026-09-20 21:50

Designer desk, concepts mode, stage 1 of `/gw-plate`. Three concepts, three
different carriers. Thumbnails: `runs/ch05/concepts/A.svg`, `B.svg`, `C.svg`
(rough, full house canvas 640x430, Georgia, title y=40, italic subtitle y=66,
`#1a1a1a` on white, hairline rules at .28).

**None of the three uses the fence post, the nail or the hole as its carrier.**
That vocabulary is Chapter 4's and reusing it is the defect the Reader Panel
failed the last plate for. Each concept below draws a different image taken
from Chapter 5's own prose.

**Source lines both the concepts and the thumbnails are held to:**

- Mechanism (title, word for word): **The Remaining Nails**
- Conversation sentence: *The fight ends before the real thing gets said.
  That's not resolution. That's how a man becomes small without deciding to.*
- Lesson: *parrhesia*, plain speech, with her response a preferred indifferent.
- Challenge: calling a fight over once it goes quiet is deferred conflict,
  with interest.

## The title problem, before the concepts

The Mechanism line the plate must carry as its title is **"The Remaining
Nails."** That is Chapter 4's picture in words. So every concept here is a
drawing with no nail in it under a title that says nail, and a reader who
glances at the title alone still lands on the anger chapter.

This is a finding about the distillation, not about the drawing, and it is the
author's to rule on. Two ways out, both one line of work:

1. Retitle the mechanism from the chapter's own words. The chapter's beat
   heading **"The courage to come back"** is already in the prose and already
   names this mechanism. **"Becoming small"** is the other, also a beat heading.
2. Keep "The Remaining Nails" and accept that the title is a back-reference to
   Chapter 4 while the drawing is Chapter 5's.

The thumbnails all carry the required title so the Panel can see the conflict
at full size rather than read about it here.

## Concept A — The altitude test

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** most of what you fight about is
nothing once you back up far enough, and the one thing that does not shrink is
the one you have to go back and say out loud.

**Carrier:** apparent size against distance. Two things the same size in the
room; seen from above, one has collapsed to almost nothing and the other has
not moved at all. The drawing is the *failure of one of them to shrink*.
Nothing else on the plate carries the argument. **Not the fence post or the
nail:** the drawn objects are two plain blocks and the relationship is scale.

**From the chapter:** the view-from-above beat. "The same movement in a fight:
zoom out until the thing is at its actual scale. You are fighting about what
time to get up in the morning, not world peace." And: "But some things won't
shrink from altitude. When you zoom out and the thing is still big, that's the
signal."

**Copy it needs (10 pieces):**

1. Title: THE REMAINING NAILS
2. Subtitle: *Zoom out until the thing is at its actual scale.*
3. Column label: IN THE ROOM
4. Column label: FROM ABOVE
5. *what time to get up in the morning*
6. *almost nothing from up here*
7. *the thing that never got said*
8. *it did not shrink, so say it*
9. Closing: *Most fights are small. The one that stays big from up there is the
   one you have to go back to.*
10. Closing gloss: *Parrhesia, frank speech: say it plainly, then let it land
    however it lands.*

**Risk:** a skeptic reads it as a permission slip. "Some fights are worth
having, so pick your battles" is a much easier idea than the chapter's, and it
is what the two-row grid will say if the second row's caption does not insist
that the surviving thing has to be *said*, not *won*. Second, smaller risk: the
right column can read as perspective trickery rather than as judgment.

## Concept B — The same man, two endings

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** when a fight just goes quiet you walk
out smaller than you walked in, and the only thing that gets you out at full
size is going back and saying the true thing.

**Carrier:** one man's height drawn twice, entering and leaving, under a dashed
line held at his starting height. Top row: the exit bar is short of the dashed
line. Bottom row: it touches. The difference between the two rows is the whole
argument, and it is measurable at a glance without reading a word. This is the
`runs/ch10/plate.svg` form: the same object under the same pressure, twice.
**Not the fence post or the nail:** the drawn object is the man's own size.

**From the chapter:** "Both of you are slightly smaller than when you started."
"The question the chapter title asks: are you leaving the fight the same size
you were when it started?" And the fourth outcome: "someone has the courage to
come back."

**Copy it needs (11 pieces):**

1. Title: THE REMAINING NAILS
2. Subtitle: *Are you leaving the fight the same size you were when it started?*
3. Row label: IT WENT QUIET
4. Gloss: *nobody came back to it*
5. *the fight stops*
6. *walked in his size, walked out smaller*
7. Row label: YOU GO BACK
8. Gloss: *you say the true thing, calm*
9. *the same fight*
10. *walked out the size he walked in*
11. Closing: *Parrhesia, frank speech: you govern the saying, never how it
    lands.*

**Risk:** the skeptic reads the shortened bar as the cost of the fight itself,
so the plate becomes "fighting makes you smaller, so avoid fighting" instead of
"ending it without saying it makes you smaller." The middle captions are what
stop that, and captions carrying the load is exactly what the standard warns
about. Lesser risk: a bar shrinking reads to some men as losing the argument,
which is the chapter's other failure mode and not this one.

## Concept C — The room cools, the thing runs on

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** the room goes quiet and you call the
fight finished, but the thing you never said is still running underneath, and
only going back and saying it stops that line.

**Carrier:** two lines on one clock. The temperature in the room rises, falls
and flattens into calm; below it, the unsaid thing starts at the same moment
and keeps running straight past the point where the fight "ended." One line
ends, the other does not, on the same timeline. A single upright stroke late on
the lower line is where going back cuts it. **Not the fence post or the nail:**
the drawn objects are a temperature curve and a line that outlives it.

**From the chapter:** the opening beat. "The temperature in the room shifts
before the first word gets said. You feel it the way you feel weather." Plus
the outcome that names the gap: "you fight hard, both take damage, and then time
passes and you call it resolved without the real conversation."

**Copy it needs (8 pieces, 9 lines):**

1. Title: THE REMAINING NAILS
2. Subtitle: *The fight ends before the real thing gets said.*
3. *the temperature in the room*
4. *calm*
5. *quiet again, so you call it over*
6. *the thing that needed to be said, still running, with interest*
7. *going back and saying it / is the only thing that ends this one* (two lines)
8. Closing: *Parrhesia, frank speech: say the true thing once you are calm, and
   let her answer be hers.*

**Risk:** it reads as a chart, and a chart makes a man look for data that is
not there and cannot be put there. A second misreading is fatalism: the lower
line running to the edge of the plate can say "it never goes away" rather than
"one thing ends it." In the rough the lower line still runs on past the cut
stroke, which is that misreading already visible; in a finished draft the line
would stop at the cut.

## Notes for the author

- *Parrhesia* appears on all three, always inside a running sentence with its
  plain-English gloss attached, never at display size. The last plate was
  flagged for setting the Greek word large; none of these do.
- No numbers, no statistics, no quotations on any of the three. Marcus and
  Seneca are in the chapter but neither is quoted on a plate.
- The Chapter 4 plate set the nail, the head, the hole and the board as shared
  primitives. Concepts A, B and C deliberately share none of them, so Chapters 4
  and 5 will no longer look like one drawing made twice.

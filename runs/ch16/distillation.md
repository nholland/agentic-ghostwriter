# Chapter 16 Distillation — The Marriage in Flow

**Mechanism:** Calm and strength make the marriage sound; warmth makes it a home

**Conversation sentence:** Your marriage is a home the two of you own, and you keep it up with small, warm things on ordinary days; nobody owes anybody, and when you're calm, strong and warm at once, it's at peace, at its best, and flourishing.

Warmth is what the calm and the strength were for. Calm alone can go cold. "I can only control me" is true, but it belongs in your head as a mindset, not said out loud to your wife. Warmth starts with compassion. You don't take on her emotions. You go to her, and you want her at her best. Think of your marriage as a house the two of you own. Just living in it wears on it a little every day, so you keep it up and now and then make it better. The upkeep is small: good morning when you're away, the coffee, rubbing her back, a gift because you thought of her. She keeps it up too, and you still go first. Nobody owes anybody. There's no debt, only upside or zero. Calm and strength make the house sound, and warmth makes it a home. When you're calm, strong and warm at once, the marriage is at peace, at its best, and flourishing. I describe those with three words. The first is equanimity. The other two are Stoic Greek: arête, which means excellence, and eudaimonia, a life going well as a whole. That's what flow looks like for a marriage.

**Lesson:** Warmth is what the calm and the strength were for, and the small things you do for her on ordinary days are what keep your marriage a home.
**Challenge:** Tomorrow, before she asks for anything, do one small thing for her because she was worth a minute of your day, and that night ask yourself whether the life you share is better now than it was a year ago.

**Practice:**
1. **Proactive.** Every day, without being asked, do one small thing for your wife because you thought of her. Make the coffee, rub her back, or say good morning when you're away.
2. **Reactive.** When she's upset with you and you feel the urge to tell her what you can and can't control, keep that thought to yourself. Sit down next to her and ask what's going on.
3. **Reactive.** When she starts talking, put your phone down. When you notice she's tired before she says so, take something off her plate.

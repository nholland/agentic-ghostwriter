# Chapter 16 Distillation — Warmth Is Strength

**Mechanism:** Calm, strength and warmth together put the marriage in flow

**Conversation sentence:** The calm and the strength were for something: when you add warmth, on ordinary days and without being asked, the two of you become one marriage at peace, at its best, and flourishing.

Warmth is what the calm and the strength were for. Calm alone can go cold. "I can only control me" is true, but it belongs in your head as a mindset, not said out loud to your wife. Warmth starts with compassion. You don't take on her emotions. You go to her, and you want her at her best. Most of it is small: the phone down when she talks, something taken off her plate when she's tired. Think of your marriage as one account the two of you share. You both put in, nobody keeps count of whose was whose, and nothing you put in is owed back to you. You keep putting in on easy days because hard stretches come, and then the marriage lives on what is already there between you. When you're calm, strong and warm at once, the calm, the strength and the warmth stop being separate jobs. Then the marriage is at peace (equanimity), at its best (arête, excellence), and flourishing (eudaimonia, a life going well as a whole). That's what flow looks like for a marriage.

**Lesson:** Warmth is what the calm and the strength were for, and it shows up in small things done for her on ordinary days.
**Challenge:** Tomorrow, before she asks for anything, do one small thing for her because she was worth the minute, and that night ask yourself whether there's more between the two of you now than there was a year ago.

**Practice:**
1. **Proactive.** Every day, without being asked, do one small thing for your wife because you thought of her. Make the coffee, rub her back, or say good morning when you're away. Don't keep count of them.
2. **Reactive.** When she's upset with you and you feel the urge to tell her what you can and can't control, keep that thought to yourself. Sit down next to her and ask what's going on.
3. **Reactive.** When she starts talking, put your phone down. When you notice she's tired before she says so, take something off her plate.

# Ch16 plate: standalone read

Reader Panel (gw-panel), stage 5. I looked at the plate first and on its own, then read the distillation.
Plate: `runs/ch16/pdf/plate.png`. Distillation: `runs/ch16/distillation.md`.

## What it says to him (plate alone)

Calm and strength by themselves lean apart and can go cold, but with warmth on top, shown on ordinary days without being asked, they stand together as one marriage at peace, at its best and flourishing.

## Against the distillation

Conversation sentence: "The calm and the strength were for something: when you add warmth, on ordinary days and without being asked, the two of you become one marriage at peace, at its best, and flourishing."

**PASS.** My sentence carries every part of it: calm and strength were for something (the subtitle, and the leaning pillars on the left that hold nothing up), warmth added (the black capstone), on ordinary days without being asked (the note above it), one marriage (the label under it), and at peace, at its best, flourishing (the bottom line).

## Skeptic: the misreading it invites

A defensive man can read the capstone as a lid, the last and optional piece that goes on once the real work of calm and strength is done: "I've got the pillars, so warmth is a finishing touch." The subtitle ("Warmth is what the calm and the strength were for") argues against this. The left side does too, because without warmth the pillars stand apart and hold nothing up. So the drawing guards against it already, and I don't think it needs a fix. This is a note, not an edit. If the Designer wants to close the gap further, the only change I would make: in the left panel, move the dashed box's label from "calm can go cold" to "calm alone can go cold". The word "alone" is already in the distillation, and it puts the weight on missing warmth rather than on calm being wrong.

Second, minor: the dashed box sits over both pillars, but its words name only calm. A reader might wonder whether strength goes cold too. That does no harm to the takeaway.

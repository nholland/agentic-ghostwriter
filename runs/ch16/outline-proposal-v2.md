## Proposed entry

**Premise:** Part III ends where the whole book has been heading. A husband who can stay calm (the River) and carry weight (the Oak) adds warmth (the Sun): attention, effort and joy he invests every day. When all three are working, the marriage stops being two people managing each other and becomes one thing, in flow.

**The reader's takeaway:** He sees the marriage as a joint account he keeps investing in, and knows what it looks like when it is working: at peace, at its best, flourishing.

**Key points:**
1. **The capstone (opening, short).** Calm got him through the storms; strength let him carry the load. Warmth is what those were for. (River and Oak named once each, as the book's own images.)
2. **The old picture, briefly.** Protector, provider, quiet until he demands or blows up. Calm was the first fix, and it could go cold: "I can't control you," her answer, and his lesson that "I can only control me" is a mindset, never a line said to her. One short section, ending on the turn toward warmth.
3. **Warmth is compassion that moves toward her.** You don't take on her feelings; you want her in her highest state, and you act on it. Simple preferred indifference: "I'd like to conduct myself in a way that makes our marriage stronger, my wife happier, and helps us get towards eudaimonia."
4. **The joint account.** A relationship is an investment, not a burden. Every kindness is a deposit into one account you share: good morning when traveling, the coffee, her back, the gift. Hers go in too. Nobody counts whose was whose; the bank never owes you money; you just keep it growing, because hard stretches draw on it. Covey is credited for the emotional bank account. Your grandfather's advice is a one-line callback to Ch12.
5. **Flow.** The modern word for when everything is going well: you're at peace and at your best. The Stoics had three words for it, and a marriage can have all three: equanimity (at peace), arête (at its best), eudaimonia (flourishing). "The health of the whole is more important than the health of the individual."
6. **Close, positive.** What it looks like on an ordinary day: the walk around the block, the movies, "She's my best friend." She feels loved, desired, a priority. The last line is yours, to confirm: *"You should have the most credits with your wife because she is your number one."* With the joint account, "credits" may read as your side. Option: *"…the most invested in your wife, because she is your number one."*

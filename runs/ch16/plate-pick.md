## Cold reads (written before reading the distillation)

**A. Two posts, then the stone.** What he takes away: calm and strength by themselves are two posts with nothing on top and "can go cold"; put warmth across them and you have one marriage that is at peace, at its best and flourishing.
- What he might misread: two posts look like two people, him and her, and not two qualities in him. One shared label, THE CALM AND THE STRENGTH, under both posts makes that worse. A defensive man also reads it as "I already hold the thing up, and warmth is the lid on top," so warmth looks like trim instead of the point of the whole thing.

**B. Steps up, a fall, a floor.** What he takes away: small things added every day build something up, so a bad month knocks you down but not to zero.
- What he might misread: the arrows point down onto each step, so they look like blows or pressure, not deposits. The fall reads as the marriage going downhill. Nothing in the drawing connects calm, strength or warmth to the line; they appear only in the title. A defensive man reads "A BAD MONTH" as her bad month, or reads the whole plate as "save up credit so you can cash it in later."

**C. Three boxes, one marriage.** What he takes away: calm leads to peace, strength leads to the marriage at its best, warmth leads to flourishing, and warmth is the heaviest.
- What he might misread: three separate arrows, each going to its own result, say the three qualities work separately. That is the opposite of "together" and "at once." It also looks like a slide, so he may skim past it as a checklist.

## Against the Conversation sentence

> The calm and the strength were for something: when you add warmth, on ordinary days and without being asked, the two of you become one marriage at peace, at its best, and flourishing.

- **A** says the main point outright: calm and strength were incomplete, warmth completes them, and the result is one marriage at peace, at its best, flourishing. It shows "without being asked." It does not show "on ordinary days."
- **B** says the Lesson and the Challenge ("small things on ordinary days", "more between you than a year ago"), not the Conversation sentence. "The calm and the strength were for something" is nowhere in the drawing.
- **C** shows the three results clearly, but its three separate arrows undercut "together." It never says the calm and the strength were *for* anything.

## Pick: A, the capstone

**One line why:** A is the only concept where my cold read already was the Conversation sentence (calm and strength were incomplete until warmth made them one marriage). B draws a different sentence, and C draws a list.

**Weighing the Designer's notes:**
- **Capstone is a second image.** The distillation and the chapter's `metaphor_family` both use the joint account, and A doesn't draw it. That gap is real, and it is for the author to rule on, not for me. But the standard he set is that the plate stands alone, and the joint-account concept (B) is the one that fails that standard. It shows deposits without showing the Mechanism. On the plate, the stronger image should win over the declared one. If he rules that the joint account must be the image, B needs the three named inside the drawing, and per the Designer that needs new words from him.
- **B is the only joint-account concept.** I agree it is the weakest on the Mechanism. My cold read also found a misread the Designer did not list: the downward arrows read as blows, not deposits.
- **Fallback if A is ruled out:** C. A first edit could join C's three arrows into one before they enter the enclosure, so it reads as "together" and not as three separate pipes.

## Edits for A's draft (each specific enough to draw)

1. Label the posts separately, CALM under the left post and STRENGTH under the right, in both blocks. Cut the two "THE CALM AND THE STRENGTH" labels. This stops the posts reading as him and her, and it ends the label repeating. All three words are in the Mechanism, so the grounded check should pass.
2. Make the right-hand stone visibly *join* the posts. The Designer's notes say the posts "stand apart." In the drawing they look identical to the right-hand posts, so pull the left posts slightly apart or tilt them. The difference between the two blocks then shows warmth making them one thing, and doesn't depend only on the dashed slot.
3. Move "calm can go cold" so it sits next to the empty dashed slot, where the warmth is missing. Right now it reads as a caption on the posts.
4. Optional, if the italic cap allows: put "on ordinary days" in the gloss above the stone with "without being asked". That is the one part of the Conversation sentence A leaves out. It needs a three-word match in the prose to pass the grounded check. That is unchecked.

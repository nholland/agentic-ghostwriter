### B0. Opening and orientation (~120 words)

### B1. The old blueprint (~300 words), key point 1

Source: `/stories/the-old-blueprint-of-a-man.md`. Author's lines (verbatim; pick, do not stack all):
- "Men were fearless. They were protectors. They were providers and they provided for their families."
- "My sense is that most of the men I grew up around would have thought it way too weak to talk about your feelings."
- "A man bore things quietly or if he finally was pushed to the edge it was okay for him to lose himself in anger and blow up."
- "you basically made demands, you had benign neutral conversation, or you kept quiet."
- "Their word was the law" (grandfather and father).
- Optional: "It was a way to show that they were powerful."

**Callbacks, a clause each, never retold:**
- **Ch1's lineage:** the grandfather devoted fifty-plus years and quick to yell; the father who escalated.
- **Ch4's nails. Purpose of the clause:** the blueprint called the blow-up "okay"; Ch4 already showed
  the cost the blueprint never counted, that each outburst leaves a mark. So the clause attaches to
  "okay for him to lose himself in anger and blow up" and says, in effect, that the blow-up the old
  rule permitted is the nail Chapter 4 talked about. Ch4's printed line is "An apology pulls the nail.
  It doesn't fill the hole." (`chapters/ch04/refined.md` line 79). Do not re-quote it, do not retell
  the fence, and do not claim the men of the blueprint never apologized (he did not say so).

New here is what they did with **feelings**: demand, neutral talk, silence, blow-up. Add no detail of
yelling, breaking or throwing beyond what he gave ("curse words, yelling, screaming, and occasionally
throwing things around the house"). No nature/lioness claim (gap, section F).

### B3. What Stoicism offered, and how he misused it (~500 words), key point 3, the central story

Sources: `/frameworks/self-command-not-detachment.md` (P2, accepted), `/stories/i-cant-control-you-said-aloud.md`.

**(a) What it gave him.** His realization first, then the old text:
- "it put a label on the fact that being angry was a state of temporary madness. You were effectively out of control and a man who is powerful and strong is in control. A man who is effectively slavish to his emotions is weak"
- Then Seneca, house edition, one sentence: "Certain wise men, therefore, have claimed that anger is temporary madness." (*De Ira* I.1, trans. Basore). Basore's English matches his phrase; that is the beat. **Seneca reports the line from "certain wise men"; do not write "Seneca called anger..."** Ground Seneca in a clause (a Roman statesman and adviser writing a book on anger). Optional second fragment, separately: "unmindful of ties". Quote only the short sentence (the longer span carries an unconfirmed OCR dash). See `/citations/seneca-de-ira-1-1-temporary-madness.md`.
- Also his: "I don't have to give in to the emotions of a situation. When someone is upset I am able to remain calm and balanced." (paraphrase fine; **if quoted, "balanced" is why "balance" is kept out of the family declaration, section C**).

**(b) The overcorrection, said aloud.** Told from inside, in this order:

3. **Her answer, THE TURN:** "If I have a man who doesn't care how I feel, if I have a man who believes that he's in a relationship by himself, then he will find himself in a relationship by himself."
   Her answer is the chapter's climax line (01-voice gives the climax to "a line from a real exchange
   in a marriage"). Give it its own paragraph. Nothing in the same paragraph competes with it.
4. **His gloss, one or two short sentences:** "She was very quick to communicate." / "She did not want to be married to a stone." **"Stone" is his summary, not her words: never inside her quotation marks.**
5. **The inside of the moment (Round 3; this is the bridge the round-1 brief lacked).** He did not

### B4. Compassion, and why he wants her well (~330 words), key point 4

- "you can feel compassion for somebody without taking on their emotions."
- "compassion is a much stronger word because you don't want the other person to feel upset, anxious, or sad. You want them to be in their highest state."
- "People want connection and my wife craved connection. I crave connection with her"
- "of course, I don't want my wife unhappy." (Round 2 wording.)
- Why (his reason): "one of the Stoic principles is that we are all here for each other as part of the cosmopolis. We want to find happiness and joy and success working with each other. We are here through the virtue of justice"
  - **Gloss for cosmopolis** (plain English, first use): the Stoic idea that every person is a citizen of
    one world-city, bound to the others by shared reason; justice is what each member owes the rest.
    Optional Marcus, Long: "my city and country, so far as I am Antoninus, is Rome, but so far as I am a
    man, it is the world." (*Meditations* VI.44). "Cosmopolis" is not Marcus's word; do not put it in
    his mouth. `/citations/marcus-aurelius-meditations-4-4-and-6-44-cosmopolis.md`. Justice was named
    "Kind" in Ch1's four virtues: a clause of callback fits.
- Preferred indifferents as daily life (callback; Ch2 and Ch5 defined the term). Source:
  `/frameworks/attachment-vs-devotion.md` "Where a Stoic lives all day" (P4). "Dispreferred and
  preferred are states where you exist almost all day long. It's the absolutes of detachment or
  attachment that you effectively get to only a few times." And: "I do prefer to make her feel valued. I do prefer to make her feel loved".
- "Stoicism gets a bad rap because a lot of times people can seem like they are unemotional or detached and I think that is a sign of immaturity in the study of the philosophy."
- **Not Ch2 again.** Ch2 owns the definition ("You don't have to feel what the other person is feeling.
  You have to see it and recognize it"), the soup, emotional contagion, and "Governance as armor.
  That's not this." Ch15 printed one compassion line. Here compassion is the cure for the other failure:
  not absorbing her (Ch2) but going unreachable.
- **Do not claim the Stoics rejected empathy.** Inbox #146: the Stoics reject pity, not empathy.
  "Empathy is the wrong emotion" is his view ("I think"); present it as his. Optional modern clause:
  Singer & Klimecki 2014 distinguish empathy (sharing a feeling) from compassion (warm concern);
  search-only, name it, no numbers, no wording.

### B6. Close of the Sun arc: from me to us (~250 words), key point 6

- "Being happy as an individual is extremely different than being happy in a relationship."
- "the health of the whole is more important than the health of the individual"

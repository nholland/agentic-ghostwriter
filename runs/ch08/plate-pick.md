## Step 1 — the three cold reads

**A.** The same small things land on two lives: the man who never says them out
loud ends up with a pile and a scale that has already tipped, the man who says
each one as it happens stays level.
*Skeptic's read:* "So I'm supposed to make a comment every time something bugs
me." Second misread: the level scale says the work itself disappears if you talk
about it.

**B.** The one thing he is arguing about is the only part showing; weeks of
things nobody counted are stacked under the line holding it up.
*Skeptic's read:* the hidden stack is *her* tally of his failures — a scoreboard
kept on him. The rising columns read as a chart of grievances, not his own
uncounted weeks.

**C.** When the feeling surfaces it came from one of two directions, and each
direction has its own move; neither is waiting on her.
*Skeptic's read:* another flowchart proving he is wrong either way, and "IT IS
YOURS" reads as fault assigned rather than credit already paid.

## Step 2 — the pick

**A.** Its cold sentence is the Conversation sentence in the stranger's words:
uncounted small things tipping quietly until the weight is felt — and its
subtitle, *The tip feels sudden. It never is.*, is "unfairness rarely arrives as
one moment" said plainly, carried by a drawn pile rather than a caption.

Agrees with the Designer, reached cold and independently. A is also the only
concept that draws a scale, so the title-rule ruling he flagged does not bind.

**Runners-up.** B owns the exact words "never counted" but needs a carrier that
is not a bar chart and a fix for the empty top third, or it reads as her
scorekeeping. C needs the accumulation drawn, not compressed into one black
rectangle — it plates the Lesson while the Mechanism goes missing.

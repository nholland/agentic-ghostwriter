## Concept A — the same weeks, twice

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** the same small things land on both
scales, and the one where they get said out loud never tips.

**Carrier:** one object drawn twice under the same pressure, the Ch10 model.
Six identical weights fall toward two identical scales. On the left they reach
the pan and pile up and the beam is over. On the right each one meets a short
stroke partway down, stops there, and the beam is level. The difference between
the two drawings is the whole argument, and the corrective is a drawn thing (the
stroke that stops the fall), not a caption.

**From the chapter:** "Individually, each one is nothing. Together, over weeks,
they add weight to an invisible scale. One day something small tips it." Plus
practice 1: "When something stings, name it to yourself right then. An
irritation you've named loses its power to quietly compound."

**Copy it needs (8 items):** title *THE TIPPING SCALE*; subtitle *The tip feels
sudden. It never is.*; shared line *The same weeks land on both of these.*; two
caps labels *UNCOUNTED* / *NAMED AS IT LANDS*; one gloss under each scale
(*Nothing said out loud, so all of it stays on and one day it tips.* /
*Each one said out loud, so none of it stacks and the scale holds level.*);
closing *Nothing tips that you name on the day it lands.* The shared line and
one of the two glosses could go if the panel wants it tighter.

**Risk:** a skeptic reads the level right-hand scale as the claim that naming a
thing makes the work itself go away, which is not what the chapter says. The
chapter says a named irritation stops compounding, not that the load lightens.
The stopping stroke is meant to carry that, and it may not carry it alone.

## Concept B — under the surface

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** the one thing he is arguing about is
the only part above the line, and weeks of small uncounted things are stacked up
underneath it.

**Carrier:** a single horizontal line, with almost nothing above it and a slowly
rising stack of small marks below it, week by week, until the last column
crosses. Nothing above the line until the stack reaches it. The picture argues
that what he can see is the top of something long, not an event.

**From the chapter:** "The small stuff underneath has been landing for a long
time before it ever reaches the surface," and the distillation's Challenge,
"before the day it finally breaks the surface."

**Copy it needs (7 items):** title; subtitle *The day it surfaces is not the day
it started.*; two caps labels *WHAT YOU NOTICE* / *WHAT YOU NEVER COUNTED*; one
gloss on the surfaced mark, *the one you argue about*; one gloss on the two
hollow marks that dropped out of the stack, *said out loud, so it never
stacked*; closing *Name each one as it lands, and nothing has to surface.*

**Risk:** it reads as an iceberg, which is a stock image and not the chapter's,
and a skeptic reads the hidden mass as *her* hidden load rather than his own
uncounted weeks. The rising stack also reads as a bar chart, which puts a
quantity register on a plate set that does not otherwise measure anything. One
layout fault to fix if it is picked: the top third of the canvas is empty, the
same defect the author already named on the current plate.

## Concept C — which way it is pointing

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** when the feeling finally shows up,
which direction it came from decides what he does, and neither answer is waiting
for anything.

**Carrier:** one mark, the day it surfaces, splitting into two branches that are
drawn differently on purpose. Left, her complaint lands on him and the branch
forks into exactly two ends: a solid block (true, so it gets built) and a single
short stroke (not true, so it gets said once). Right, the work is his, and the
line carries its payment mark at its own start, with a dashed empty box further
along that the line never reaches.

**From the chapter:** "Is what she's saying true, or not true?" and "It's a
construction project: real change... over time she can watch you fix it," and
"The payment already cleared, at the moment of the doing."

**Copy it needs (10 items):** title; subtitle *Which way the weight points
decides what you do.*; *the day it finally surfaces*; two caps labels *IT LANDS
ON YOU* / *IT IS YOURS*; *true, so you change it*; *not true, so you say it
once, without heat*; *already paid, the moment you did it*; *her noticing, which
is a separate thing*; closing *Two directions, two tools, and no third thing to
wait for.*

**Risk:** it is the copy-heaviest of the three and the closest to a flowchart,
which is exactly the cross-set fault the Panel named. It also draws the
chapter's Lesson at the cost of the Mechanism: the accumulation, the thing the
Mechanism line is named for, is one small black rectangle at the top.

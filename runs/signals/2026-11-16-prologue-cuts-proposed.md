## Proposed cuts

Line numbers are from the current `refined.md`.

### P1. Line 31: the stacked analogies and the repeated "born" sentence

- **Current:** "Stoicism works like lifting, or running a company, or learning an instrument. You build it through practice, not because you were born with the right temperament for it. You don't have to be born calm to build it."
- **Proposed:** "Stoicism works like lifting. You don't have to be born calm to build it."
- **Reason:** One analogy instead of three, and two sentences said "you weren't born with it" back to back.

### P2. Line 39: the last sentence restates line 37

- **Current:** "A reachable man is the strong one. A man who's found a quiet way to disappear from his marriage looks calm from the outside and is doing damage on the inside."
- **Proposed:** "A reachable man is the strong one."
- **Reason:** Line 37 already said it: "Nothing I was doing looked bad from the outside. From the inside of the marriage..." The cut lets the thesis line close the paragraph.

### P3. Line 41: windup removed, weakness point moved up

- **Current:** "If picking up a book like this feels like admitting you don't have it all figured out, I want to address that directly. I've needed help with things that mattered: a coach, a mentor, a hard book, a hard conversation with somebody further down the road. That's not weakness. That's the shape of building anything real."
- **Proposed:** "If picking up a book like this feels like admitting you don't have it all figured out, that's not weakness. I've needed help with things that mattered: a coach, a mentor, a hard book, a hard conversation with somebody further down the road. That's the shape of building anything real."
- **Reason:** "I want to address that directly" is a windup. Reordering the author's own words also drops the "That's not X. That's Y." shape.

### P4. Line 43: split one 33-word sentence

- **Current:** "If only one of you is, it's still a problem, just a smaller one, and in my experience, one person doing this work seriously tends to change what becomes possible for the other."
- **Proposed:** "If only one of you is, it's still a problem, just a smaller one. In my experience, one person doing this work seriously tends to change what becomes possible for the other."
- **Reason:** It was the longest sentence in the Prologue. The comma becomes a period and no words change.

### P5. Line 43: weakness repeated, in the wrong paragraph

- **Current:** "A lot of men skip this kind of work entirely. Somewhere along the way they got the idea that a real man just handles his marriage without help, and that needing to work on it is a sign of weakness. I've never met a man who believed that and was actually good at this."
- **Proposed:** [cut]
- **Reason:** Line 41 already makes the "needing help isn't weakness" point. Here it interrupts a paragraph about two people sharing the work.

### P6. Line 45: the fourth way of saying "go first"

- **Current:** "Waiting to see what she does before you commit to your own side just delays things for both of you."
- **Proposed:** [cut]
- **Reason:** The paragraph says "go first" in five ways. This is the one the paragraph can lose without breaking. "That's what leading means" still points back to "invest... on your own initiative," and "Doing yours fully" still points back to "your own half."

### P7. Line 49: the closing run of analogies

- **Current:** "You don't get to skip the hard part in anything that actually matters. Skip the gym today, and it's easy, right up until your body can't do what you need it to when it counts. Spend everything you make, and today feels easier, right up until the year you actually needed something saved and there's nothing there. The jelly donut runs on the same math: fast and easy now, slow and hard later, on all the days the weight won't come off. Marriage runs on the same math. You don't get to skip the hard part. You only get to choose which hard part you build something with. You choose your hard."
- **Proposed:** "You don't get to skip the hard part in anything that actually matters. You only get to choose which hard part you build something with. You choose your hard."
- **Reason:** This is the filler Marcus named: three analogies, "the same math" twice, and the opening sentence said again. Line 47 has already made the case with real marriages, so the run adds nothing.
- **Fallback, if the author wants one image kept:** keep only the gym sentence, after the first sentence. It matches the lifting image in P1.

### P8. Line 53: the boat

- **Current:** "I was in the same boat you're probably in. I've been building that boat since we started dating in 1995,"
- **Proposed:** "I've been building this since we started dating in 1995,"
- **Reason:** It repeats line 9's "same boat" and turns a figure of speech into something being built. "Building" is already this passage's word. The rest of the paragraph stays as written.
- **Fallback, for the author to weigh:** cut the whole paragraph (51 goes straight to the close). This also removes "If this book gives you a head start... that's the whole point," which is a promise to the reader. That falls under #177, so it is not proposed here.

## B. The chapter spec, in working order (from `03-outline.md`, approved) (revised 2026-10-24 round 2)

- **Premise:** Kindness is the warmth a husband gives into ordinary days, and the small cutting
  remark is its first test. The floor: never be the worst part of her day, so he does not return a
  zing. The ceiling: be the best part of her day. Kindness is not the same as being there. It is
  patience, understanding, and taking something off her plate.
- **Takeaway:** He can hear a cutting remark as a sign of how someone feels inside, answer it with
  kindness, and ask each day whether he was the best part of hers.
- **Key point 1, the zing (the floor).** Confess the pattern first: every small critique met with
  an equal or bigger reply. Then two different questions. If she says "lazy," he asks "Is that
  truly the case?" If yes, fix it; if no, let it go. Far more often the remark is an outward sign of
  how a person feels inside: a wife facing a packed weekend, a child melting down because they are
  hungry or tired. Not a verdict on him, or on the child's love, or the parenting. He does not have
  to be offended. The whole household, not only his wife.
- **Key point 2, the long game.** Checkers is answering every exchange; chess is the long game.
  Ignore the zing, pour in kindness, and the tone of the whole house changes. He would rather have a
  good night than win each exchange. Not surrender; his own advice, learned through experience.
  "Would you rather be right or be married?" is **one line, and its first printing in the book**
  (round 2): inbox 141, which would add it to Ch5, is still parked, and no landed chapter prints it.
  Introduce it as his own question with "I tell people." It is not a callback (D31; deviation (l)).
- **Key point 3, kindness is not presence.** Patience, understanding, taking a load off her plate:
  a foot rub, a chore taken (left unnamed, deviation (m)), letting her talk about her day. Kind not because they are acts of
  service but because they are usually hers and he is taking them off her. **Ch12 owns coffee, wine
  and breakfast;** this chapter says what makes an act kind, not which act.
- **Key point 4, the best part of her day (the ceiling).** The audit question: "Was I the best part
  of her day?" One line on compassion (see her load, act, stay steady yourself: deviation (a)); the
  full distinction is Ch16's. "Floor" and "ceiling" are structure, not printed words (deviation (h)).
- **Central story:** his wife's full agenda meeting his rest, told as a recurring pattern, not a
  dated scene. Her lines in his words. Shown as stress, not attack. His two responses. No detail
  beyond what he gave. Must not reuse Ch14's small bothers, Ch6's TV-and-feet-up zing, or Ch2's
  homecoming cascade.
- **Stoic lesson:** Marcus I.9 (Long), Sextus "entirely free from passion, and also most
  affectionate" (anchor; *philostorgia* named once, deviation (e)); Seneca *De Beneficiis* II.1-2
  (Stewart 1887) (supporting; II.1 alone, deviation (d)); Seneca *De Ira* II.34 (Basore 1928)
  (optional; section 3 only, round 2); Epictetus *Enchiridion* 20 (Long) (fallback for key point 1). All
  unverified. "Discipline of kindness" is not named in prose.
- **Reader ah-ha (proposed, the author to confirm):** "I was asking whether I'd been treated well.
  The better question was whether I was the best part of her day."

  at most a clause; no numbers.

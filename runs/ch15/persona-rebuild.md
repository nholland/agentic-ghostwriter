# Ch15 Reader Panel, scoped review of the rebuild (one round)

Reader Panel (gw-panel), cold. Date 2026-10-25 from the environment. I had no shell, so I could not read the clock (Rule 1) and the time is unrecorded.
Scope: `runs/ch15/refined.md` prose above "## Editor's Notes" (rebuilt on "meet hostility with kindness, frustration with patience, disconnection with warmth"). Only findings that are essential. Ranked, most damaging first.

## Essential findings

**1. "She's just stressed" with nothing stopping him from saying it to her. Harm to the marriage.** (Skeptic, Paul)
Lines: "a line like that is more a sign of how stressed and overworked she is" (l.17); "It's an outward manifestation of how she's feeling on the inside. Maybe she isn't in a good mental state." (l.21).
A defensive man takes this reading and says it to her: "you're just stressed." That turns a zing into a fight about her state of mind. This is the same risk that got the tantrum paragraph cut. The reading is good when he keeps it to himself, but the chapter never tells him to.
Fix (desk line, after "Either way, you don't have to be offended."): add "That reading is for you to keep, not something to say to her."

**2. "None of this is surrender" is asserted with nothing behind it. Objection 2 is left unanswered.** (Skeptic, Paul; author's call)
Line: "None of this is surrender. It's my own advice to myself, learned through experience." (l.41)
The rebuild cut "giving in or" and the cruel-remarks line. What remains around this sentence: "irrespective of what she is doing", "She deserves kindness every day, always ... You don't wait for her to earn it", and Fix 3's "only". The Skeptic reads all of that as "capitulation dressed as wisdom", the exact objection `02-audience.md` says must be met. Paul, whose wife's remarks may really be contempt, hears "absorb everything". The Chapter 11 pointer (l.21) is the only counterweight, and it sits two sections earlier. These cuts were made in session, so this is his call. Flag only. If he wants a repair, restore one plain line after "None of this is surrender.", for example "Letting a zing go doesn't mean doing whatever she says."

**3. The Seneca gloss promises more than Seneca, and it puts every fight on him.** (Skeptic)
Line: "A zing only turns into a fight if you send one back." (l.25, desk gloss)
Seneca says it takes two. "Only" makes that a guarantee, which means any fight that happens is his fault. It also adds to the weight in Finding 2.
Fix: "A zing rarely turns into a fight unless you send one back."

**4. "Equally tired" invites the comparison Ch6 and Ch7 take apart.** (Beta reader Eric, Tension)
Line: "When she's tired, you may be tired too. That's two people who are equally tired." (l.35)
The line moves from "may be" to "equally" and so sets up a parity claim. The likely result is that he says "I'm tired too", which is the tally out loud. That is the move Ch6 ("the tally never gets read out loud") warns against.
Fix: "That's two tired people."

## Flag, not counted

Ch1 and Ch8 ("The moment goes back to Chapter 1: three seconds, then the question") have already set it up as the book's question. Here it gets no callback, so a reader coming days later misses that this is the spine. The question is his wording. A cheap fix to the lead-in, if it is desk wording: "it comes down to the question from Chapter 1:".
- **Not raised again (already ruled):** the laziness check repeats Ch6 l.53 and Ch8 l.29. The author ruled "Middle" and kept it.

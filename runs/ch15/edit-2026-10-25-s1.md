**The zing.** My family has a word for that kind of comment. We call it a zing, and there are so many in a day it's hard to keep count. A zing is a little slight, a cutting remark. It's a critique, only small.

I didn't handle them well. Every little critique from her was met with an equal or escalated response. I had no patience, I didn't let things go, I wasn't kind. **Frankly, neither was she.**

~~Answering back made sense, the way most habits do: you use what rewards.~~ **Arguing styles drift over time, almost like evolution. You use what rewards.** I'd feel tired. I'd feel frustrated that immediately she was giving me things to do. Her words felt like she was calling me lazy. **And the ironic part is that it wasn't about any one topic. I was fed up with how we argued.**

You know that pull. You're worn out, the comment stings, and answering it feels like the natural thing to do. What changed for me came slowly, through experience. ~~I started asking who has the strength to de-escalate.~~ **I started asking: who has the strength to de-escalate? Somebody has to be calm. Somebody has to be patient. Somebody has to be the one who brings the warmth.**

We have a saying in our house: "Never be the worst part of somebody's day." It's a spot check, a way to make sure we're not unkind. It's also the reason not to send a zing back. A sharper answer is the quickest way to be the worst part of her day.

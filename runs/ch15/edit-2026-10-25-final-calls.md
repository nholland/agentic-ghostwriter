## Your four calls (each is your line)

**1. "Most men would say no."**
*Now:* "I think a lot of men, if you asked them whether they're the best part of their wife's day, would say no."
The voice rules you set ban the "most men fail" form, because it makes the reader feel like the exception.
*Suggested:* "If you asked yourself whether you're the best part of her day, the honest answer might be no."

**2. "None of this is surrender" has nothing behind it now.**
Skeptical readers hear "do whatever she says."
*Suggested:* put back the line we cut: "None of this is surrender. **Letting a zing go doesn't mean doing whatever she says.** It's my own advice to myself, learned through experience."

**3. "Acts of service."**
*Now:* "Why are those kind? They are acts of service, but that isn't why they're kind. It's because these are normally things that she does..."
Two problems. Chapter 12 says acts of service is *her* love language, so denying it clashes. And "those" covers the foot rub and the listening, but the reason only fits the chore.
*Suggested:* "Why is the chore kind? It's an act of service, the language she reads best. But it's kind because these are normally things that she does, and I'm taking chores and work off of her plate."

**4. Two small swaps.**
- "That's two people who are equally tired." → "That's two tired people." ("Equally" starts a tally of who's more tired, which Chapter 6 warns against.)
- "tell her I see her" → "tell her what I see her carrying." (Chapter 14 says a bare "I see you" can land hollow.)

# Seneca, On Clemency II.4-6 (Stewart): help, not pity

**What it says, plainly:** Help her steadily; don't be swept into her distress. Seneca's target is pity, and he insists the Stoics are the gentlest school.

# Wording (as read; copied verbatim from the research file)

> II.4: pity is "a disease of the mind."

> II.5: all good men will show mercy and mildness, but will avoid pity, which is a vice incident to weak minds which cannot endure the sight of another's sufferings

> II.5: Pity considers a man's misfortunes and does not consider to what they are due: mercy is combined with reason

> II.5: no school of philosophy is more gentle and benignant, none is more full of love towards man … its maxims are, to be of service and assistance to others.

> II.6: he will dry the tears of others, but will not mingle his own with them

> II.6: the wise man will not pity men, but will help them and be of service to them.

# Seneca, On the Happy Life XXIV (Stewart): an opportunity for a benefit (money context)

**What it says, plainly:** A man of means can do good with his money anywhere, including inside his own house.

# Wording (as read; copied verbatim from the research file)

> Wherever there is a human being, there is an opportunity for a benefit: consequently, money may be distributed even within one's own threshold

**Evidence label:** PRIMARY READ at page-text, per section B4 of the research file. This
establishes wording only. Not `verified`.

# Possible use in Ch15 (proposal only; the author decides)

Only with its context named, or not at all (research file). 'Within one's own threshold' fits the home only as a stretch.

# Cautions

- CONTEXT DEFECT: the passage is about giving money, including to slaves and freedmen in one's own house. The popular line 'where there is a human being there is an opportunity for kindness' lifts it out of that context, and 'kindness' is not Stewart's word ('benefit').
- Do not print it as Seneca's sentence about home life (research-kindness D).
- House-edition gap: 06-sources.md sets no edition for Seneca's dialogues. Stewart 1889 is public domain; Ch4 printed De Ira as Basore. The author should rule before a dialogue is quoted (inbox candidate in both research passes).

# Seneca, De Ira II.34 (Stewart): a quarrel taken up on one side falls to the ground

**What it says, plainly:** It takes two to fight. The stronger one gives way first.

# Wording (as read; copied verbatim from the research file)

> II.34.5: If any one is angry with you, meet his anger by returning benefits for it: a quarrel which is only taken up on one side falls to the ground: it takes two men to fight. But suppose that there is an angry struggle on both sides, even then, he is the better man who first gives way; the winner is the real loser.

> Latin, as given in the research file: *nisi paria non pugnant … ille est melior qui prior pedem rettulit; victus est qui vicit.*

> II.34.1: It is the part of a mean and wretched man to turn and bite one's biter

# Basore's wording, read 2026-10-24 (page-text)

Read in the University of Toronto scan of the Heinemann 1928 volume, "Seneca, Moral Essays I, with an
English translation by John W. Basore" (archive.org item moralessayswithe01seneuoft; Loeb Classical
Library; contains De Providentia, De Constantia, De Ira, De Clementia). The text is OCR with some
noise; the line break in "withdraws" is reconstructed. The Latin facing page matches the Latin in the
Stewart concept above.

> Does a man get angry? Do you on the contrary challenge him with kindness. Animosity, if abandoned by one side, forthwith dies; it takes two to make a fight. But if anger shall be rife on both sides, if the conflict comes, he is the better man who first withdraws; the vanquished is the one who wins.

(De Ira II.34, Basore's page headed "ON ANGER, II. XXXIV. 4-XXXV. 3".) Same passage as the Stewart wording above, different English: Stewart has "it takes two men to fight" and "the winner is the real loser"; Basore has "it takes two to make a fight" and "the vanquished is the one who wins".

Rights: the volume is a 1928 publication. Works published in the United States in 1928 entered the US public domain on 1 January 2024; the volume appeared simultaneously in London and New York. Status outside the US is not confirmed. Basore's 1935 volume III (De Beneficiis) remains in copyright and is not covered by this.

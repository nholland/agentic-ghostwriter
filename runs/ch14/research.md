## Direct answer to the three research questions

**Contentment versus complacency.** These are not standardized research
variables; the distinction is editorial. Relationship studies separate
*acceptance of the partner* from *maintenance behavior*. A married-couple
study found that accepting a partner and feeling accepted were associated
with satisfaction, though it was cross-sectional
(`/okf/citations/rossman-lerner-cordova-2022-partner-felt-acceptance.md`;
authors' abstract inspected; do not present as causal). A longitudinal married-couple study links
maintenance behavior, perceived responsiveness and gratitude in a reciprocal
pattern (`/okf/citations/kubacka-et-al-2011-gratitude-maintenance.md`). The
author's earlier framework, `/okf/frameworks/romance-as-disciplined-attention.md`,
already calls for care made visible through action. Candidate distinction for
the author, **not accepted yet**: contentment stops making her earn her worth
through endless improvement; complacency stops noticing and caring for her.

**Keeping interest and dedication.** Three mechanisms have relevant support:
1. Notice and respond to what matters to the present partner. Existing
   `/okf/citations/perceived-partner-responsiveness-reis.md` frames this as
   being understood, valued and cared for. The author's
   `/okf/frameworks/continuing-curiosity-as-partners-change.md` is already a
   Chapter 13 idea; Ch14 should use it to explain loving a changing person,
   not repeat Ch13's date tactics.
2. Appreciate specific acts and keep acting. Kubacka et al. connect gratitude
   to reciprocal maintenance across four years in married couples, but the
   abstract alone cannot establish a simple causal prescription. Existing
   `/okf/citations/gordon-et-al-2012-gratitude-relationship-maintenance.md`
   supports a similar appreciation/maintenance association, with its own
   source limits.
3. Share chosen experiences that both enjoy. Existing
   `/okf/citations/aron-et-al-2000-novel-arousing-activities.md` reports
   short-term experimental gains from novel shared tasks. Existing
   `/okf/citations/harasymchuk-et-al-2021-planning-date-nights.md` and full
   primary text at `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170361/`
   associate approach-oriented date planning with exciting activities and
   reported closeness. Neither warrants “schedule novelty and the marriage
   will be saved,” nor does novelty alone answer what makes a spouse enough.

**Determining “enough.”** No study yields a universal threshold. The best
evidence reframes what a person is measuring. In one cross-sectional sample,
matching ideals about warmth and intimacy related more strongly to
relationship quality than matching ideals about attractiveness and success
(`/okf/citations/rodriguez-hadden-knee-2015-intrinsic-extrinsic-ideals.md`).
Three-year newlywed data suggest that more match beyond one's ideal does not
consistently yield more acceptance or satisfaction, but results vary by
measure and do not supply a moral line
(`/okf/citations/buyukcan-tetik-2017-ideal-standards-acceptance.md`). The
author can use these to question a beauty/income scoreboard. He alone must
decide how the chapter names a concern that does need speech.

## Stoic grounding

The targeted source check is in `stoic-research.md`. Seneca, Letter 92.11–13,
prefers health and strength but locates goodness in sound choice
(`/okf/citations/seneca-letter-92-health-strength-and-judgment.md`). Seneca,
Letter 104, cares more for his own health because of Paulina
(`/okf/citations/seneca-letter-104-health-for-paulina.md`). Musonius Rufus,
Lecture XIIIA, teaches mutual love through health and sickness
(`/okf/citations/musonius-rufus-on-marriage.md`). Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 30,
asks what one's relationship calls one to do, without supplying a modern
threshold for when to raise a marital concern
(`/okf/citations/epictetus-enchiridion-30-relational-duties.md`). Use the
book's reader-facing term **preferred external** on first mention, per
`/okf/frameworks/preferred-externals-terminology.md`.

## Research gaps and overclaim controls

- `/okf/citations/ch14-appearance-change-and-attraction.md`: unverified.
  The author observes a possible connection among aging, changing bodies and
  infidelity; this is a research question, not a causal claim.
- `/okf/citations/ch14-pornography-romance-media-expectations.md`:
  unverified. No claim that porn or romance novels cause unrealistic marital
  expectations without a relevant primary study and scope.
- `/okf/citations/infidelity-motivation-gendered-split.md`: pre-existing,
  unverified; do not rely on it for a motive claim.
- The author's biological courtship-to-complacency frame at
  `/okf/frameworks/biological-mate-seeking-vs-marriage-complacency.md` still
  says research is needed for its evolutionary mechanism. Treat it as the
  author's observation, not an established biological law.
- Do not say “everyone's attraction fades,” “a good spouse never raises
  weight,” “health doesn't matter,” or “if you water it, it will thrive.”
  Each turns a live tension into a universal rule or promise.

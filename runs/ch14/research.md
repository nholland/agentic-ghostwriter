## The mindset material now available

The author has asked for a positive practice, not merely an instruction to stop
comparing. These are **source-backed options**, with the marriage synthesis
proposed in `proposed-concepts.md`, not a new author-approved protocol.

| Question | Source finding | Careful marriage application |
|---|---|---|
| What deserves attention? | Marcus, *Meditations* VII.27, George Long: consider existing goods and how desired they would be if absent; the same passage warns against excessive attachment. | Notice what this life with this person contains before evaluating what it lacks. A wife is a person, not an owned good. |
| What can one appreciate besides looks or output? | Marcus VI.48, Long: recall the virtues of the people near you and keep them in view. | Attend to her character and the particular person she is. Irritations need not be renamed virtues. |
| Can gratitude be practiced rather than waited for? | Emmons & McCullough (2003): randomized gratitude-listing exercises changed some well-being outcomes compared with particular controls. | Naming a specific good gives attention something concrete to do. A wife-focused adaptation is an application, not a tested protocol from this study. |
| How can the familiar become noticeable again? | Koo et al. (2008): considering how a positive event might never have happened improved immediate feelings in experiments; Study 4 examined romantic relationships. | Consider the ordinary circumstances by which the two of you met and could have missed each other. This is a possible exercise, not imagined bereavement or a threat of loss. |
| How does private appreciation become love she can receive? | Gordon et al. (2012): appreciation, responsiveness and commitment were associated across multiple designs. | Say what you value and act with care. This bridges appreciation to outward warmth without promising reciprocity. |

Do not flatten contentment, positive affect, satisfaction, gratitude and
commitment into one measured outcome. No paper here measures the complete
moral practice the author calls “enough.” Their convergence supplies plausible
parts; the complete chapter meaning remains his synthesis.

**General gratitude experiment.** Canonical
`okf/citations/emmons-mccullough-2003-counting-blessings.md`.
Original paper read: https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/pdfs/GratitudePDFs/6Emmons-BlessingsBurdens.pdf .
Three randomized studies used student and neuromuscular-disease samples.
The benefit varied with outcome and comparison condition; in Study 2 positive
affect improved relative to hassles, but not social comparison, and negative
affect did not differ. This supports practicing attention to benefits, not a
promise of universal or permanent contentment. Keep the chapter's claim small;
no health prescriptions, percentage improvements or marriage outcome claims.

**Mental subtraction experiment.** Canonical
`okf/citations/koo-et-al-2008-mental-subtraction.md`.
Original article read: https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/KOO%20ALGOE%20WILSON%20GILBERT%20%282008%29.pdf .
Study 4 included 88 participants in relationships of at least five years,
selected for at-or-above-average initial satisfaction. The 15–20-minute
writing exercise concerned how partners might never have met or become a
couple. The absence condition showed a larger immediate satisfaction increase
in the planned contrast. Do not generalize to distressed marriages, durable
changes, or a universal best practice. The source itself discusses mixed
findings for simple gratitude exercises; it does not justify “gratitude always
works.” Do not build a new movie metaphor from the paper's title.

**Relationship bridge, existing source with new primary access.** Canonical
update `okf/citations/gordon-et-al-2012-gratitude-relationship-maintenance.md`
reconciles the existing book concept's old access limitation. Primary PDF:
https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/To_Have_and_to_Hold-_Gratitude_Promotes_Relationship_Maintenance_in_Intimate_Bonds.pdf .
Study 2 began with 99 undergraduates; 4% were married. Seven daily reports and
a nine-month follow-up connected appreciation with commitment and persistence.
The persistence association weakened after controlling initial satisfaction;
feeling appreciated did not predict growth in own appreciation over nine
months. Use as association, not an experimentally proven chain. Ch12 already
uses it: avoid retelling the study in detail. Its existing `verifiable` status
is retained, evidence improves to `page-text`; nothing is `verified`.

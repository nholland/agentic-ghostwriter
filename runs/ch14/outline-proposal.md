constitution, not an accepted content concept.

```diff
- **Premise:** The comparison a man loses to is never a real rival — it's an imagined composite built from other people's best single traits, and no real wife, or real husband, can survive being measured against a highlight reel.
+ **Premise:** A husband practices contentment by giving sustained attention and gratitude to the real person his wife becomes across years of change. He tends the marriage he has, so beauty, income, talent, and passing comparisons do not become conditions on his love.
- **The reader's takeaway:** He can name the specific mechanism that turns a stranger's one striking trait into apparent proof his wife isn't enough, and has a standard — contentment as a chosen, practiced discipline, not a feeling he's waiting to arrive — for what "enough" actually means in the marriage he already chose.
+ **The reader's takeaway:** He can practice enough through attention, specific appreciation, gratitude, and care for the marriage he has. He can notice when a preference has become a demand his wife must satisfy to be loved.
- **Key points:**
- 1. The hedonic treadmill, applied to one specific marriage: the courtship-era intensity was never purely about her — it ran substantially on novelty and uncertainty, and its fading is universal, not a verdict on her, on him, or on the marriage; a man who doesn't know this mistakes ordinary brain chemistry for proof something is wrong
- 2. Miswanting and the comparison trap: attraction to a striking trait in someone else — a laugh, a look, an easy rapport — is a comparison between one spiking quality and his wife's entire, known, real person, complaints and bad days included; no whole person survives that comparison, including her, if she ran the same one on him
- 3. "Enough" as jurisdiction aimed at desire itself: the same governing principle used against outsourced mood (Chapter 2) and scorekeeping (Chapter 7), now aimed at wanting — he doesn't control the stray attraction crossing his mind, only whether he feeds it or names it
- 4. Proactive vs. reactive, and why this book leads with the first: most men only address contentment after the wandering eye or the resentment has already shown up (gratitude lists after the fact, "this is normal" self-talk, chasing novelty impulsively). The Stoic Husband treats it as a discipline practiced in advance — naming the coming lull before it arrives, planning real novelty before boredom goes looking for its own, and savoring as a standing habit rather than a rescue
+ **Key points:**
+ 1. Enough begins with where he places his attention: notice the actual life and person in front of him before an imagined alternative sets the standard.
+ 2. Appreciation grows specific. Remember what could have been absent; see the small acts, traits, and history he has begun to treat as guaranteed; thank her for what she actually brings to the life they share.
+ 3. Watering is active love: appreciation appears in attention, gratitude, and reciprocal care. The marriage studies can support modest claims about these practices; they cannot promise to cure disconnection.
+ 4. Comparison and adaptation explain why the mind wanders. A brief wisdom boundary leaves room to care for health and raise genuine concerns without making his wife's changing body, income, or gifts the measure of her worth.

+ **Central story/example:** The author's real account of his wife as a light sleeper, morning person, frugal keeper of small things, mother on sabbatical after an executive career, gardener, and woman whose new gray hairs he loves. Use only details that survive author review; do not invent dialogue. The work-trip comparison may remain a clearly fictional secondary example if useful.
```

The author redirected emphasis on 2026-09-29. Contentment's mindset and practice
are the center: attention, appreciation, gratitude, and what it means to
"water" the marriage already chosen. The complacency boundary gets a brief,
honest acknowledgement. Comparison and proactive practice remain subordinate
mechanisms. Research should draw on general contentment thought and evidence,
then apply it carefully to marriage. The research pass must test, rather than assume, the empirical
claims about aging, attraction, and media-shaped expectations. The exact
wisdom test for whether a concern deserves a conversation remains unresolved
by the interview and is not invented here.

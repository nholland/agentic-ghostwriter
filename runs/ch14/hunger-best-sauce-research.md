# Ch14 — "Hunger is the best sauce": origin and Stoic standing

*Researcher, cold. 2026-10-02 06:15. Commissioned after interview Round 6 (`runs/ch14/interview.md`, line 184 onward): the proverb is the author's own addition and his wife did not say it. He believes it is Stoic and wants it translated back to Stoicism.*

Concept written: `runs/ch14/okf/citations/hunger-best-sauce-socratic-not-stoic.md` (status unverified, evidence_source page-text, quote_form none).

## Verdict

**No, it is not a Stoic phrase.** It is a proverb credited to **Socrates**. Xenophon records it first, then Cicero. Stoics say related things, and Seneca says something very close in Letter 123.2. In modern European languages, the words "best sauce … is hunger" are most visibly fixed by Cervantes (1615).

## Safest wording for the book

> An old proverb, older than the Stoics, says hunger is the best sauce. Xenophon puts it in Socrates' mouth. Seneca lived it: stuck with bad bread, he chose to wait until hunger made it taste good (*Letters* 123.2).

Do not print "the Stoics say", and do not put quotation marks around the proverb as anyone's words. Any verbatim Seneca line goes to the Fact-Checker and the author's Gummere copy first.

## Plate alone: ten-second takeaway

Written from `runs/ch14/pdf/plate.png` alone, before reading anything else:

> "If you keep noticing what you value in your wife, it pours out of you as kinder replies, and that is what keeps your shared life growing."

What each piece of text refers to, on a cold read:

- **Title, "Appreciation becomes care":** a claim that one thing turns into another. The drawing shows it as can to water.
- **Subtitle, "Notice what you value in your wife":** the instruction, the thing he does.
- **"appreciation" inside the can:** what he holds and carries.
- **Bold "a kinder answer" by the stream:** names the water in mid-air, the form the appreciation takes as it leaves him. It sits beside the lower part of the stream, above the grass line, not on or under the grass. So it reads as *the pour itself*, not clearly as *what the water becomes where it lands*. That is acceptable: can, then pour, then grass already reads as appreciation, then care, then the shared life, which is what the title says.
- **Italic "the life you share":** names the grass and ground being watered.

**Received vs. damage avoided:** nothing on the plate is withered, broken or rescued. The grass gets something, and the plate shows giving and growth, not harm prevented. On this axis the plate is right.

**Skeptic, the misreading the drawing invites:** "a kinder answer" does not say whose answer it is. A defensive man can read it as what *he gets back*: notice her good points and she answers you more kindly. That turns the plate into a trade. A second, weaker misreading is that she is the lawn and he is the gardener. The label "the life you share" mostly closes that off, because the grass is the marriage, not her.

## Comparison with the distillation

Conversation sentence: "You practice enough by noticing what you value in your wife and letting that appreciation change how you care for her."

The movement survives cold: noticing, then appreciation, then changed care. "Enough" is not on the plate, and the mechanism holds without it.

**Verdict: EDIT (one relabel).** The core takeaway lands. But the distillation says outright that gratitude "asks for no particular response from her," and the one ambiguous label invites exactly the opposite reading, that her kinder answer is the payoff. The middle of the distillation already says whose word it is: "The same trait can get a harsh word or a loving one, and your attention picks the word." The plate should say the same.

## Edit list

1. **Relabel "a kinder answer" to "your kinder word".** Keep the same position, bold weight and size. "Your" makes it his conduct, not her response. "Word" matches the distillation's "your attention picks the word." It also drops "answer," which implies a question or a complaint came first. If the designer wants to keep "answer," then "your kinder answer" is the minimum fix: the "your" is the part that matters.

No other edits. Specifically:

- **Do not move the stream label down to the grass.** It would crowd "the life you share," and naming the pour is the right job for it: the pour is where appreciation becomes care.
- **Do not relabel the grass "her."** The distillation says "care for her," and the plate's recipient is the shared life. This is a deliberate small drift and it should stay. Making her the lawn is the worse misreading.

## Round 2: author's answer to exploratory questions

Author began with change in long marriages. He distinguished two causes of
changed appearance: neglect of personal health, which the chapter must not
excuse, and ordinary aging, which should not be treated as a partner's failure.
His own words:

> Contentment in the marriage can lead to a lack of focusing on yourself and your health, etc. This is very common. A lot of people let themselves go and that's not great. This chapter should not be an excuse for that. You still need to maintain your health.

> Just the fact that you stay married over a long period of time means, naturally, that the traditional stance of beauty begins to fade.

He asked how spouses can find joy through changes in their bodies, remain
attracted to a real person, and examine whether their beauty standards have
become unreasonable. This is the author's question, not a research finding.

> You learn to love your partner as they change, as they evolve, and you learn the discipline of enough, which is that there's a huge spectrum in beauty.

He extended comparison beyond looks to cooking, humor, and income. He named
romance novels and pornography as possible sources of distorted expectations,
and asked for research. These causal claims remain unverified.

> One of the things I love about the Stoics, and even the cynics, is that the discipline of enough really means that you find yourself being hyper-aware of when you're attached to something that's outside of you.

He compared contentment with a spouse to contentment with a small house or
dependable car, without saying a spouse is a possession. His favored line:

> The grass is really greener where you water it.

Personal details offered about his wife: she is a light sleeper and wakes him
to check sounds at night; she is a morning person who gets the household up;
she is frugal, saves small things, and sometimes retrieves items from the trash;
he loves her new gray hairs, which she does not want others to see; she was a
high-powered executive who earned a great deal and is now on sabbatical as a
full-time mother with a “wicked green thumb.” He described loving her quirks,
including the friction. These are author-supplied details, not a license to
publish her private preferences or career details before disclosure is discussed.

> She is enough for me, despite how our bodies change, despite how she was a high-powered executive and earned tons of money, and now she has taken a sabbatical and she is a full-time mom with a wicked green thumb. She is enough and it takes tons of work to make sure that you realize that the discipline of enough is critical.

He said friends' inability to accept change or unmet preferences has cost them
dearly. No friend's story or outcome was specified; do not invent one.

> Even though something may not be what you prefer, you aren't attached to that because you are happy with enough.

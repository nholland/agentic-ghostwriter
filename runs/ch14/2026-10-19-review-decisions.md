## B. Three choices only you can make.

**6. Who is noticing, in "When either of you notices it, you can say so kindly"?**
It can mean you admit it when you catch yourself, or it can read as permission to tell her she is comparing you.
(a) "When you catch yourself doing it, you can admit it to her kindly."
(b) "When either of you catches the other comparing, you can say so kindly, and hear it kindly."
**My recommendation: (a).** The chapter is about your attention, and (b) hands a defensive reader the "what about her?" objection.

**7. The breakfast and hunger paragraph reads as a backhanded compliment.**
Right now "hunger makes poor bread taste good" is followed by "Anything she makes is enough for me." A reader hears: her cooking is the poor bread. Xenophon also appears with no introduction.
Proposed ending of that paragraph: "An older proverb says hunger is the best sauce. Xenophon, who wrote down Socrates' conversations, puts it in Socrates' mouth. Seneca lived it: with only poor bread on hand, he waited until hunger made it taste good (*Letters* 123). The bread didn't change. His hunger did. When I come to the table glad to be there, anything she makes is enough for me."
The last sentence adds your reading, so it needs your yes. **My recommendation: approve.**

**8. The chapter ends twice.**
"Slow down enough to taste them." closes on an image. Then "Thankfulness is how you keep wanting what you already have." adds a moral. The voice rules ban both a moral close and a second ending. The readers split: your main reader found the last line the most quotable sentence in the chapter; the others said the chapter had already landed.
**My recommendation: keep the thankfulness line as the close** (it is your own 2026-10-02 direction and the spec's key realization) **and cut "Slow down enough to taste them."** If you would rather keep the image, the thankfulness line moves up into "Let appreciation become care" and the spec's key realization changes.

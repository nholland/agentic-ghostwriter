## Direct source findings

1. **Seneca, Letter 92.11–13 (Gummere).** Health and strength are sought as
   according to nature, but the good lies in the judgment of how one chooses
   and uses them. This supports the distinction between valuing health and
   making another person's appearance a moral or relational verdict. The
   latter application to marriage is an inference, not Seneca's wording.
   Source: https://senecaletters.com/en/letters/92
   Citation: `/okf/citations/seneca-letter-92-health-strength-and-judgment.md`.
2. **Seneca, Letter 104.1–5 (Gummere).** Seneca records Paulina urging him to
   protect his health. He comes to care for himself partly out of care for
   her and calls attending to one's old age for the sake of a loved one a
   kindness. This directly complicates a reading of “indifferent” as “health
   doesn't matter.” It speaks to one's own care, not a license to police a
   spouse's body. Source: https://monadnock.net/seneca/104.html
   Citation: `/okf/citations/seneca-letter-104-health-for-paulina.md`.
3. **Musonius Rufus, Lecture XIIIA, Lutz translation.** Marriage is described
   as shared life and mutual love “in health and in sickness,” with reciprocal
   devotion. The same passage criticizes a partner who neglects the other or
   fixes attention elsewhere. This is stronger support for enduring
   companionship than for any claim that a spouse's health is irrelevant.
   Lutz is not the house's public-domain quotation standard: paraphrase only.
   Source: https://sites.google.com/site/thestoiclife/the_teachers/musonius-rufus/lectures/13-0
   Existing citation: `/okf/citations/musonius-rufus-on-marriage.md`.
4. **Epictetus, Enchiridion 30 (Long).** Duties are determined by the relation,
   and attention goes to one's own conduct even when the other person falls
   short. This is an ethical direction, not a rule for which needs may be
   discussed. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm
   Existing citation: `/okf/citations/epictetus-enchiridion-30-relational-duties.md`.

## Limits

- None of these texts says weight gain is automatically no problem, or gives
  a threshold for when to raise a concern. Nor do they directly discuss a
  spouse leaving paid work. Treat aging, weight, and income as circumstances
  distinct from the person's character, while leaving the author's proposed
  wisdom question open.
- “Preferred indifferent” is the classical category, not “unimportant.” The
  book's chosen reader-facing phrase is “preferred external” (see
  `/okf/frameworks/preferred-externals-terminology.md`).
- The clinical/empirical questions on weight, attraction, infidelity, and
  media-shaped expectations have not been researched by this targeted pass.
- The Epictetus relationship passage includes severe father/son duties and
  should not be stretched into advice to endure harm or remain silent.

# Source finding

Emmons, R. A., & McCullough, M. E. (2003). Counting blessings versus burdens: An experimental investigation of gratitude and subjective well-being in daily life. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 84(2), 377–389. DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377.

# Findings and limits

Three randomized studies compared gratitude listing with hassles, neutral events, social comparison, or a control. Samples included students and people with neuromuscular disease. Benefits appeared on several well-being outcomes, not all outcomes. Study 2's daily gratitude condition exceeded hassles on positive affect but not downward social comparison; negative affect did not differ. Physical-health findings varied. The study supports a bounded claim that deliberately attending to benefits can affect reported well-being under these conditions. It does not demonstrate durable marital contentment, fidelity, restored attraction, or a universal best gratitude schedule. Original PDF abstract, methods and results passages inspected; no quotations supplied.

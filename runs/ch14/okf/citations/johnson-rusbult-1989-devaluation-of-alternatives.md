# Source finding

Study 1 (longitudinal): perceived quality of alternatives decreased over time among those who stayed and increased among those who left. Study 2 (computer dating paradigm): greatest devaluation by highly committed people facing highly attractive alternatives. Study 3 (simulation): more rejection of alternatives under high commitment. Devaluation tracked commitment more than satisfaction.

# Limits

Shows commitment changes how alternatives are perceived; does NOT show alternatives are actually worse, so it cannot support 'the grass is not greener.' Mostly dating/student samples. Temptation is Chapter 21's territory ('trust-after-betrayal'); flag any Ch14 use to its brief. A later dissertation notes the theory's assumptions are under-tested.

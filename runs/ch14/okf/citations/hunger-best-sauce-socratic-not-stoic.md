# Source finding and limits

**Not a Stoic phrase.** The sentiment is credited to Socrates, not to any Stoic.

- Xenophon, *Memorabilia* 1.3.5: Socrates came to food so that "the desire for food was his relish" (Greek *ὄψον*). Dakyns renders it "the appetite he brought to it was sauce sufficient." Socrates says the same to Antiphon at 1.6.5.
- Cicero, *De Finibus* 2.90 (Latin read on Perseus): Socrates, *audio dicentem, cibi condimentum esse famem, potionis sitim*. That is, hunger is the seasoning of food and thirst of drink. There is no "best" in the Latin. Cicero sets it against the Epicureans.
- Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 5.97 (Latin read on Perseus): *desideriis omnia ista condiri*, "all these things are seasoned by desire". Then Socrates walks till evening *obsonare ambulando famem*, "to buy hunger as his relish by walking". Yonge's 19th-century English adds "an appetite is the best sauce". That "best" belongs to the translator. The surrounding passage reports Epicurean views (Perseus apparatus: Epic. fr. 459).
- The wording "the best sauce in the world is hunger" is printed in Cervantes, *Don Quixote* Part II ch. 5 (1615), where Teresa Panza says it. The Spanish reads "La mejor salsa del mundo es la hambre". Read on Gutenberg.
- The Latin proverb form *optimum condimentum fames* (Erasmus) and an English use in Udall's *Apophthegmes* (1542) come from search only. They are **not confirmed**. The archive.org host and the Tilley and EEBO pages were blocked from this container.

**The nearest Stoic passage** is Seneca, *Letter* 123.2 (Gummere, the house edition, read as Wikisource transcription). He has only bad bread at hand, and says hunger will make it good, so he will not eat until hunger bids him. Related: *Letter* 119.4 ("A starving man despises nothing") and 119.14 ("Hunger is not ambitious"). Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus* (Diogenes Laertius 10.130–131, Hicks): bread and water give the highest pleasure to hungry lips. That one is Epicurean, not Stoic.

No hit in Long's *Meditations* (Gutenberg #2680) or in the Long, Higginson or Crossley Epictetus selections, for a string search on sauce, relish or hunger. A string search is not proof of absence. Musonius Rufus Lectures 18A–B, on food, were not read: Lutz is in copyright and not reachable.

Safe use: call it an old proverb going back to Socrates, which Seneca lived out (123.2). Do not call it Stoic, and do not put it in quotation marks as any ancient author's words. Any verbatim Seneca line needs the author's own Gummere copy.

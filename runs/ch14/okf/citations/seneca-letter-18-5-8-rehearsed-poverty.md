# Source finding

18.5: set aside days content with the scantiest fare and coarse dress, asking whether this is the condition one feared. 18.6: train in peace for stress, like a soldier on manoeuvres. 18.7: make it real, three or four days or more; afterward one leaps for joy at a pennyworth of food and understands that peace of mind does not depend on Fortune. 18.8: one is merely doing what thousands of slaves and poor people do every day.

# Limits

The closest ancient parallel to the author's 'you appreciate it once you have lived the cost', but its subject is fear of poverty and self-sufficiency, not gratitude to another person. Ch14 already carries the dinner section and hunger as sauce (Letter 123); a third food image would stack. Lead only.

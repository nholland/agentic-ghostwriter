# Story interview kit: married men, Prologue through Conclusion

- **Built:** 2026-11-16 13:58 (session clock), by the Researcher (`gw-researcher`)
- **Why:** Marcus's reader signal, "You really have no stories, and you personally have risked nothing" (`runs/signals/2026-11-16-marcus-chat1.md`, `-chat2.md`).
- **Not decided here:** whether other men's stories go in the book at all. That is inbox #174.
- **Lessons:** each one-line lesson below is quoted or closely trimmed from `03-outline.md` (premise, takeaway or key point) or the chapter's `distillation.md`. The source is marked after each line.

## How to run these

- **Consent first.** Tell him what it's for. Tell him his name and anything identifying (town, job, kids, dates) will be changed. He can skip any question, or pull a story later.
- **Record and transcribe.** Get his yes to the recording on the recording.
- **30-45 minutes, 3-4 chapters per interview.** Don't run the whole kit.
- **One moment, then stay in it.** When he drifts into advice or opinion, pull him back with:
  - "What happened right before?"
  - "What did you say, exactly?"
  - "What happened a week later?"
- **Keep it on his side.** Ask what he did and felt, not what she's like. Let silences run.
- **Raw intake only.** Each story is kept as raw intake. It enters the book only through `/gw-sources` and the author's approval.

## Prologue
**Lesson:** The mistake this book exists to keep the reader from repeating: Stoicism as armor instead of presence, composed but unreachable, until his wife named it. *(outline, premise and key moves)*
1. Tell me about a time you thought you were staying calm, and she told you that you'd checked out.
2. Walk me through the stretch when you realized you were winging it as a husband. What set it off?
3. Tell me about a night that showed you you're strong everywhere except at home.

## Introduction: The Man Without a Blueprint
**Lesson:** What it costs a man to have only one or two of calm, strength and warmth instead of all three. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about a time you were dependable but cold at home. When did you find out what it was costing?
2. Tell me about a time being easygoing turned into going along with everything. What did it cost you?
3. Was divorce ever said out loud, or nearly? Walk me through that day.

## Chapter 1: The Three-Second Window
**Lesson:** Between what she says and your response is three seconds. What fills it, chosen or inherited, is your marriage. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time you said the thing in the first three seconds and couldn't take it back.
2. Tell me about the first time you heard your dad's reaction come out of your own mouth.
3. Tell me about a time you caught yourself before you fired. What did you do instead, and how did it go?

## Chapter 2: Stop Outsourcing Your Peace
**Lesson:** When her mood sets your mood, you've outsourced your peace to someone who never signed up to carry it. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a night her bad mood became your bad mood. How did the rest of the house end up?
2. Walk me through a time you came home already sour and it rolled downhill to the kids.
3. Tell me about a time she was low and you stayed steady. What did you actually do?

## Chapter 3: Stop Defending Yourself
**Lesson:** Every time you defended yourself, you taught her the conversation wasn't worth having. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time she raised something and your defense was ready before she finished. What happened?
2. When did you notice she'd stopped bringing things to you? What tipped you off?
3. Tell me about a time you said "you're right" and stopped explaining. What changed after?

## Chapter 4: Anger Is Failed Leadership
**Lesson:** Anger isn't strength. Every outburst drives a nail you'll never fully pull out. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about the worst time you lost your temper at home. Who saw it, and what did it cost?
2. Tell me about a time you apologized for blowing up and could tell the apology didn't fix it.
3. Walk me through a time you felt the anger rise and chose something else. What did you do?

## Chapter 5: How to Fight Without Becoming Small
**Lesson:** The fight ends before the real thing gets said. That's not resolution. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a fight you both called "over" where the real thing never got said. When did it come back?
2. Tell me about a fight where you said something you're still not proud of. What was it really about?
3. Tell me about a time you went back, once things were calm, and raised the real thing. How did it go?

## Chapter 6: Duty Without Resentment
**Lesson:** Resentment can come from work you feel nobody sees, or from more work than you can keep carrying. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a night one comment about the housework landed like a verdict. What did you say back?
2. Tell me about a stretch when you were carrying more than you could keep up. How did it come out?
3. Tell me about a time you honestly were the one slacking. When did you see it, and what changed?

## Chapter 7: The End of Scorekeeping
**Lesson:** The ledger you keep in your head can only count the visible things, so it was always going to tell you that you're ahead. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time you were sure you gave more, then saw what she'd been carrying.
2. Tell me about an argument where you listed everything you do. How did that land?
3. Tell me about a time you quit keeping count and just gave more. What happened at home?

## Chapter 8: When Your Marriage Feels Unfair
**Lesson:** Unfairness rarely arrives as one moment. It's small things you never counted, tipping quietly until you finally feel the weight. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about the moment the marriage suddenly felt unfair to you. What small thing tipped it?
2. Tell me about a time you asked for a real change in who does what. What exactly did you ask?
3. Tell me about a time she said things weren't fair to her, and she was right. What did you do?

## Chapter 9: Silence Is Not Peace
**Lesson:** He kept saying yes expecting something back, and when it didn't come, the disappointment piled up quietly. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a stretch of saying "whatever you want" while keeping a list in your head. How did it end?
2. Tell me about the day you realized you'd stopped caring. What had you not been saying?
3. Tell me about a time you said plainly what you wanted instead of swallowing it. What happened next?

## Chapter 10: Boundaries Are Strength
**Lesson:** A boundary is only real if you can say in one sentence what it protects; holding it on the night someone you love is upset is the job. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a line you never drew, about money, family, or how you two talk, until it cost you.
2. Tell me about holding a line while someone you love was upset about it. What did you say?
3. Tell me about a house rule you'd defend but never explained. What happened when somebody pushed on it?

## Chapter 11: Speak or Endure
**Lesson:** You keep arguing with her in your head, but you haven't told her what's bothering you. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about an argument you ran in your head for weeks and never had with her. How did it come out?
2. Tell me about a hard season you just had to get through. Where did you go numb?
3. Tell me about something you finally said out loud after holding it too long. What happened that week?

## Chapter 12: Romance Is a Discipline
**Lesson:** Romance takes effort, and when a week gets heavy it's what goes, because it has no deadline and nobody watching. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time she asked when you last took her out and you couldn't remember.
2. Tell me about the last time you planned something for her a week ahead. What was it, and how'd she react?
3. Tell me about a time you showed love your way and it didn't land with her. What did you learn?

## Chapter 13: Pursue Her After You Have Her
**Lesson:** Winning her was the start. You keep choosing her by staying curious about who she is now. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time you realized you didn't know what she'd want anymore. What tipped you off?
2. Tell me about something she mentioned for months before you really heard it. What happened when you did?
3. Tell me about a time you asked her a real question and remembered the answer. What came of it?

## Chapter 14: She Is Enough
**Lesson:** Contentment grows through practiced attention and appreciation for the real person you chose. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time you caught yourself comparing your marriage to someone else's life. What pulled you back?
2. Tell me about a harsh word you used to have for something about her. What made you change the word?
3. Tell me about a time you thanked her for something specific. What did you say, and what happened?

## Chapter 15: Be Patient, Be Kind
**Lesson:** Meet hostility with kindness, frustration with patience, and disconnection with warmth, and ask whether you were the best part of her day. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a time a small sharp remark turned into a real fight. What did you say back?
2. Tell me about a night you were both fed up and you chose patience anyway. What did you do?
3. Tell me about a day you made her day better on purpose. What did you do, and how did you know?

## Chapter 16: The Marriage in Flow
**Lesson:** Your marriage is a home the two of you own; life wears on it through nobody's fault, so you keep investing in it. *(distillation)*
1. Tell me about a season when the marriage was really working. What were you doing differently then?
2. Tell me about a time you said something like "I can only control me" to her. How did it land?
3. Tell me about a time life wore you two down through nobody's fault. How did you come back?

## Chapter 17: The Sexless Marriage
**Lesson:** A sexless stretch is rarely just one thing, and it needs clearer thinking than most men bring to it. *(outline, premise and takeaway)*
1. Tell me about a long dry stretch in your marriage. What did you do early on that didn't help?
2. Tell me about the first honest conversation you had about it. What did you actually say?
3. Tell me how you found out what was really going on. What changed after?

## Chapter 18: Living Separate Lives
**Lesson:** The roommate marriage, functional, civil and quietly hollow, is invisible because nothing dramatic has happened; the way back is small daily choices to re-enter. *(outline, premise and takeaway)*
1. Tell me about when you realized you two only talked logistics anymore. How long had it been?
2. Walk me through the first time you tried a real conversation again. How awkward was it?
3. Did living like roommates ever bring you near separating? Walk me through that point.

## Chapter 19: When Faith, Values, or Ambition Diverge
**Lesson:** A marriage that started aligned and drifted needs his clearest and most honest thinking, not his most optimistic. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about the moment you realized you two wanted different things from life.
2. Tell me about a time one of you changed in faith or values. How did you handle it at first?
3. Tell me about deciding whether a difference was livable or a dealbreaker. What decided it?

## Chapter 20: The Marriage Underneath
**Lesson:** Two people can be devoted parents and barely married. *(outline, key point 1)*
1. Tell me about a stretch with young kids when you were good parents but barely a couple. When did you notice?
2. Tell me about a small habit that kept you two connected through the kid years. How did it start?
3. Tell me about a night after the kids were asleep that reminded you there was still something between you.

## Chapter 21: Trust After Betrayal
**Lesson:** Trust is rebuilt in days and actions, not in conversations; the drift that comes before betrayal is where a man can still choose. *(outline, key points 2 and 4)*
1. Tell me about a time you drifted toward someone you shouldn't have. What did you do when you noticed?
2. If you ever broke her trust, with money, a secret, or someone else, walk me through the day it came out.
3. If your trust was broken, tell me about the day you decided whether to stay or go.

## Chapter 22: Fear Is Not Commitment
**Lesson:** There is a form of staying that is not virtue; it is fear wearing a virtue costume. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about a time you stayed mainly because leaving scared you. What were you actually afraid of?
2. Walk me through the night you came closest to leaving, or asking for a divorce. What happened?
3. If you divorced, tell me about the moment you knew it was over. What did it cost?

## Chapter 23: Repair Quickly, Love Deliberately
**Lesson:** A marriage's strength is how quickly and honestly it repairs: real accountability, not apology theater. *(outline, premise and takeaway)*
1. Tell me about an apology you made that made things worse. What did you say, exactly?
2. Tell me about a time you owned your part without explaining it away. What shifted after?
3. Tell me about a time you let a rupture sit too long. What did waiting cost you?

## Chapter 24: The Marriage You Build Every Day
**Lesson:** A great marriage is compounded; the absence of small daily acts is the slow decline nobody decided on. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about a small daily habit you dropped, and when you noticed what dropping it cost.
2. Tell me about something small you've done for her for years. What has it turned into?
3. Tell me about a slow slide in your marriage that nobody decided on. When did you finally see it?

## Chapter 25: Become Easier to Love
**Lesson:** A man can be steady, reliable and good at the work of the marriage and still be grim company. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about a time you were doing everything right and she still seemed worn out by you.
2. Tell me about a stretch when you were no fun to be around at home. How did you find out?
3. Tell me about a time you set out to just enjoy her company. What changed?

## Chapter 26: Friendship Is the Hidden Engine
**Lesson:** The friendship at the center of a marriage is the most underprotected thing in it, and a husband with no friends of his own makes her carry all of him. *(outline, premise and key point 2)*
1. Tell me about when you realized she was your only real friend. What tipped you off?
2. Tell me about a time you two had fun together like friends again. What were you doing?
3. Tell me about getting a real friendship back with another guy. What did it change at home?

## Chapter 27: Growing Old Without Growing Apart
**Lesson:** Couples become strangers by increments; the long game is re-choosing each other through the real transitions, knowing neither of you is promised the decades. *(outline, key points 1, 3 and 4)*
1. Tell me about a big change, kids leaving, retirement, a health scare, that pulled you two apart.
2. Tell me about something you learned about her after twenty-plus years. How did you find out?
3. Tell me about a scare that made you stop putting things off with her. What did you do after?

## Chapter 28: What They Inherit
**Lesson:** "My kids aren't learning what I tell them about love. They're learning what they watch me do." *(outline, reader ah-ha)*
1. Tell me about a time you saw your own temper or silence show up in one of your kids.
2. Tell me about a time your kids watched you two fight. What did they see, and what came after?
3. Tell me about a time you saw your grown kid handle love the way you'd learned to. What was it?

## Conclusion: The Blueprint
**Lesson:** He didn't have a blueprint handed to him, so he became one. *(outline, premise)*
1. Tell me about a recent moment you handled differently than you would have ten years ago.
2. Tell me about a night lately when you went to bed at peace with how you'd been as a husband.

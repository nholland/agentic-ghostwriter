A tense evening at home can start with how you walked in the door, and you're usually the last one to see it. The mood you bring home reaches everyone, one person at a time, and it often ends with someone who had no part in it.

You've had the kind of day you can feel in your shoulders. You come through the door still holding it. She says something ordinary, it lands wrong, and you snap. Nothing dramatic, just edges. The tone she takes into the kitchen, where the kids already are, isn't the one she started the day with. The oldest gets short with the youngest. The youngest, out of people to be short with, kicks the dog. The dog did nothing. Nobody in that house chose any of it. You came home with what the day gave you, and the house took it from there. Because the worst of it happens two rooms away, you may never connect it to the minute you walked in.

Marcus Aurelius ruled the Roman Empire for nearly two decades, through plague and war, and kept a private journal the whole time. In it, he wrote about the part of a man that decides what a moment means. "The ruling Reason it is that can arouse and deflect itself, make itself whatever it will, and invest everything that befalls with such a semblance as it wills." (*Meditations* 6.8, Haines translation.) In plain English, the part of you that decides can wind itself up or calm itself down, and it says what your day means. The Stoics called it the *hegemonikon*, the ruling faculty: not the part that reacts, but the part that governs. Everyone in the house has one of their own. Her mood is hers, and the kids' moods are theirs. What you bring through the door belongs to you.

The practical tool is a backward trace, and it works best once the house is quiet. When an evening has gone sideways, you can walk it back one step at a time. When did the room turn, who was in it, and where had that person's mood come from? Some nights the answer is her long day, or a kid's rough one at school, and it got to you before you noticed. Some nights the answer is you at the door, still holding the day in your shoulders. Over a few evenings, the trace shows you how far your arrival reaches. Your ruling faculty then has something specific to govern, and it's the minute you walk in.

The dog's fine, but he had no business being part of your commute.

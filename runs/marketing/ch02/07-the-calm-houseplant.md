# You Read Some Stoicism and Went Behind Glass

**Chapter source:** Ch2: Stop Outsourcing Your Peace (refined.md, "The corrective")   **Subject lines:** 1. Feeling as bad as she does won't help her 2. The first ten seconds after she tells you something hard 3. What she sees when you nod through her awful week
**Status:** Draft, awaiting author approval. Not posted.

---

If you've read a little Stoicism, there's a decent chance you've tried the version where you turn into a very calm houseplant. She tells you about her awful week and you nod slowly, like she's walking you through a quarterly report you didn't ask to see. You're calm, sure. You're also no help to her at all, and she can tell. Somewhere between the reading and the couch, a lot of us decided steady meant watching her from behind glass.

The opposite mistake looks a lot more loving. She's fried, so you get fried with her. You sigh in all the right places and get angrier at her boss than she is. By nine o'clock you're both at the kitchen table feeling terrible. That feels like being on her side. In practice, two people in a low is a lower low. She brought you one bad week. Now she has her week plus your reaction to it, and your reaction is one more thing she has to manage. It works the same in the other direction. When you're the one having the awful week, what helps is someone in the room who stays kind and level while you're low.

The Stoics were Greek and Roman philosophers from about two thousand years ago. They put a lot of weight on the part of you that decides what a moment means and what you do about it. Keeping that part in charge was most of the work, and it was never a way to care less. Say she's sick, the miserable kind, and you walk in with soup. You're warm. You make one small, quiet joke that lightens the room without brushing off how bad she feels. You're not swept into her misery, and you're not performing cool from across the room either. You're just steady, and that steadiness turns out to be the thing you brought her, along with the soup.

The next time she tells you something hard, a lot gets decided in the first ten seconds. In the nodding version, your eyes stay where they were and you wait for her to finish. The warm version is mostly small physical stuff. You turn around from the counter and put the phone face down. You sit close enough that your knee touches hers. Then you say one thing back that proves you caught the specific part. "Wait, the same person who approved it is now blaming you for it?" The joke waits until she's had a while to be mad out loud, and it's aimed at the situation or at you. It takes about ten seconds and no speeches.

The soup can come from a can.

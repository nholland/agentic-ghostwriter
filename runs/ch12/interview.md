### What goes into romance

> "You have to first understand the five love languages of your partner: how do
> they give love and how do they receive love? When you get love in a way that
> doesn't resonate with you, a natural response is to overlook it or basically
> not see the value. If someone throws it in your face or even does something
> terrible like downplay it, that's quite insulting because the person is putting
> in effort. You just might not receive it that way."

> "On the reverse side I really love the golden rule, which is that whenever you
> put effort in, perhaps it's in a way that your partner doesn't always
> appreciate."

### His definition of romance

> "I think first we have to get to a definition of romance and it's **trying to
> win favor with your partner. It's not about you, it's about them** and a
> critical part is being mindful of it. You have to study the person to know what
> they like. You have to communicate with the person to know what they like. You
> have to try to incorporate things like **a little bit of mystery, a little bit
> of surprise, and a little bit of intrigue.**"

### Where the decay starts

> "The reality is that when you're single and alone, this feels worth it but when
> you're in a marriage it starts to fade to the back and this is where the decay
> begins in the marriage. If a partner doesn't feel wanted, valued, loved, and
> cherished, then this is where cracks begin. Resentment begins to enter the

### The bare minimum

> "It's Valentine's Day and after work you swing by Walgreens and you buy a card

> "The trouble is that romance is as much an art as it is a science and it's like

**AUTHOR RULING — the Walgreens scene is excluded from the book.** His words:

## Corrections and contradictions — what round 1 changed

### 1. The outline's core mechanism is not what he actually believes

The outline says: *don't wait to feel romantic; practice until the feeling
follows* (**askesis**, feeling-follows-practice).

What he described is a different mechanism entirely: **romance is the first line
item cut when life gets expensive.** "The more life takes a toll on you, the less
energy you have to invest in your partner." That is not a man waiting for a
feeling. That is a man whose budget is over-committed, spending his effort
somewhere it shows a faster return.

These produce different chapters. Feeling-follows-practice tells him to act
anyway. Energy-allocation tells him romance is competing with work, kids and
exhaustion, and losing — which means the intervention is about priority and cost,
not about mood. His grandfather's line ("put your wife first") is the
energy-allocation answer, not the askesis answer.

**Proposed outline revision — NOT APPLIED. This house does not write in the book
repo. See Outline Revision Proposal below.**

### 2. The golden rule and the five love languages contradict each other

He said both, one sentence apart:

- "You have to first understand the five love languages of your partner: how do
  they give love and how do they receive love?" → *give love in her currency.*
- "On the reverse side I really love the golden rule ... whenever you put effort
  in, perhaps it's in a way that your partner doesn't always appreciate." → *give
  love in yours, and she should credit the effort.*

The golden rule is the exact failure mode he had just diagnosed one paragraph
earlier — a man putting in real effort in his own language and it not landing.
He also already named the receiving side of it as the sin: downplaying a partner's
effort is "quite insulting because the person is putting in effort."

Held for round 2 (question #2). This is the chapter's sharpest available idea and
it should not be resolved by a desk on his behalf (Rule 9).

### 3. He asked for research; the outline says the research burden is low

> "At a high level I think we should do some research here because I'm guessing
> there is a lot."

The outline rates this chapter **"Research burden: Low — Stoic discipline and
practice; this is primarily application and scenario."** He is overriding that.
The Researcher desk should be dispatched with a real brief, not a thin one.

**Flag for the Researcher, not a finding of fact:** the five love languages is a
popular-psychology construct whose empirical standing is contested in the
relationship-science literature. The author is currently using it as a load-
bearing pillar. The Researcher must establish where it actually stands before the
Ghostwriter builds on it — and if it does not hold, the underlying idea (partners
differ in how they give and receive care) may need a different support. Do not
assert either way without a citation concept.

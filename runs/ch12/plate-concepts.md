## A. The list with an empty day column

*(the current concept, rebuilt to stand alone)*

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** everything in his week that had a day
attached got done, and the one thing that is for his wife has no day beside it,
which is why it did not happen.

**Carrier:** a second column. The list is ruled the same all the way down, but
the right column, headed *the day it lands*, has a solid mark on every line and
one empty dashed box on the last. The argument is the gap in one column of an
otherwise complete form. The dashed box is the Lesson drawn rather than written:
it is a box waiting to be filled in, and it matches the empty *this protects
\_\_\_\_\_\_* box in the Ch10 plate.

**From the chapter:** "it's never the mortgage or the kid's game at eight on a
Saturday. Those shout. They have a day attached and other people watching." The
heading is Marcus's correction as the chapter states it: "Do the necessary things
instead. That list is harder to write." The last entry is the chapter's own
practice, "whether anything you did was for her alone."

**Copy it needs (10 pieces):** title; subtitle *A week gets done in the order of
what has a day attached.*; two column heads (*THE WEEK'S NECESSARY THINGS*, *THE
DAY IT LANDS*); five list entries; one gloss (*the line left blank*); closing
line *The ones with a day attached got done. Give this one a day too.* This is
the wordiest of the three, and the five entries are the reason.

**What changed from the current plate:** the day column is new and is what makes
it legible cold, because it names the missing quantity instead of leaving a mark
absent for an unstated reason. The lowercase gloss *her* under a blank line is
gone, replaced by a written entry in the same weight as the others. The
language pair (*said in your language* / *heard in hers*) is cut; it was a second
argument riding on the first and it is what the author found confusing.

**Risk:** a skeptic reads it as a scheduling tip, calendar your wife, and the
chapter's second half (the form care arrives in) is absent entirely.

## B. The heavy effort that stops short

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** the effort he puts the most weight
behind is the one that does not reach her, and the one he never thinks of is the
one that gets through.

**Carrier:** weight against reach, across one vertical line. A thick black bar,
the heaviest mark on the page, runs from the left and stops well short of the
rule marked *what she reads as care*. A thin hairline, the lightest mark on the
page, crosses it and lands. The inversion is the whole argument and it works
before any label is read: big stops, small arrives.

**From the chapter:** "your effort can be real and still land flat. You put your
back into something in your own language, she thanks you and means it, and it's
flat." And "The language I'm worst at is the one she reads best."

**Copy it needs (7 pieces):** title; subtitle *Real effort, made in your own
shape, can still land flat.*; one label on the rule (*WHAT SHE READS AS CARE*);
two bar labels (*THE LANGUAGE YOU ARE BEST AT*, *THE LANGUAGE YOU ARE WORST AT*);
one gloss each (*the one you reach for without thinking*, *the one that never
occurs to you*); closing line *The shape you are worst at is usually the one she
reads best.* Leanest of the three.

**Risk:** the skeptic reads it as "big gestures are bad, small gestures are
good," which is not the chapter's claim; the chapter's claim is about form, not
size. It also does not draw the title. Nothing here says *no deadline*, so the
plate argues the second half of the chapter under the first half's name.

## C. Two rows of weeks, one that quietly stops

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** the demands with a day attached ran
unbroken through every week, the one thing for his wife stopped early, and
nothing anywhere marked the stopping.

**Carrier:** two rows of weeks running left to right from the same starting rule.
The top row is marked in every week. The bottom row is marked for a few weeks and
then goes empty for most of the page. The argument is the length of the blank
stretch against the row above it, and the reason a man never noticed is drawn:
there is no mark for stopping, only the absence of marks. The Lesson is one drawn
object, a single open square at the far right of the lower row, the only outlined
mark on the page, sitting in this week.

**From the chapter:** "Close to five months, and I hadn't felt one of them go
by." And "Nothing in a week tells you how long it's been." The open square at the
end is "a muscle that puts her there is one you can start using tonight."

**Copy it needs (8 pieces):** title; subtitle *Nothing in a week tells you how
long it has been.*; two row labels (*THE THINGS THAT SHOUT*, *THE ONE THING FOR
HER*); one gloss each (*a day attached, and somebody waiting*, *no day, and
nobody waiting*); one gloss over the gap (*the stretch you never felt go by*);
one gloss at the open square (*tonight, in the shape she reads as care*); closing
line *It is a thing you stopped doing. Starting again asks nobody's forgiveness.*

**Risk:** the rows can be read as a scorecard of how often he performs, which
turns recognition into accusation, and the open square could read as one more
week rather than as the repair. The chapter's second half survives only as a
six-word gloss, which is thin.

## Notes for whoever draws the winner

  in C the gap gloss sits close under *THE ONE THING FOR HER* and wants moving;

  fill*, which is the sense Ch10 uses.

  badly (two arguments, two registers) but C and B share a horizontal axis and
  could in principle merge. I would rather the Panel pick one than approve a
  merge, because the merge is how the current plate got confusing.

# Gordon et al. (2012) — Feeling Appreciated

The loop the paper describes: feeling appreciated by your partner makes you
more appreciative of them; being more appreciative makes you more responsive
to what they need; being more responsive is associated with more commitment
and with still being together later. It runs the other way too, which is what
makes it a loop rather than a one-directional finding: your appreciation
feeds her sense of being appreciated.

# Why It Matters for Ch12

It is the evidence under the author's first link. "If a partner doesn't feel
wanted, valued, loved, and cherished, then this is where cracks begin" is a
strong claim, and this is the paper that lets a version of it be made honestly:
felt appreciation is not a nice extra, it is bound up with responsiveness,
commitment and whether the relationship lasts. It also supplies the loop the
author gestured at from the receiving side — that downplaying a partner's
effort is corrosive, because being appreciated is what makes a person
appreciative.

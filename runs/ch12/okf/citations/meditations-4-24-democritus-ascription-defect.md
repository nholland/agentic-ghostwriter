# The Defect

Printed in the refined chapter:

> In 4.24 he picks up an old line he credits to the philosopher Democritus: if
> you want to be untroubled, do few things.

Long's IV.24, read 2026-09-16:

> Occupy thyself with few things, says the philosopher, if thou wouldst be
> tranquil.

"He credits to the philosopher Democritus" is a claim about what Marcus did.
In this edition Marcus credited "the philosopher," unnamed. The chapter's
second half — Marcus correcting the maxim to "do the necessary things" — is
accurate and survives untouched.

# Why It Matters Beyond This Sentence

06-sources.md exists because nine citation defects reached compiled prose and
six were printed. Its stated cause is that "search fails confidently." This
one is the same failure with a different surface: no search invented the
Democritus name, because the name really is in the scholarship and in other
translations. What the chapter did was silently import an editorial gloss into
the house edition and then report it as Marcus's own act. That is defect #10's
pattern (filed as one thing, printed as another) in a new form, and it is
worth a line in quality/citation-defects.md when this chapter is migrated.

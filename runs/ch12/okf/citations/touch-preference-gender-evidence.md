# The Flag

Interview material, round 3: *"every man loves touch as a love language."*

01-voice.md, Never Do: *"Never assert a universal claim about all men ('every
man is built this way,' 'all men do this,' 'no man wants that').... A flat
universal is one counterexample away from a reader dismissing the whole
claim."* This is that, exactly.

It is also the kind of sentence that costs the chapter its most valuable
reader. 02-audience.md gives Eric a well-calibrated detector; a man whose own
wife initiates touch and who himself would rather be talked to will stop
reading at that sentence.

# The Repair, Ranked

1. Cut the general claim and keep his instance. "I lay my head in her lap
   while we watch TV and she rubs my head" does the whole job and is
   unfalsifiable because it is his.
2. Scope it: "most men," "a lot of men."
3. If the author wants the population claim stated, the honest form is that
   surveys find men more likely than women to name touch first — attributed as
   a survey, with no number.

# Bennett (1979) — The Platinum Rule

Bennett's argument, in a communication-studies chapter, is that the Golden
Rule contains a hidden assumption: that other people are essentially like you,
and so treating them as you would want to be treated will land. Where that
assumption is false, the Golden Rule produces sympathy (imagining how *you*
would feel in their position) rather than empathy (imagining the world from
*their* position). His replacement, which he names the platinum rule, is to do
unto others as they would have done unto them.

# Status

**Verifiable, not verified.** Origin, year, venue and argument corroborated
across independent listings. The exact printed wording of the rule is not
confirmed and may not be quoted.

# Why It Matters for Ch12

The chapter's central claim (author's ruling, round 2) is the platinum rule
applied to marriage. Bennett's framing is a better fit than Alessandra's
business version for two reasons: it is the origin, and its whole point is the
failure mode the author independently described — a man putting in real effort
in his own currency and it not landing, because he assumed she wants what he
would want.

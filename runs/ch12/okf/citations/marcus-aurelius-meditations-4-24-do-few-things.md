# Meditations IV.24 — Do Few Things

The sense of the passage, stated in this file's own words and NOT as a
quotation: Democritus advises doing few things in order to be untroubled.
Marcus improves on it — do the things that are necessary, and what reason
requires of a creature made for society, and no more. Cutting the unnecessary
buys both tranquillity and doing the thing well.

# Why It Fits Ch12

The chapter's mechanism, per the interview, is energy allocation: when life
gets expensive, romance is the first line item cut. The Stoic answer is not
"work harder," which is the answer a tired man will refuse. It is that the
cutting is going to happen either way, and the only real question is whether
he decides what gets cut or lets the week decide. That is Marcus correcting
Democritus: not fewer things for their own sake, the necessary things.

It also lands the grandfather's line in Stoic terms. "Put your wife first" is
a statement about ordering under scarcity, which is what this passage is about.

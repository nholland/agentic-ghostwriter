# Chapman (1992) — The Five Love Languages

The claim: each person has one primary love language among five (words of
affirmation, quality time, receiving gifts, acts of service, physical touch);
partners who identify and speak each other's language have better
relationships.

The status: a bestselling trade book built from a pastoral counselling
practice. It is not a research finding and was never validated before it
became one of the most widely repeated ideas about relationships in print.

# Status

**Unverified — a gap, and the gap is the point.** The book exists; the
evidence behind its central claims does not. Treat as a popular construct, not
a citable model.

# What Survives, and What Does Not

**Does not survive:** one primary language per person; five distinct
categories; matching languages predicting relationship quality.

**Survives, and is the thing the author actually means:** people differ in
which expressions of care register most strongly for them, and care delivered
in a form a partner does not read as care can fail to land even when the
effort behind it was real. That idea has genuine support under a different
name — perceived partner responsiveness, and felt appreciation. See
`runs/ch12/research.md`, assignment 1.

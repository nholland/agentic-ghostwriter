# Selterman, Garcia & Tsapelas (2019) — Infidelity Motivations

495 people who reported having been unfaithful were asked why. The motives
sort into eight distinct factors. Neglect is one of them; so are anger, lack
of love, low commitment, sexual desire, desire for variety, situational
factors and self-esteem.

# Status

**Verifiable, not verified.** Abstract-level. Restricted to non-causal use.

# Why It Matters for Ch12, and Where It Stops

The author's chain ends "resentment begins to enter the relationship and
infidelity begins to enter." This is the nearest real evidence and it will not
carry that weight. What it supports: *neglect is one of several things people
name when they explain their own infidelity.* What it does not support: that
neglect produces infidelity, that it is the main route, or that a man who lets
romance lapse is on a track toward it.

The bundle's own
[Infidelity Motivation — Gendered Split](/citations/infidelity-motivation-gendered-split.md)
concept already carries the complicating finding that argues directly against
a deterministic reading: a large share of people who have affairs rate their
marriage happy. Whatever Ch12 says about infidelity has to survive that.

# Impett, Park & Muise (2024) — Love Languages Evaluated

Chapman's five love languages rests on three assumptions. This review tests
each against the published evidence.

1. **Everyone has one primary love language.** Not supported. Study after
   study finds people endorse all five as meaningful ways to give and receive
   love. The idea of a single dominant channel does not survive measurement.
2. **There are five, and they are distinct.** Not supported. The five
   categories overlap substantially, which undercuts the claim that they are
   separate languages at all. The original typology also came from a narrow
   sample (white, religious, mixed-gender, traditional couples) and leaves out
   things that matter to more egalitarian couples, such as supporting a
   partner's goals outside the relationship.
3. **Speaking your partner's language produces a better relationship.** Not
   supported. No consistent association has been found between satisfaction
   and either matching or mismatching. All expressions of love are associated
   with higher satisfaction, regardless of anyone's self-reported primary
   language.

What the authors point at instead: **perceived partner responsiveness** —
being attuned to what a partner actually needs and feels — and a balanced-diet
metaphor in place of the language metaphor, in which a relationship needs a
full range of nutrients rather than one.

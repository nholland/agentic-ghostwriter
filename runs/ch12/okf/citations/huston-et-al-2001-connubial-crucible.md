# What It Says

Couples were followed from the newlywed year for thirteen years. Differences
in how intense the newlywed romance was, and how much negative feeling the
couple expressed, predicted both who was happy at thirteen years and how long
the marriages that ended lasted first. The mechanism that did the predicting
was the abatement of love and the decline in overt affection — not increasing
negativity.

# Why It Matters To This Chapter, And Where It Must Not Be Overstated

It is the strongest available answer to the reader's silent objection: "we
don't fight, so we're fine." This chapter is in a book whose Part I and II are
largely about conflict, and this is the finding that says the quiet erosion is
the more dangerous mode. That is the chapter's premise, and until round 2 it
had no evidence at all.

It is also the finding most likely to be overclaimed. See the reach caution in
verification_note. The honest sentence is about what forecasts trouble, not
about what causes divorce.

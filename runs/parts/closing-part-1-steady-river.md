# Closing page, Part I: The Steady River (draft, two versions)

## Version A

*The river has been tested, and it held. A stone hits, and it slows before choosing its way. It feels every storm without becoming one. When the bank pushes back, it bends without arguing. Rain swells it, and it doesn't flood the fields. Blocked, it doesn't go still. It presses until the way opens. The canyon is the proof, cut one ordinary day at a time.*

## Version B

*The river is you, steady under pressure. When something hits, you pause before you answer. You feel someone else's mood without making it yours. When criticism lands, you hear it instead of defending yourself. Anger still rises, and you choose what happens next. In a hard conversation, you don't go quiet. You say the true thing. A canyon is cut one ordinary day at a time.*

**Version A, sentence by sentence**

1. "The river has been tested, and it held." Turn. Names the page as the river *after* Part I's pressure (Part I promise: governs himself under pressure).
2. "A stone hits, and it slows before choosing its way." The pause before reacting, Ch1 (the three-second window, *prohairesis*). The stone is the trigger; "slows before choosing" is the gap.
3. "It feels every storm without becoming one." Not taking on her weather, Ch2. "Feels" keeps Ch2's line that feeling her mood is connection; "without becoming one" is the line that becoming it is the problem.
4. "When the bank pushes back, it bends without arguing." Hearing criticism without defending, Ch3. The bank pushing back is the criticism; bending is letting it change the course. Risk: "bends" could read as giving in, which Ch3 warns against; sentences 6-7 are the counterweight.
5. "Rain swells it, and it doesn't flood the fields." Anger as a choice, Ch4. The swell is Seneca's involuntary first motion; not flooding is the second stage he governs. The fields carry the cost (what a flood takes) without the nail image, which would be a second metaphor and a callback.
6-7. "Blocked, it doesn't go still. It presses until the way opens." Fighting without shrinking, Ch5. Going still is calling the fight over once it goes quiet; pressing until the way opens is coming back and saying the true thing (*parrhesia*).
8. "The canyon is the proof, cut one ordinary day at a time." Durable fact. The one permitted echo of the opening's last line ("one ordinary day at a time"), turned from promise (the river *is patient enough* to cut a canyon) to result (the canyon is there).

**Version B, sentence by sentence**

1. "The river is you, steady under pressure." Turn. Names the element as the man, as Parts IV and V do, and states the Part I promise in its own words.
2. "When something hits, you pause before you answer." Ch1, the pause.
3. "You feel someone else's mood without making it yours." Ch2. "Someone else's" instead of "her" to keep marriage vocabulary off the page; the reader makes the connection.
4. "When criticism lands, you hear it instead of defending yourself." Ch3.
5. "Anger still rises, and you choose what happens next." Ch4. "Still rises" keeps Ch4's point that the first motion isn't the failure; the choice is what follows.
6-7. "In a hard conversation, you don't go quiet. You say the true thing." Ch5. "Say the true thing" is Ch5's own plain phrase. "Hard conversation" stands in for "fight", so no domestic scene word was needed; none is used.
8. "A canyon is cut one ordinary day at a time." Durable fact and the echo: these five skills are built by repetition, the way the opening said the river works.

# What You Hold Now

It started with the trash on Tuesday night. You'd hauled it for years without wanting thanks, and one offhand comment could still undo all of it. She was folding. You were sitting. Every job from the bucket labeled "whatever needs doing" vanished, and the tally in your head wanted to be read out loud.

So you stopped reading it. You said what you saw: "I see you're working. I appreciate it." And the honest question: am I actually being lazy right now, or are we just out of rhythm?

Then you looked at what the tally was made of. The router, the tournament weekend, the stories with a name. The small things, hers and yours, never made the count, so it always told you that you were ahead. My grandfather's rule still holds: give 60 percent, expect 40.

Dropping the count didn't mean pretending nothing was unfair. What feels sudden almost never is. When she names an imbalance, you check whether it's true first. When it's yours, you pause, look at the work together, and make one request. You can thank her for what she carries and still ask for help with what you can't keep carrying.

A request has to be said out loud, and that's where the agreeable husband gets stuck. Every "sounds good" you didn't mean was a deal she never agreed to, and a list nobody saw but you. Epictetus told his students to be picky with their words. Hiding things from your wife was never what he meant.

That led to the lines you hold. A boundary protects something you can name. A preference is something you want, and it folds when somebody leans on it hard enough. Naming the line is the easy half. On the night holding it makes you the bad guy in your own house, you hold it anyway.

A line is something you decided. Most of what you live with, you never decided, so you learned to sort it. Some of it needs a conversation. Some of it you live through together, still letting the kids ask you a question without getting snapped at. Some of it you leave alone, without reminding her what you put up with. None of it needs one more argument in your head that you aren't willing to have with her.

You still carry the weight, and none of it lives only in your head anymore.

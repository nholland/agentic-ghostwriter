# Warm on Purpose

*(Title is a suggestion.)*

It started with a question you couldn't answer. She asked, without any anger, when you last took her on a date, and you counted back through months you never felt go by. Nothing in a week tells you how long it's been. So you started doing it on purpose: the dishes before she names them, the pastry she never stops for, a card planned a week out instead of grabbed at Walgreens.

Effort only counts in a form she can read, though, and yours came out in your own language. You reached for the Italian restaurant she loved at twenty-six and couldn't name one thing she'd be excited about this Saturday. So you asked. There was a garden along the back fence she'd been mentioning for months, and you'd been hearing her the way you hear the radio. Asking was useful. Remembering the answer on Tuesday is what she noticed.

Once you were paying attention, you saw more than her plans. You saw what you'd stopped valuing. I've told my wife we live like hoarders, and the word I much prefer is thrifty. It's the same trait either way, and what I'm paying attention to picks the word. My kids complain about dinner until it's their turn to cook. The woman you compare your wife to is someone you've never lived with, so she's just a dream.

That's what keeps you kind when a Saturday turns sharp. A line about the things that need doing is more a sign of how stressed she is than a ruling on you. You roll up your sleeves before you say a word, and you let the rest go, because it takes two to make a fight.

Each step needed the one before it. Effort that isn't meant for who she is now can be real, and she still won't read it as love. Attention that never turns into appreciation leaves you noticing what bothers you about her faster than her kindness. And it's hard to stay patient with a woman you've stopped enjoying. None of it waited for her to earn it.

You can name the last romantic thing you did for her, you know where the first bed is going, you have a kinder word for what she saves, and when the next zing comes, your hands already know what to do.

## Draft Notes

**Finding first: the recap overlaps Chapter 16, and the overlap is narrower than it might look.** Chapter 16 already summarizes Chapters 12-15 in one sentence ("You've seen what it looks like in the last four chapters: effort in her language, attention when you're together, noticing what you already have, kindness when she's sharp."). It also does the arc's payoff (River, Oak, the house, flow, "she is your number one"). Read straight after Chapter 16, a recap that listed the four moves again would just repeat that sentence. The recap does two things Chapter 16 doesn't: (1) it lays the four chapters out in sequence and shows why each one needs the one before it (the fifth paragraph). Chapter 16 lists them side by side. (2) It brings back the concrete scenes (five months, the garden, hoarder and thrifty, the Saturday zing), which Chapter 16 never recalls. That is enough to justify the page, but it is thin. If the author feels the recap reads as a second ending right after Chapter 16's close, the other placement to try is before Chapter 16, or at the front of Part IV as a look back. Moving it is his call, and I have not drafted that version.

The recap never names River, Oak, flow, the house, home, investing, or "number one", so it doesn't redo Chapter 16's job. It contains no bank, account, deposit, credit or withdrawal words, and no sun, night or winter lines. I avoided "tonight" too, even though Chapters 12 and 13 both end on it.

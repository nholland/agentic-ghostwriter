## 1. Ranked findings

**1. Ch12 — off the mark.** *Takes away now:* three full bars and one short one; romance is lower than his other habits. *Should take away:* romance faded because it is the one thing with no deadline and nobody watching, and it returns only by being done, in the form she reads as care. *Diagnosis:* wrong mechanism named, plus decorative. Four faults: the title is not the distillation's Mechanism; WALKING/RUNNING/READING are three identical black rectangles so the entire argument sits in the caption; the romance bar has three segments and two labels (the pale grey block is unexplained); and it is the only plate carrying a source line (*After Epictetus, Discourses 2.18*). The chapter's second half — care arriving in her language, the shape he is worst at being usually hers — is absent, and that is what the ah-ha depends on.

**2. Ch07 — off the mark.** *Now:* most of marriage is small and uncountable. *Should:* the tally could only count his own visible half, so it was always going to say he is ahead. *Diagnosis:* right family, wrong claim. It argues volume, never ownership; the chapter's own "arithmetic done on one page of a two-page ledger" is the mechanism and is not in the picture. Titled by a framework, not the Mechanism. `runs/ch07/plate-notes.md` flags both and offers the replacement — take its option 1. Skeptic's counter-read: the dot field can be read as *his* uncounted contributions, which inverts the chapter.

**3. Ch05 — right mechanism, drawn unclearly, and contradicts Ch04.** Ch04's plate says in its own caption *"The nail comes out. The hole stays where it was."* Ch05 shows a nail out and **no hole** — only a faint dashed vertical rule. Two adjacent plates make opposite promises with the same object. The removed nail still renders with a point and reads as driving inward, despite `runs/ch05/plate-notes.md` recording that fix. Large dead space above the board; PARRHESIA set in a display register nothing else uses.

**4. Ch01 — right mechanism drawn as a chart, on the book's first plate.** The half-second/two-and-a-half split is genuinely Ch1's prose, so this is a reading problem, not a false claim. The heaviest mark is the smallest element and its label fights the title; nothing shows *what fills* the gap, which is the chapter's subject; two measured quantities set a quantitative register the set does not keep. Paul (the reader in trouble) hears "you have half a second to fix yourself" — accusation, not recognition.

**5. Ch02 — labels do the work the drawing should.** The argument is solid-versus-dashed on one thin rule, the lightest possible distinction carrying the heaviest idea. "THE MIRROR" labels a line segment; a mirror is not a line. Five italic caption lines plus untranslated *hegemonikon*, one of `02-audience.md`'s introduce-carefully terms.

**6. Ch04 — the drawing contradicts its own caption.** Sub-caption says "One fence post, three times." Rows one and two are perfect (five nails, five holes in the same places). Row three shows ten holes with no second row of nails. `runs/ch04/plate-notes.md` confirms the doubling is deliberate; the plate never says so, so it reads as an error.

**7. Ch08 — diagnosis only, one side undefined.** The right pan is empty and unlabelled, so the central relationship of a balance has no second term. The chapter's whole corrective (two tools, two directions) is absent. "Relitigating" is the least plain word on any plate.

**8. Ch06 — two plates on one page.** The top half (dashed invisible week vs solid visible moment) is a real visual argument. The bottom two-column panel is a second plate and carries the set's only quotation, and the divider splits attention exactly where the plate should land.

## 2. Edit suggestions, every chapter plate

**Ch01 — EDIT.** *Carrier: what sits inside the window, not how long it is.* Cut both time labels and the bracket/THREE SECONDS measure. Keep the span with two end labels only (*she says something* / *the words are already out*). Add two short vertical strokes rising from the span at the same point near the left end: one solid labelled *chosen*, one dashed labelled *inherited*. Relabel the dark block from THE WINDOW to **THE GAP** (the Mechanism name). Reword the closing line off measurement: *"The part you choose is the small one. It is still the one that decides."*

**Ch02 — EDIT.** *Carrier: the break in the line.* Redraw row two with a visible white gap between *her weather* and *your own weather*, with the governing part as a short upright stroke standing in that gap, not a dashed continuation. Relabel THE MIRROR to **YOU, MATCHING IT** — or, if the mirror is kept, draw row one as her weather striking a vertical rule and emerging unchanged. Cut the *hegemonikon* gloss; keep THE GOVERNING PART. Cut one of the three closing lines (keep *"The day is still hers. What comes through the door is yours."*).

**Ch03 — KEEP AS IS.** *Carrier: the four bars darkening toward full.* Second-best plate in the book; the bar behaves like a door actually closing and the rows are the chapter's own sequence. No change.

**Ch04 — EDIT, one change.** *Carrier: row two, nail gone, hole still there.* Either set the five original holes solid and the five later ones light grey, relabelling row three *"More since. She stopped counting which was which."*; **or** simply change the sub-caption from "One fence post, three times" to **"One fence post, over the years."** The second is one line of copy and fixes it completely. Everything else keeps.

**Ch05 — EDIT, structural.** *Carrier: the one empty hole with the nail beside it.* Redraw the removed nail as flat-headed, lying horizontally, head left, **blunt shaft, no point**, clearly at rest. Replace the dashed vertical rule with **a small filled dark circle in the board's face, matching Ch04's hole glyph exactly** — this single change repairs the contradiction, because the reader then sees *the nail came out, the hole is still there*, which is what both chapters say. Relabel *the one you went back to* → **the one you went back and said**. Raise the board to kill the dead space and pull the loose nail close to its hole so the two read as one event. Cut the display-size PARRHESIA; fold it into the closing line.

**Ch06 — EDIT, cut half.** *Carrier: the dashed empty bar beside the solid black one.* Cut the entire lower two-column panel including the quoted script. Add one caption under the remaining pair: *"The week is real. Only tonight is visible."* Keep the last two lines as the closing block — they are the mechanism.

**Ch07 — REPLACE CONCEPT.** *Carrier: two columns over one shared field.* Retitle **THE PRIVATE TALLY**. Replace the drawing with the Designer's own option 1: two ruled columns standing on one field of small dots — left headed *what you counted* with three legible entries written in, right ruled identically and **completely empty**, headed *what she counted*; under both, the shared dot field, captioned *"the part neither column reached."* Keep the subtitle *"The ledger can only count what it can see."* Add: *"Half the data, and it always said you were ahead."* The big-rock/small-rock version belongs to the framework, not this chapter.

**Ch08 — EDIT.** *Carrier: the dotted mass on the low pan versus the single dot above it.* Label the empty pan: *"everything you told yourself was fine."* Relabel *Relitigating the last one...* → **"Arguing about the last one misses where the weight came from."** Add the corrective as one line along the loaded pan's edge: *"Name each one as it lands, and nothing has to tip."* Keep the scale — it is the strongest physical object in the set.

**Ch09 — EDIT, trim.** *Carrier: the long dashed return arrow that comes back empty.* Cut the three short dashed stubs right of *Respect / Warmth / Being asked about*; they duplicate the return arrow and read as unfinished drawing. Cut one closing line — keep *"Say it plainly and kindly, while the list is still short."* Keep the ruled list with *a list only you are keeping*; it is the best mark on the plate and connects to Ch11's box.

**Ch10 — KEEP AS IS.** *Carrier: the empty "this protects ______" box under the folded line, against the solid footing under the held one.* **This is the best plate in the book and should be the model for the rest**: one object drawn twice under the same pressure, the difference between the two drawings *is* the argument, and the corrective is a visible physical thing rather than a caption.

**Ch11 — EDIT, small.** *Carrier: the full box beside the empty one.* Cut either the displayed FORTY TIMES / ZERO pair or the subtitle — the same two figures currently appear twice, in the two largest type sizes. Recommend keeping the subtitle. Retitle to **THE CONVERSATION SHE'S NEVER HEARD**, matching `chapters/ch11/distillation.md` word for word. Cut one of the three closing lines ("Each pass makes the next one more expensive" and "Raise it now and you are also defending the delay" say the same thing). The three-way sort is deliberately not drawn and the panel agrees: the sort is the chapter's coverage, the silence is its mechanism.

**Ch12 — REPLACE CONCEPT.** *Carrier: one item missing from a list of the week's necessary things.* Retitle **THE THING WITH NO DEADLINE**. Cut the three full black bars entirely. Cut the source line. Replace with the chapter's own image: **a short ruled list of the week's necessary things, five or six entries, each with a solid mark beside it — and one final ruled line, blank, no mark, glossed *her*.** Under it: *"Everything on this list had a day attached. One thing did not."* Then add the dropped second half as one small mark: beside the blank line, two glosses, *"said in your language"* and *"heard in hers"*, with only the second connecting to the line. If only one change is affordable: retitle, and cut the three top bars.

## 3. The Part plates

They work as a set and better than most chapter plates, because they were built to be felt and they keep that promise. Plates 1 and 2 are one idea done twice, well: a cross-section showing the mark patience leaves over time. The canyon's tiny wavy water line at the very bottom is the whole argument — the agent that cut all that rock is the smallest mark on the page. Keep both untouched. **Plate 3 is the weakest and the odd one:** it renders as a dome or a fingerprint, not as warmth or reaching; the two small gaps in the middle lines are presumably the "opening" the caption names and are far too subtle to be felt at page size. It also breaks the set's own logic — 1 and 2 show *the thing the element made*, this shows an ambiguous surface. Suggestion: keep the line vocabulary but draw the opening plainly — the same stacked arcs, parting clearly outward from a point low on the page, so the lines are visibly leaning toward something. Captions verified: all three are the exact last sentences of `parts/part-1-steady-river.md`, `part-2-sturdy-oak.md`, `part-3-warm-sun.md`. Production note: two of the three PNGs carry a long `books-the-stoic-husband-parts-plate-N-` prefix and one does not.

## 4. Cross-set note

What the family does well is real: one monochrome line-and-rule register that reads as a book rather than a slide deck; a big serif title plus a one-line subtitle that survives phone width; and a discipline no plate desk broke, of never putting a claim on a plate the chapter does not make. The best of them (Ch10, Ch03, Ch08, Ch11) all do the same thing — they turn an abstraction into one physical relationship a man can hold, and the difference between two states of that object *is* the argument. The pattern making the others hard to read is the inverse, with three faces. **The caption stack:** most plates carry four to six lines of italic copy (subtitle, a gloss per panel, a divider, two closing lines), so the eye reads the words, gets the point from the words, and skips the drawing — which is exactly "hard to understand the key takeaway," because the takeaway was never in the picture. **An unstable grammar:** the filled dark bar means accruing cost (Ch03), visible work (Ch06), rehearsal (Ch11), a skill in full use (Ch12); the filled dot means a permanent hole (Ch04), an uncounted small act (Ch07), a unit of weight (Ch08); the dashed outline means invisible (Ch06), unsaid (Ch11), unfinished (Ch10), lapsed (Ch12). A reader taking one chapter every few days cannot build a grammar from that, and the worst case — the nail meaning opposite things across Ch04 and Ch05 — actively misleads him. **Diagnosis without cure:** ten of twelve draw the failure and leave the Lesson to a caption; Ch10 is the one plate where the corrective is a drawn object, and the one plate nobody needs explained. Three fixes lift the whole set: cap every plate at a subtitle, one gloss per element and one closing line; fix the four primitives' meanings book-wide in a `design-language.md`; require that the Lesson appear as a mark, not a sentence.

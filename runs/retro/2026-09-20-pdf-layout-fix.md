## What worked, with evidence

**The root cause was one thing, and it was fixed at the source.** Two of three
defects reduce to a single dead token. `compile.py` stopped *emitting* `\pagebreak`
rather than a renderer learning to *strip* it — the correct direction, and the one
the 2026-09-16 second-renderer finding said was missed last time.

**The `.pb` marker over a blanket `h2` rule was the right call, and it is
load-bearing.** Counted in the shipped HTML: 18 `<h2>`, 12 of them "Putting It
Into Practice" and **six legitimate in-prose headings** (The Standard; The River,
the Oak, and the Sun; Oak/River/Sun; The Danger of Becoming Only One). A blanket
rule would have broken six pages to fix twelve. The reason is recorded at the
point of the decision.

**The hand-mirrored page-break parity actually holds.** `chapter_pdf.py` cannot be
executed here (neither `weasyprint` nor `markdown` imports; GAPS.md registers it),
so the parity claim in the commit message was unverified when made. Compared
declaration-by-declaration it is nonetheless correct:

```
('figure.plate', 'ok', [after:always, before:always, inside:avoid], [same])
('.pb',          'ok', [before:always], [before:always])
('h1',           'ok', [before:always], [before:always])
```

## The one thing the parity claim overstates

`figure.plate` matches on page-break semantics and diverges elsewhere:
`chapter_pdf.py` gives the image `max-height: 8.6in` with no vertical centering;
`chapter_pdf_local.py` gives it `6.8in` inside a `min-height:7.3in` flex box. The
8.6in was copied from `div.plate img`, which has **no figcaption**; `figure.plate`
does, plus a 0.3in caption margin. On weasyprint's default page with 1in margins
that is close enough to the content height to be worth proving rather than
assuming — and it cannot be proven in this container. Stated, not filed as a
defect, because it is unrunnable by anyone here and GAPS.md already carries the
reason.

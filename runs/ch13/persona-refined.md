## Essential findings

### E1. The chapter got wider, not deeper — and lost its central example and her side of it. (Tension reader; Eric; continuity to the outline. Ch13, whole.)

The author said it felt short and wanted **room to develop these ideas**. The
revision went from 1,176 to 1,501 words while going from roughly three topics to
seven: presence, words, touch, love-language list, service, gifts, remembering,
occasions, curiosity. Net effect per idea is *less* development, not more. Two
specific losses make that visible:

- **The outline's central example is now a hypothetical aside.** The outline names
  it: "The man who asks his wife on a date — after twelve years — and realizes he
  doesn't know what she'd want to do anymore." The prior draft dramatized this and
  carried a discovery through the chapter (an interest she had taken up; asking
  later how it turned out). The revision reduces it to two paragraphs beginning
  "Imagine asking your wife out after twelve years," and the discovery becomes a
  conditional: "She may tell you about an interest she has now that you wouldn't
  have expected then." Nobody in this chapter learns anything. The author's
  requested additions displaced the scene rather than joining it.
- **Key point 2 of the outline is missing.** "What she actually experiences when
  pursuit stops: not indifference, but invisibility — the specific feeling of
  being furniture in her own marriage, known but not seen." Her interior
  experience appears once, in six words: "She wanted the part of you that came
  with it." Everything else is a husband-side behavior. This is the single
  strongest available answer to "why do this," and it is the material that would
  have filled the room the author asked for.

Consequences the personas name:

- **Tension reader:** Chapter 13 is the flat stretch. Chapter 12 ends at real
  pitch ("She's been waiting to be one of the necessary things"). Chapter 13 opens
  on a mild scene and then *defuses it in the third paragraph* — "You made time
  for her both nights. That counts for something." Nothing after that raises the
  stakes again, because the cost of failure was moved out to Chapter 14 along with
  the removed passage. The chapter has no cost, no turn, and no scene. It runs at
  one temperature — instructional — for 1,500 words. Line 65 is the symptom in
  pure form: a five-clause recap of the five sections, i.e. the chapter listing
  itself back to the reader.
- **Eric** (primary; high bullshit detector, low tolerance for vague
  inspiration): this is the chapter where he skims. Chapter 12 earned him with a
  grandfather, a five-month count, a sightseeing flight over Nashville, and a
  named researcher he could check. Chapter 13 has no first person at all, no
  anecdote, and no mechanism — the Stoic content is one Seneca paraphrase used as
  decoration. What he would say to a friend: *"Twelve was good. Thirteen was the
  one that could have been a magazine listicle — put your phone down, bring her a
  pastry, don't buy the Walgreens card."* That is the worst sentence this book can
  earn from Eric, and it is available here.
- **Paul** (in crisis): feels addressed by the last-romantic-thing question — it
  is the kind of concrete measure he came for. Where he puts it down is the recap
  paragraph, for the opposite reason to Eric: his marriage is on the wall and the
  chapter's largest gesture is a remembered pastry. Nothing here claims pursuit
  fixes a crisis, which is honest, but nothing tells him what to do with the
  gap either.

**Cost to fix:** a real revision pass, not a patch. Restore the twelve-year date
as a scene with a discovery in it; add a short passage on what she experiences
when pursuit stops (outline key point 2); cut or fold line 65's recap; accept
that two of the seven topics get one sentence rather than a paragraph each.
Likely lands 1,600–1,800 words. This is the chapter the author actually asked
for when he said it felt short.

### E2. The love-languages paragraph repeats Chapter 12's explanation, against an explicit boundary instruction. (Eric; continuity. Ch12 ¶"Her currency, not yours" vs Ch13 ¶5 of "Be there for the time you share.")

The feedback is unambiguous: "It can **briefly recall** the list but should **not
repeat** chapter 12's explanation of it."

- Ch12: "It's a popular list rather than a science, and nobody has shown that
  matching hers changes anything. As a tactic rather than a philosophy it does one
  thing well: it makes you look at all five instead of the one you'd reach for."
- Ch13: "The five love languages are a popular way to remember that care has more
  than one form... They aren't a scientific rule that every person has one fixed
  language. They're useful here because they can show you what you've let fall
  away."

Same three moves in the same order: popular-not-science, no fixed language, use
it to check the ones you neglect. Ch13 also re-enumerates all five, which Ch12
had already done concretely (his touch, her acts of service). For Eric, reading a
chapter a week, this is the paragraph where the book repeats itself — and it is
the caveat, the part that felt rigorous the first time, so the repetition costs
credibility rather than just space.

Compounding: **Ch13's hook question is Ch12's hook question.** Ch12 opens on his
wife asking "when the last time was that I took her on a date"; Ch13's fourth
section opens "Can you name the last romantic thing you did for your wife?" The
author asked for the second question, so keep it — but it must not arrive with no
acknowledgement that Chapter 12 opened on its twin. One clause referring back
converts a repeat into a callback.

**Cost to fix:** replace the paragraph with one or two sentences that recall the
list and point forward only ("You met the list in the last chapter; use it here
as an inventory of what you have let fall away"), and add one backward clause at
the last-romantic-thing question. Under 15 minutes. Frees ~60 words for E1.

### E3. The boredom study claims a statistical control the feedback's research check does not state. (Skeptic; Ch13 final section.)

Prose: "In one long-term study, couples who described their marriage as boring
were less satisfied nine years later, **even after the researchers accounted for
how satisfied they were at the start**."

The research check in `author-feedback-2026-09-24.md` says only: "A 2009
longitudinal study found reported marital boredom predicted lower satisfaction
nine years later. This is an association, not proof that boredom causes
infidelity or divorce." The baseline-adjustment clause is not in the check, and
it is the clause doing the persuasive work — it is what upgrades the finding from
"unhappy people call things boring" to something worth acting on. Two further
small overstatements in the same sentence: "couples who described their marriage
as boring" (the design measures individual spouses' reports, not couple-level
agreement), and "one long-term study" without a year or name, which for Eric is
weaker than either naming it or dropping it.

The hedge that follows is genuinely good and should be kept ("That's a reason to
pay attention to the pattern, not a prediction about your marriage"). It does not
license the unsupported clause above it.

Note for the Publisher, not a prose finding: the Editor's Notes point to
`research-addendum-2026-09-24.md`. If that addendum quotes the paper establishing
the baseline control, this finding downgrades to a citation-status matter and the
clause can stand with the study named. I did not read the addendum; that is the
check that resolves this. Absent it, cut the clause.

**Cost to fix:** delete one clause, or name the study and cite the control from
the addendum. Minutes either way.

## Optional findings

### O1. The Chapter 14 seam is now unbuilt, and that is a Publisher decision, not a Ch13 defect. (Tension reader vs the author's instruction — a disagreement I am not averaging.)

The author was explicit: remove the noticing-other-women passage and do not use
it as a transition. The revision complied, correctly. But the outline's
"Transition to Chapter 14" depended on exactly that move, and Chapter 14 opens on
the comparison mechanism. Chapter 13 now ends "Put the phone down, and give her
the evening you came to share" — a clean close with no forward edge at all.

The tension reader says the arc has a hole at the seam. The author's instruction
says the hole is preferable to the passage. Both are right, and the resolution is
not in Chapter 13: **Chapter 14 should absorb the bridge into its own opening**
rather than Chapter 13 reaching for it. Flagging so this does not get silently
re-inserted into Ch13 by a later pass citing the outline.

### O2. The river collides with the book's title metaphor. (Continuity; Introduction vs Ch13.)

The Introduction establishes the river as *the husband's* calm adaptability —
"Flow with wisdom." Ch13's Seneca/Heraclitus passage makes the river *her*
changing over twelve years. Both are legitimate readings of the image, and the
voice check counts four river-family mentions as within cap, but a reader who
learned the river as one of his three qualities now meets it as a property of his
wife, with no line reconciling the two. One sentence would do it ("You know the
river as the shape of your own steadiness; Seneca used it for something else
first"), or use Heraclitus's change without the word.

### O3. Nothing in the chapter grounds pursuit in Arête. (Skeptic; Eric.)

The steel-man opposition, stated at its strongest: *this chapter asks a man for
permanent unilateral effort whose only stated measure is his wife's pleasure —
her enjoyment, her ease, her being remembered — with no account of what he owes
himself, and no answer for the man who does all of it and gets nothing back. That
is courtship as a permanent audition, and it is the performance mindset the book
itself names as the reader's error: optimization instead of identity.*

Where the chapter answers it: honestly, in three places. "Don't make a show of
how much you gave up, and don't wait beside the finished task for applause" cuts
transaction. "Taking initiative means being willing to notice and learn, not
deciding you know better than she does" refuses paternalism. "Some years are
crowded and hard... You don't need to turn every date on the calendar into a
performance" makes the demand livable.

Where it does not: the "why" is never lifted above her reaction. The outline's
own Stoic principle for this chapter — "love as ongoing election, not past event"
— is never stated, and the word for it is never reached for. Ch12 at least had
reciprocity as an argument ("If you give her the bare minimum, the bare minimum
is what comes back"). Ch13 has a boredom study. Two sentences naming pursuit as
election — something he does because it is who he decided to be, and which would
be worth doing even in a week she does not notice — would answer the skeptic and
would sound like this book rather than like advice. Folds naturally into the E1
revision.

## Audience outcomes, named

**Eric (primary).** Addressed by: the gift/shared-account passage, which takes an
objection he has actually made and answers it without moralizing; and the
last-romantic-thing question, which is a measure rather than a mood. Accused by:
nothing — the chapter is gentle throughout, arguably too gentle to hold him.
Puts it down at: the love-languages paragraph (E2, déjà vu) or the recap
paragraph (E1). To a friend: *"Chapter 12 was the one that got me. Thirteen says
put the phone down and remember the pastry — true, but I didn't need a chapter."*

**Paul (secondary, in crisis).** Addressed by: "If you have to work back through
months to find it, start there," which gives him a first move that costs nothing
and needs no cooperation; and "A favor done with resentment can leave her wishing
she'd done it herself," which names something he probably did last week. Accused
by: "You gave little thought to the woman you were celebrating" — the one line
with an edge, and it is earned. Puts it down at: the closing recap, because the
scale of the remedy does not match the scale of his problem, and nothing in the
chapter acknowledges that gap. To a friend: *"It's a list of small things. I think
the small things are right. I don't know if they're enough for us."*

Neither reader has a strong abandonment point; the risk here is skimming, not
closing the book. That is a reading judgment, not a retention result.

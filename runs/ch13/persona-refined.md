## Round-1 essentials: closed

**E1 (depth not width; her side; the twelve-year date; no recap close) —
resolved.**

- *Depth over width.* The added length goes into two developed passages, not new
  topics. Topic inventory is unchanged from the version I failed.
- *The twelve-year date is now a scene with real discovery.* "Say you decide to
  take her out after twelve years" runs through a failure (he can't name what
  she'd want now), her actual answer (the lake town), the reason behind it (a
  garden she has been describing for months), and a consequence on a later day
  (the Tuesday he asks where the first bed goes and she walks him out with a
  phone light). Somebody learns something in this chapter now, and the learning
  has a cost and a follow-through. Eric's round-1 verdict ("could have been a
  magazine listicle") no longer holds: this is the passage he would repeat.
- *Her side is restored.* "What it looks like from her side" carries outline key
  point 2 as its own section, including the consequence chain (stops mentioning
  what she wants, organizes her own birthday, says "whatever you want") and the
  honest beat that a first good evening may not be met with delight. This is the
  material that answers "why do this," and it is now present rather than
  compressed into six words.
- *The recap close is gone.* The six-imperative summary is cut; nothing follows
  the one-sentence close. The tension reader's flat-stretch finding is downgraded
  to optional (O3): the chapter now has a scene and a turn, though its
  temperature still sits below Ch12's.
- *Bonus, round-1 O3.* "A choice isn't something you made once. It's something
  you either keep making or quietly stop making" states the outline's assigned
  principle — love as ongoing election — which the prior version never reached
  for. Round-1 O2 (river vs. the title metaphor) is also answered in one sentence.

**E2 (no repeat of Ch12's love-languages explanation or hook) — resolved.**

- The caveat paragraph is gone. What remains is one sentence: "You met the five
  love languages a chapter ago: time together, words, service, gifts, touch. Use

  Cutting the caveat does not leave a typology claim behind: "Maybe acts of

- The hook no longer collides. "Not the last date you took her on. A date is one
  evening; this is wider than that" distinguishes the question from Ch12's
  opening, and "a chapter ago" two sections earlier gives the reader the anchor.
  Weaker than an explicit callback (see O2), but no longer a repeat.

**E3 — not re-raised.** The boredom sentence now matches the addendum's direct

## Optional findings, ranked

### O1. The study's survivorship limit is the one part of the addendum the prose drops. (Skeptic; Ch13 final section.)

The addendum says the sample is 123 **still-married** couples and "excludes
couples who divorced before year sixteen." The prose says "followed married
couples for years." A skeptic who looks the paper up finds that the marriages
most likely to show boredom's damage are the ones not in the denominator — which
cuts *against* the alarming reading and is therefore worth saying, not hiding.
This is not a claim beyond the addendum; it is an omission of a limit the
addendum flags. **Cost:** four words ("couples still married at the end"), or
leave it and accept the exposure. Minutes.

### O2. The Ch12 hook is distinguished but never acknowledged. (Continuity; Eric.)

Per `02-audience.md` the reader takes a chapter at a time, often days apart. "Not
the last date you took her on" tells him the question is different; it does not
tell him Chapter 12 opened on its twin, which is the thing that would convert a
faint echo into a deliberate return. **Cost:** one clause ("Chapter 12 opened on
the date question; this one is wider"). Under five minutes. Optional because the
current wording is not a repeat — it just leaves the callback unclaimed.

### O3. Chapter 13 is still cooler than Chapter 12, and the two personas split on whether that matters. (Tension reader vs. Eric — not averaged.)

Ch12 runs on the author's own marriage: twenty-four years, five months counted,
the Nashville flight. Ch13 has no first person at all, by the author's own
direction, and its heat now comes entirely from the invented second-person scene.
The **tension reader** says the arc dips: after Ch12's pitch, a chapter with no
author in it reads as a step back from confession to instruction. **Eric** says
the opposite — the garden scene is checkable against his own house in a way
another anecdote about someone else's marriage is not, and he prefers it. Both
readings are available from the same text. I am not resolving it: if the author
wants Ch13 warmer, the lever is one or two lines of his own, not more scene.

### O4. "Furniture in her own marriage" is a second image, and the skeptic's misreading is adjacent. (Skeptic; continuity.)

The Line Editor already flagged the metaphor as a judgement call; I agree it reads
as plain description and would keep it. The adjacent risk is different: a
defensive reader can take the whole "her side" section as an accusation with no
accompanying account of what he is owed, and close the section deciding the book
has switched sides. What holds him is two things already in the text — "she
doesn't usually decide you've stopped loving her" and the election sentence. Both
are load-bearing against that misreading; a later pass should not trim either for
length.

## A — Reciprocal interest

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** Turn toward your wife, enjoy hearing what's happening to her, and keep sharing yourself too.

**Carrier:** Two literal facing people, joined by a question travelling toward her and a returning conversational curve. Separate labels keep what matters to each spouse distinct; the exchange connects them without requiring shared tastes. The curved paths signify conversational direction, not an additional object metaphor.

**Chapter image used:** Literal attention: “turn toward her while she's still telling you about her day.” Reciprocal contribution: “You share what matters to you.” The declared river is not pictured; no replacement metaphor is introduced.

**Copy, counted:** 36 words total, 7 text elements. Title 6; becoming subtitle 6; “Ask what happened next.” 4; “what matters to you” 4; “what matters to her” 4; “Let yourself be pleased for her.” 6; “You share what matters to you.” 6.

**Skeptic's misreading:** The arrows may imply an exchange owed back to him. The prose leaves room for her not to talk; the picture must not suggest a required response.

## B — Ask in the present

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** Both of you keep changing, so take an interest in her current answer instead of relying on what you remember from dating.

**Carrier:** Two flowing water bands remain alongside one another. The faint upstream relation marks remembered answers; the strong present relation connects them at “now,” explicitly through interest in her answer. Flow carries time; current attention is the salient connection. Neither band drifts away from the other.

**Chapter image used:** “Its water keeps changing, and so do we.” The old/current relationship comes from “You kept using the answers you already had” and “you asked the woman who's here now.”

**Copy, counted:** 42 words total, 11 text elements. Title 6; water subtitle 8; “when you were dating” 4; “now” 1; “the answers you already had” 5 across two lines; “Be interested in her answer.” 5 across two lines; becoming sentence pair 6; “you asked the woman who's here now.” 7 across two lines.

**Skeptic's misreading:** The bands could still read as generic change rather than two people's changing interests. The present connection's active wording is essential, and a cold read should judge whether it is enough.

## C — Interest that returns

**A stranger sees this and takes away:** Listen warmly now and remember enough to ask about it when you talk again.

**Carrier:** Two literal conversation moments in sequence, with the same speech mark returning later. The recurring content makes sustained attention visible rather than reducing pursuit to a single good question. The people stay turned toward one another in both moments.

**Chapter image used:** Literal conversation: “Let her finish. Say what you're glad about. When you talk later, remember enough to return to it.” No replacement metaphor for the river is introduced.

**Copy, counted:** 39 words total, 10 text elements. **Skeptic's misreading:** This may read as a listening tip rather than continuing discovery of a changing person. Its becoming subtitle supplies that context; the cold panel should assess whether the picture earns it.

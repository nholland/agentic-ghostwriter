# Ch13 plate: standalone read (gw-plate Stage 5)

Plate: `runs/ch13/pdf/plate.png`. I read it alone first and wrote sections 1-4 before opening `runs/ch13/distillation.md`.

## 1. The blind read (one sentence)

"If you plan her Saturday around what she liked when you met her, you'll miss. Ask her who she is now, because you're the one who stopped being curious."

## 2. The two lines and the box, as he reads them

- **Box**, "one thing she'd be excited about this Saturday", is the target. It's concrete and he can picture it. It works.
- **Grey dashed line** from "arrange the whole thing / what she liked at twenty-six" stops short of the box and has no arrowhead. He reads it as effort built on old information that never reaches her. It reads as intended: a miss, not an unfinished line.
- **Solid arrow** from "ask her / who she is now and ask about it again" reaches the box. He reads it as the move that works.

## 3. The skeptic's misreading

- **"Arranging is the wrong move."** The bold label on the losing branch is "arrange the whole thing", so a man who plans dates reads it as the plate calling his effort the mistake and telling him to stop leading and just ask. The chapter doesn't say that. Its point is that the effort is real and the information behind it is stale. Nothing on the plate says the effort is fine. This is the main misreading.
- **Accusation.** The caption "What stopped was your curiosity" points at him with nothing to soften it. Without the concession that he still loves her and still does things for her, a defensive reader hears "you stopped caring," which is an accusation.
- **"She changed" as a grievance.** This is mild. "what she liked at twenty-six" against "who she is now" can be read as "she's not the woman I married." The caption puts the change on his curiosity, not on her, so the plate mostly blocks this. It's a low risk.
- **"won her"** is trophy language, but it is the mechanism verbatim, so I note it and don't propose an edit.

## 4. Layout defects

- The right label repeats "ask": "**ask her** / who she is now / and ask about it again". The second "ask" is tied to the time element that's missing. Minor.
- The two lines end at different heights (dashed about 530px, arrow about 455px). That's intentional and reads correctly.
- No overlaps or clipping. The hierarchy (title, box, branches, rule, caption) is clean.

## 5. Against the distillation

Conversation sentence: *"You can love your wife and keep doing things for her, and still have stopped being curious about who she is now."*

My blind sentence gets the second half: curiosity stopped, about who she is now. It misses the first half, the concession that he loves her and keeps doing things for her. That concession is what makes the sentence the chapter's and not an accusation, and its absence is what opens both skeptic readings in section 3.

**Verdict: NOT PASS. Edit list:**

1. **Caption (required).** Replace "What stopped was your curiosity / about who she is now." with the Conversation sentence in the author's words, split over two lines: "You can love her and keep doing things for her, / and still have stopped being curious about who she is now." This puts the concession on the plate, turns the caption from accusation into recognition, and tells him the dashed branch is still love that misses its target, not the wrong move.
2. **Left bold label (recommended).** Relabel "arrange the whole thing" to "plan it for her". Keep the subline "what she liked / at twenty-six". This softens the branch from "controlling the whole thing" to ordinary effort, which matches "keep doing things for her."
3. **Right label (optional).** Relabel "who she is now / and ask about it again" to "who she is now, / then ask again later in the week". This removes the repeated "ask" and matches the Challenge line.

Do not change the box, the two lines or the title.

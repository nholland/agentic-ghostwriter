## 1. What it says to him (plate only)

"If you plan Saturday around what she liked at twenty-six, you'll miss. Ask her who she is now, and ask again later in the week. You can love her and do things for her and still have stopped being curious about her."

## 2. The box and the two lines

- **Box:** the target, meaning one thing she would actually be excited about this Saturday. It reads as concrete and doable.
- **Dashed grey line (left):** planning a date from what she liked at twenty-six. It has no arrowhead and stops about 85px short of the box, so it reads as a miss. That lands in under ten seconds.
- **Solid arrow (right):** asking her who she is now. It reaches the box, so it reads as the way that works.
- The two columns sit symmetrically on the box's centre (433px and 430px from centre). The contrast is clear.

## 3. Skeptic's misreadings

- **Does it blame his effort?** Mostly no, and the caption does the work: "keep doing things for her" grants the effort before it names the gap. There is one residual risk. The failing line is labelled "planning a date", so a defensive man glancing only at the drawing can read "your planning is the problem". The body line under it ("what she liked at twenty-six") pins the fault to the source, not the effort, so this is minor.
- **Does it make her change a grievance?** No. "Who she is now" is neutral. "At twenty-six" is a fact, not a complaint about her.
- **Does it accuse him?** The title "after you've won her" can be heard as "you stopped trying once you'd won her". The caption answers that. Some readers may also hear "won" as conquest framing. That is the book's mechanism wording, so it is not a plate defect.
- **The strongest misreading:** "Asking her kills the surprise, so the plate is telling me I can't surprise my wife any more." Nothing on the plate rules this out. It is not fatal, because the chapter itself says familiar routines are fine and she should have room to surprise *him*. Noted, not required.
- **Small ambiguity:** "ask her / who she is now / again later in the week" parses a bit oddly. Read cold, "again" has no first time to point back to. It is the Challenge's "ask about it again later in the week", compressed.

## 4. Layout

Balanced. Title, box, arrows, labels, divider and three-line caption stack evenly. The side margins are symmetric. The space below the caption is a little larger than the space above the title, which reads as normal bottom padding rather than dead space. The 640x520 canvas holds the three-line caption without crowding the divider. The widest label line, "again later in the week", stays inside the divider's width. No collisions and no clipped text.

## Verdict against distillation

Conversation sentence: "You can love your wife and keep doing things for her, and still have stopped being curious about who she is now."

**PASS.** The caption carries the sentence nearly verbatim, with "her" for "your wife". The drawing shows the same idea as an action: doing something for her (planning a date) from old knowledge misses, and curiosity about who she is now hits. My plate-only sentence contains the Conversation sentence and adds the Challenge. That is the right order for this plate.

Optional, not required for PASS:
1. Relabel the left bold header "planning a date" to "planning from memory". This parallels the imperative "ask her" and moves the fault from his effort to the stale source.
2. Change the right body to "who she is now, / then ask again later in the week". This gives "again" a first ask to refer back to.

# Chapter 13 Distillation — Pursue Her After You Have Her

**Mechanism:** Keep learning her after you've won her

**Conversation sentence:** You can love your wife and keep doing things for her, and still have stopped being curious about who she is now.

Pursuit after marriage is interest: the attention you give her while you're together and the curiosity you keep about who she's becoming. Before she said yes, you noticed what she liked and remembered what she told you. Marriage made that feel optional. Twelve years in, planning a date, you can find you no longer know what she'd want, because you stopped learning about her somewhere along the way. When your attention drifts, she can tell you've stopped noticing her. She feels taken for granted, needed and walked past, and she starts asking for less. Put down the phone and be in the hour you offered. Ask about her, and remember the answer. Notice what she handled and say so. You can't do anything about what you never saw. Familiar routines can be good; a marriage still needs room for her to surprise you.

**Lesson:** Winning her was the start. You keep choosing her by staying curious about who she is now.
**Challenge:** Name one thing she'd be excited about this Saturday. If you can't, ask her, and ask about it again later in the week.

**Practice:**
1. Once a week, ask her one question about her life that you can't already answer. Remember what she tells you, and bring it up again later.
2. When she invites you to spend time together, put your phone away and give her your attention. If your mind wanders, come back to the moment you agreed to share.
3. At the end of each week, name one thing she handled that you noticed, and tell her you saw it.

## Findings

1. River attribution: Heraclitus predates Stoicism. The familiar twice-in-the-same-river wording is transmitted through later accounts; Plato, Cratylus 402a, and Plutarch are source leads. Fragment B12 emphasizes changing waters in the same rivers. Use a paraphrase pending edition-level confirmation, not an authenticated verbatim Heraclitus quotation. Scholarly reference read: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/
2. Perel's own writing connects novelty and discovery to continuing desire, and explicitly discusses routine day/work questions. This is clinical interpretation, not a controlled demonstration that marriage inevitably becomes boring. Primary article read: https://www.estherperel.com/blog/letters-from-esther-53-novelty-is-a-powerful-aphrodisiac-heres-how-to-have-more
3. Perel also treats a partner's separateness as an opportunity for discovery. Different interests need not be evidence of a failing bond. Editorial implication: distinguish separate tastes from indifference to what those tastes mean to the partner. Primary article read: https://www.estherperel.com/blog/when-are-you-drawn-to-your-partner-in-long-term-relationships
4. Reis et al. (2010), Are You Happy for Me? How Sharing Positive Events With Others Provides Personal and Interpersonal Benefits, provides experimental evidence on enthusiastic responses to good news. Experiment 3 found higher immediate trust, perceived responsiveness, and willingness to disclose personal material relative to a fun-interaction condition. These were laboratory interactions with a confederate, not proof of a years-long marital withdrawal cycle. Full paper read: https://www.sas.rochester.edu/psy//people/faculty/reis_harry/assets/pdf/Reisetal.JPSP_2010.pdf
5. Phone-use/retaliation diary studies are promising additional leads, not fully reviewed evidence here. Publisher pages returned 403: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2022.107398 and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563222001455 . Do not claim they establish the author's whole causal sequence.

## Proposed interpretation, not an approved content concept

The familiar person keeps changing; habitual responses can prevent either spouse from noticing. The child's drawing example shows attention the husband already knows how to give. It does not require him to fake excitement or treat his wife as a child. Separate interests can supply new material for curiosity. Automatic affection is not inherently empty; the author's concern is affection accompanied by absent attention.

The sequence 'less attention, less sharing, less mutual interest' is a plausible chapter mechanism to investigate, not a universal or established causal law on the evidence reviewed here. The husband's renewed attention cannot guarantee reciprocation.

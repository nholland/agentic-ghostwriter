# Chapter 13 research addendum — 2026-09-24

Scope: evidence for the author's new direction (presence, initiative, romance, remembering, gifts, and boredom), plus Stoic alternatives to the Seneca/Heraclitus river. This is a source check for revised prose, not a claim that all of these studies prove a prescription. No citation status has been promoted to `verified`.

## Claims and limits

| Chapter idea | Evidence checked | Safe use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Put the phone away during shared time. | A [2025 meta-analysis of partner phubbing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12106345/) found negative associations with relationship and marital satisfaction. | Supports the practical concern. Most included studies are observational; do not claim one evening's phone use causes divorce. |
| Do thoughtful tasks without making her manage each one. | [Ciciolla & Luthar's study of 393 U.S. married/partnered mothers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8223758/) found disproportionate responsibility for household management associated with strain and lower partner satisfaction. A [2024/25 study of cognitive household labor](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11761833/) also links the mental part of housework with women's well-being. | Supports distinguishing doing a task from noticing, planning, and monitoring it. Samples and correlational designs do not justify saying all women want the same tasks or that every question is a burden. Ask preferences; do not outsource every act of noticing. |
| Use the love languages as prompts, not fixed types. | [Impett, Park & Muise (2024)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09637214231217663) reviewed the framework's central assumptions and found little support for discrete primary languages or matching as the key to a good relationship. Some individual correlational studies point in a more favorable direction. | The list can help a reader notice neglected forms of care. Do not call it a validated diagnostic theory or imply a single preferred form makes the others dispensable. |
| Thoughtful gifts can matter even with shared money. | [Flynn & Adams (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2008.11.003) found gift-givers overestimate how much higher price improves recipients' appreciation. Gift literature treats giving as a symbolic relational act, but the shared-bank-account objection itself has not been experimentally tested here. | Ground the shared-money point in the author's practical observation. Avoid promising that every recipient reads thought or expense as love. The argument for attention over price is compatible with the study. |
| A recurring occasion needs meaning rather than autopilot. | [Garcia-Rada, Sezer & Norton (2019)](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/garcia-rada%20sezer%20norton_c5106adb-2f23-4725-bb74-d0ab4f755a5b.pdf) distinguish mutually meaningful couple rituals from mere routines across four studies. | Supports the difference between a chosen anniversary ritual and a rote purchase. Does not prove a particular card or date plan is better for every marriage. |
| Boredom is worth addressing. | Direct reading of [Tsapelas, Aron & Orbuch (2009)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24354632_Marital_Boredom_Now_Predicts_Less_Satisfaction_9_Years_Later): in 123 still-married couples, year-seven boredom predicted lower year-sixteen satisfaction after baseline satisfaction was controlled. The sample excludes couples who divorced before year sixteen. [Esther Perel's essay](https://www.estherperel.com/blog/letters-from-esther-53-novelty-is-a-powerful-aphrodisiac-heres-how-to-have-more) argues for curiosity and novelty in familiar relationships. | Write “predicted lower satisfaction,” not “caused divorce/cheating.” Perel is a clinical interpretation, not the longitudinal study. Ordinary familiar evenings are not themselves a problem. |
| Divorce and infidelity statistics should not carry the chapter. | [Rosenfeld's paper](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/a/7536/files/2015/06/Rosenfeld-Gender-of-Breakup-paper.pdf) found wives more often wanted the divorce in a U.S. heterosexual sample, but did not establish romance as the reason and did not simply count filing paperwork. A [GSS analysis through 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28517944/) found men more likely than women to report extramarital sex. | Neither backs the proposed causal story. Leave both out of Chapter 13. |

## Stoic lessons that could serve this chapter

1. **Musonius Rufus, Lecture XIIIA: marriage as active mutual devotion.** The [primary lecture text](https://sites.google.com/site/thestoiclife/the_teachers/musonius-rufus/lectures/13-0) describes companionship and each spouse working for the other's good. This is the strongest thematic match for service, presence, and sustained pursuit. Chapter 6 already uses Musonius on a shared life, so Chapter 13 would need a brief callback rather than a new full introduction. The available Cora Lutz translation is still in copyright under `06-sources.md`; paraphrase only unless rights are resolved.
2. **Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 30: a role is lived in actions.** In the [house George Long translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm), he reasons from relationships to what one ought to do, rather than judging only the other person's conduct. Applied carefully, being a husband is an ongoing practice, not a title acquired on the wedding day. The ancient examples about fathers and obedience are unsuitable as marriage instructions; paraphrase the narrow principle and preserve the wife's agency. The existing OKF card's `evidence_source` says page-text while its note says the wording came from search summaries; confirm from the opened Gutenberg text before any verbatim use.
3. **Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 29: count the continuing work before entering the contest.** The [same primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm) warns that wanting a prize is different from accepting the training required to pursue it. It maps to courtship followed by sustained effort. It also risks repeating Chapter 12's athletic/habit image, so it is less attractive for the prose here.

**Editorial recommendation:** The revised draft keeps Seneca's river because it explains why a husband must keep learning the person beside him. If the author wants the Stoic lesson to emphasize continuing effort instead, replace that paragraph with a concise Musonius callback on mutual devotion. Avoid stacking three ancient lessons in this chapter.

# Reader Panel: Ch12 / Ch13 split, read back to back

Date: 2026-09-30. This comes from the session environment. The panel has no shell, so the clock was not read.
Inputs: `runs/ch12/refined-split.md` then `runs/ch13/refined-split.md`, prose only (Editor's Notes ignored). Also read: `02-audience.md`, and `03-outline.md` Ch11 through Ch14 including the boundary notes.
Personas run: Skeptic, Beta Readers (Eric, Paul), Continuity. I also ran a short tension read for Q4 and Q5.

## Short answers

1. **Repeats:** yes. There is no repeated scene, but five small examples in Ch12 do Ch13's job, and one Ch13 paragraph restates Ch12's. See #1. Both chapters also explain *why it faded*, in two different ways. See #3.
2. **One sentence each:**
   - Ch12: *Romance faded because nothing in your week demands it, so keep doing it on purpose, in her language instead of yours, before she has to ask.*
   - Ch13: *Being in the room is not the same as paying attention; keep learning who she is now, or your effort is aimed at the woman she used to be.*
   A reader can say both. The risk is that he says Ch12 as "the love-languages chapter" and Ch13 as "put your phone down" (#2, #5).
3. **Handoff:** it mostly works. "Lands only when it's aimed at the woman she is now" is picked up by Ch13, but the payoff comes in its third section ("The date you can't plan"), not its opening. See #6.
4. **Ch12 sag:** mild, and only in the back third. The cause is internal restatement, not length. About 120 to 180 words could go without losing a section. See #4.
5. **Ch13 thin:** not in length. It is thin in voice: the author does not appear in it once. Its close also narrows the chapter to the phone. See #5.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Ch12's examples keep doing Ch13's job (attention, remembering, noticing)
**Raised by:** Continuity, Skeptic. **Chapters:** 12 and 13.

The thesis split holds: action versus interest. The examples do not follow it. Read back to back, these are the overlaps:

- **Asking, then remembering.** Ch12, in "Her currency": *"To her, love is you asking about the meeting she's been dreading, then asking again on Thursday, because you remembered there was a Thursday."* Ch13: *"Asking is useful. Remembering the answer is what she notices."* The garden follow-up has the same shape (*"then asked about it again"*). This is Ch13's central mechanism, stated first in Ch12. It is also a continuity fault inside Ch12. The paragraph has just said her language is acts of service, and then gives an attention example as what love means to her.
- **Keeping track, and the errand.** Ch12, "Before she asks": *"she's still carrying the part that wears her out: keeping track … There was an errand she mentioned yesterday."* Ch13: *"Noticing is also what lets her stop keeping track for both of you. When you're paying attention, you see the errand she mentioned…"* Same phrase, same example. Ch13 explicitly hands the doing back, which the outline allows. But the errand is repeated word for word.
- **Presence.** Ch12's opening: *"Being there is the part that counts."* That is Ch13's thesis sentence ("the time you gave … and the time she actually got"), spent in Ch12's first paragraphs.
- **She stops asking.** Ch12, "What's still there": *"When you stop making an effort, she may stop expecting it from you."* Ch13, "From her side": *"You end up with a wife who has gotten used to asking for less."* Same beat. Ch13 does it much better (the birthday, "whatever you want").
- **Something she mentioned once.** Ch12 gifts: *"She pointed out a book weeks ago."* Ch13: *"You noticed the thing she mentioned liking once, and you still remembered it a week later."* This one can stay. Here Ch12 uses the noticing and does not argue for it.
- **Minor:** Ch12's *"an afternoon planned around something she likes **now**"* signals Ch13 two sections before the close does.

**The skeptic's version:** "You can't separate action from attention. Every good example of doing needs knowing, so the split is cosmetic." The chapters partly answer this: Ch12's close admits the dependence and Ch13's "belongs to the last chapter" line admits the reverse. The Thursday, errand and "being there" lines are where the objection wins.

**Cost:** low, in Ch12's prose. Ch12 needs a replacement acts-of-service example for Thursday (it has to come from the author, so it is an inbox item). Cutting "Being there is the part that counts" and "When you stop making an effort…" takes about 20 words. Ch13 needs only its errand example changed, or the Ch12 errand cut, since "Before she asks" already has dishes and gas.

### 2. Ch12's title promise ("Discipline", feeling follows practice) is not in the prose
**Raised by:** Skeptic, Beta (Eric). **Chapter:** 12.

The outline's ah-ha is *"I've been waiting to feel romantic. That's backwards."* No sentence in the prose says he has been waiting for the feeling, or that the feeling follows the practice. Epictetus 2.18 gives maintenance: *"What you keep doing, you keep."* The muscle line gives awkwardness: *"the first honest attempt feels ridiculous"*. Neither says the feeling comes back. Without that, the strongest opposition is unanswered: *"Scheduled romance is performance. She can tell a man doing it from duty."* Ch12 defends *how* to give (unannounced, not traded) but never says why deliberate effort isn't fake. This is the objection Eric raises, and he raises it hard, because he runs habits and knows "fake it till you make it" when he sees it.

**Cost:** medium. It needs one or two sentences near Epictetus, in the author's words (inbox). This is also the fix that stops a reader calling Ch12 "the love-languages chapter".

### 3. Two diagnoses of the same fade, and the same absolution twice
**Raised by:** Continuity, Skeptic. **Chapters:** 12 and 13.

Ch12 says romance faded because effort is finite and romance has no deadline (*"Nobody calls you at work because you haven't surprised your wife since March"*). Ch13 says attention faded because marriage is secure (*"treated the wedding as proof you'd finished"*). Both can be true. Read back to back, though, it looks like the book explaining the fade twice with two different causes. Each version also comes with the same reassurance: Ch12 *"You didn't stop loving her"* and *"than let you think you got lazy"*; Ch13 *"You still love her, and you probably still do plenty for her."*

**Disagreement:**
- **Continuity** counts this as a finding, because the reader gets two causes and no bridge between them.
- **Beta (Paul)**, who reads days apart, will not notice, and needs the absolution in each chapter to keep reading.

The two positions should not be averaged. The real question is whether the book is read in sequence or one chapter at a time. `02-audience.md` says one sitting per chapter, which favours Paul.

**Cost:** low. One clause in Ch13 could make the causes add up rather than compete, for example "the deadline was never what kept your attention either". The author's wording is needed.

### 4. Ch12's back third restates itself (the Q4 sag)
**Raised by:** Tension (brief), Beta (Eric). **Chapter:** 12.

1,764 words is one sitting and does not sag as a length. The last four sections do sag, because the same points come round again:

- **Acts of service is his weakest, said twice:** *"acts of service is my lowest"* and then, opening the next section, *"Acts of service are the one I'm worst at."*
- **Small beats grand, said twice:** the Nashville paragraph (*"What lands is smaller"*), then *"none of it has to be grand."*
- **The count-back test repeats the opening:** the opening has *"I counted back. Close to five months"*, and "The last one you did" has *"If you have to count back through months…"*. The bridge line *"My wife's question about a date was the small version of this one"* says out loud that it is a repeat. The Line Editor flagged this line as his own.
- **Two endings:** first the research line and apology (*"you may also owe her an apology"*), then Marcus 4.24. The chapter lands, then sets up and lands again.
- **Tail sentences:** *"Winning favor isn't trading for it, either"* sits at the end of a paragraph with no link to it.

The Walgreens scene is the strongest concrete moment in the back half and should stay.

**Cost:** low. Cut the second acts-of-service line, the "grand" sentence and the bridge line, and fold the apology into Marcus. That is roughly 120 to 180 words, and Ch12 stays in range.

### 5. Ch13 is not thin in words; it is thin in voice, and its close shrinks it (the Q5 answer)
**Raised by:** Beta (Eric), Continuity. **Chapter:** 13.

At about 1,180 words Ch13 is a complete sitting. The garden story holds it up, and the Tsapelas caveat (*"it tells you nothing about divorce"*) is exactly what Eric trusts. What is thin:

- **No author presence.** Ch13 contains no "I" and no "my wife". Its central story is hypothetical (*"Say you're twelve years into your marriage"*). Ch12 is built on his date question, his grandfather, his lap-and-head evening and the Nashville flight. Next to it, Ch13 reads like a different writer. Eric came for a man who has been married 30 years, not for a case study.
- **The close narrows the chapter.** *"Put the phone down, and give her the evening you came to share."* That is the presence half. The chapter's own ah-ha is curiosity ("winning was the beginning"), carried by the garden, and the close leaves it out. The close also echoes the chapter's own line *"you can give her the evening you offered"*, which weakens it.
- **The Stoic anchor is one paragraph.** Enchiridion 30 does real work (*"Husband is a role you keep playing"*), and it is the only Stoic content in the chapter.

**Cost:** medium, and it goes to the author. One real moment of his own noticing, or failing to notice, would fix the voice gap. Do not pad, and do not invent. The close needs a rewrite that takes in curiosity; that is low cost.

### 6. The handoff pays off late
**Raised by:** Continuity. **Chapters:** 12 into 13.

Ch12's last line promises *"the woman she is now"*. Ch13 opens on *"the time you gave your wife and the time she actually got"*, which is presence. "Who she is now" arrives in its third section. Conceptually the seam holds: effort that doesn't land, then time given that wasn't received. It is just not a direct pickup. Ch12's closing sentence is 28 words and joins two ideas, Marcus's "necessary things" and the handoff, and the join shows.

**Disagreement:**
- **Continuity** would like a tighter pickup.
- **Beta (both)**, reading days apart, will not feel the seam.

**Cost:** low. Optional. Leave it unless the author wants the chapters read as a pair.

### 7. Smaller continuity items
**Raised by:** Continuity, Beta (Eric).

- **"The last chapter":** Ch13 says *"belongs to the last chapter"*, which can be read as the book's final chapter. Use "the chapter before this" or "Chapter 12".
- **Five languages, three named:** Ch12 says the list *"makes you look at all five"* and then names three (touch, words, service). Gifts get a section later, and quality time is never named. This is minor, but Eric counts.
- **Uncited research:** Ch12's *"Couples followed over the years don't usually come apart because the fighting starts … the research only says they travel together"* has no source, and none is recorded among the chapter's citations. Ch13 cites its longitudinal study properly, so read side by side, the uncited one stands out to an evidence-minded reader. The phrase "the research only says they travel together" is also hard to parse. This goes to the Fact-Checker.
- **Bare callback:** Ch12's *"You've already had the one about giving sixty and expecting forty"* restates the rule in a few words. That is enough for a reader coming back days later.

### 8. Paul and Ch13's "from her side"
**Raised by:** Beta (Paul). **Chapter:** 13.

This is a disagreement inside one reader type, and it should be kept as such.
- **Paul recognizes himself.** *"She organizes her own birthday"* and *"whatever you want"* are the lines he quotes to a friend.
- **Paul also feels accused.** *"Furniture in her own marriage"* is the line where he asks "and what about her?" (objection #2 in `02-audience.md`). Ch12 had given him something reciprocal (*"Effort in the wrong language is still effort, and you owe it a real thank you"*, and his wife *"has gotten every bit as busy"*). Ch13 gives him nothing like that.

*"Give it time. She's working out whether this is a change or just an evening"* is what keeps him reading. No fix is recommended. Watch this line at the verdict.

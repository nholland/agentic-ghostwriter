2. **Low — abstract indirection, line 61.** Exact: “Pursuit after ten or twenty years has this ordinary shape.” “Shape” announces the examples instead of saying what pursuit means. **Fix:** “After ten or twenty years, pursuit means staying interested in each other.” The following learning/sharing sentences can then develop that claim.

## Cross-chapter findings requiring author ruling

### 1. High — marriage chronology, Prologue / Ch1 / Ch12

Exact sentences: Prologue line 11, “We're over thirty years in.” Ch1 line 37, “I've been married twenty-three years.” Ch12 line 5, “Last year my wife and I hit twenty-four years married.” Prologue line 53 also places the boat-building history “over thirty years.” These cannot all be current marriage-duration statements. **Fix:** obtain the actual wedding year and distinguish time together from time married. If thirty years means together, use “We've been together over thirty years.” Date the retrospective Ch1 scene instead of silently changing the author's history. Ch13's imagined twelve-year couple does not compound this conflict.

### 2. High — contradictory anchor, Ch4 / Ch5

Exact: Ch4 line 79, “An apology pulls the nail.” Ch5 line 27, “The nail will stay in.” Ch5's preceding sentence is “An apology will end the fight.” Ch4 nails are harmful incidents and holes are remaining damage; Ch5 turns the nail into an unresolved hurt or trigger that apology leaves in place. **Fix:** retain Ch4's mapping and make Ch5 plain: “An apology may end the fight. The hurt that set it off still needs a conversation.” Also change Ch5 line 57, “That's where the nails accumulate,” to “That's how the unresolved hurts accumulate.” Do not turn the contradiction into more metaphor explanation.

### 3. Medium — mechanism vocabulary shifts, Ch1 / Ch2 / Ch4 / Ch6

Exact: Ch1 line 29, “And he had a word for the part of you that can sit in that gap for a moment before the response fires: *prohairesis*, your faculty of choice.” Ch2 line 48, “The Stoics called this the *hegemonikon*: the ruling faculty.” Ch4 line 59, “That's where your *prohairesis* lives. The governing faculty.” The two technical terms become near-interchangeable labels without explaining their relation. The OKF supports prohairesis as moral choice and Ch2's concept supports hegemonikon as ruling faculty; neither term is wholly undefined, but the book blurs the distinction. **Fix:** call prohairesis “your capacity to choose” consistently in Ch4; leave “ruling faculty” with Ch2 unless the author wants a precise relation explained.

Exact additional drift: Ch6 line 47, “It closes the gap instead of arguing about whose gap it is.” Ch1's gap is valuable response time, not marital distance to close. **Fix:** “It lets you help each other instead of arguing about who did more.”

### 4. High — promised mechanism missing from the claimed chapter, Ch8 / Ch9

Exact: Ch8 line 53, “It isn't about labor or money or recognition. It's about temperament, how much of the emotional room in the house one of you takes up at any given moment. The next chapter takes that one on.” Ch9 line 51, “Chapter 8 already showed you what happens when the calmer person in a marriage never says what they think.” Ch8 never delivered that analysis. Ch9 mainly delivers unspoken reciprocity and self-silencing, then asserts that the analysis already happened. **Fix:** replace Ch8's promise with “The next chapter looks at what happens when you stay quiet and expect something back.” Replace the Ch9 backward claim with a direct, scoped statement: “If you keep your concerns to yourself, the decisions can end up reflecting only what she has said.” Author should decide whether the separate temperament-asymmetry OKF deserves restoration elsewhere.

### 5. High — repair chapter reference is stale, Ch4 / current outline

Exact Ch4 line 97: “What's already in the fence is Chapter 16's work.” Current outline assigns repair to Chapter 17; Chapter 16 now concerns partnership. **Fix:** reference “the chapter on repair,” or update to 17 if numbered references remain house style. This is not a Ch13 defect.

### 6. Medium — repeated opening architecture across Parts, Ch2 / Ch5 / Ch8

Exact opening sentences: Ch2 line 3, “Chapter 1 gave you a gap: the three-second window between what happens and what you do about it.” Ch5 line 3, “Part I has been about the gap.” Ch8 line 5, “The last chapter took the private tally apart.” All recap prior instruction, then announce this chapter's topic. Ch8 repeats the Part I scaffolding in Part II and opens outside the reader's lived experience. **Fix Ch8:** “You've done something for the family, and a small criticism is still bothering you days later.” Begin the existing accumulation passage there; move the retrospective connection after recognition.

Secondary opening echo: Ch1 lines 11–13 puts the reader in bed replaying the incident; Ch11 line 3 ends “Now you're in bed, explaining to yourself why you stayed quiet.” Different mechanism, but the same late-night self-review staging recurs across Parts. **Low severity;** if variety is wanted, move Ch11's replay to the drive the following morning. Ch13 does not repeat this structure: ordinary exchange followed by the drawing comparison is distinct from Ch12's autobiographical question.

### 7. High — nontransactional choice versus guaranteed reciprocity, Ch7 / Ch9 / Ch12 / Ch13

Exact Ch12 line 61: “If you give her the bare minimum, the bare minimum is what comes back.” Ch9 diagnoses expecting repayment for agreeable behavior; Ch7 says what she gives is outside your control; Ch13 line 61 says “You offer time together because you want her company, without requiring a particular response.” Ch12's deterministic payoff promise revives the very transaction the arc has rejected. **Fix Ch12:** “When you stop making an effort, she may stop expecting it from you.” Keep the action and possible cost; remove the guaranteed matching return. Ch13's present wording is the coherent one.

### 8. Medium — repair versus permanence, Ch4 / Ch10 / Ch11

Exact Ch10 lines 98–99: “What the list protects is the part of a marriage that doesn't repair.” Ch4 describes incidents “that were never quite fully repaired”; Ch11 allows apology, repeated conversation, and help. Remembering harm is not the same as being unable to repair it. The absolute makes the book's repair work sound futile. **Fix:** “The list protects trust that can take years to rebuild.” Keep the warning without deciding every damaged marriage's future.

### 9. Low — river reuse changes the object, Introduction / Ch2 / Ch13

Introduction line 17: “The river is calm, but it is not weak.” Ch2 line 54 asks “can you keep flowing when the terrain changes?” Ch13 line 31: “Its water keeps changing, and so do we.” Earlier river = husband's adaptable self-command; here river = continual change in both spouses. These are **compatible**, not a contradictory metaphor. The risk is reader expectation, especially returning to the River inside the Sun part. Optional clarification: “Seneca uses a river to describe another fact about us: we keep changing.” Do not report metaphor reuse alone as a failure or remove a supported anchor merely for being reused.

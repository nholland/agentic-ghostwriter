## Direction

The chapter's center is **continuing the effort after marriage**. Communication helps, but it cannot carry the chapter. Pursuit is visible in attention, initiative, affectionate words, service, gifts, remembering, and romance. The author finds the current chapter short and wants room to develop these ideas.

## Revision instructions from the author

- Show presence in ordinary shared time: if she wants to watch a show, put the phone away; at dinner, attend to her instead of disappearing into your own thoughts.
- Acts of service should be willing and self-started. A wife who has to identify, assign, and remind him of each thoughtful task still carries the work of noticing it. Asking about preferences remains useful; making her manage every act is the problem.
- Treat the five love languages as an accessible list of ways to show effort, not as a scientific typology or a rule that each person has one fixed language. Give words of affirmation, quality time/presence, acts of service, gifts, and touch their due; do not collapse the chapter into talking.
- Address the shared-bank-account objection to gifts. The gift's value is the remembered preference and the thought of choosing it, not financial novelty. Avoid implying money or size makes it romantic.
- Ask the reader to name the last romantic thing he did. If weeks or months have slipped by, that is a concrete measure of neglected effort. Show small gestures: a note, breakfast, a planned date, a detail remembered.
- Explain that romance differs by marriage and does not require a grand gesture. Remembering should be more than a calendar alert: remember what she mentioned, what matters to her, and what she would enjoy now.
- Anniversaries and Valentine's Day can become automatic. A last-minute generic card and stale chocolate from Walgreens illustrates an occasion handled by rote; thought and advance attention matter more than expense. The author's earlier exclusion applied to chapter 12; this feedback explicitly proposes the scene for chapter 13.
- Remove the passage about noticing other women. Do not use it as a transition. The chapter's concern is taking one's wife for granted and continuing to choose her.
- Boredom belongs here as a risk of letting pursuit become routine. Include Esther Perel if her work can be used accurately; avoid presenting her clinical ideas as a measured causal result.

## Boundary with chapter 12

Chapter 12 already argues that romance requires deliberate practice and introduces the love languages as a loose practical list. Chapter 13 should demonstrate what continuing that practice looks like after courtship: present attention, unprompted service, thoughtful gifts, occasions that are not automatic, and ongoing curiosity. It can briefly recall the list but should not repeat chapter 12's explanation of it.

## Research check for the proposed factual claims

- Rosenfeld's study of heterosexual breakups found women wanted the divorce more often in the sample (roughly 69% of divorces), not merely “as much as men.” It measures reported desire for the breakup, not who filed paperwork. It does not establish that lack of romance caused those divorces. Primary paper: https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/a/7536/files/2015/06/Rosenfeld-Gender-of-Breakup-paper.pdf
- A General Social Survey analysis through 2016 found men more likely than women to report past-year and lifetime extramarital sex. This does not support saying women cheat as often as men. Definitions and self-report limits also matter. Primary study abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28517944/
- A 2009 longitudinal study found reported marital boredom predicted lower satisfaction nine years later. This is an association, not proof that boredom causes infidelity or divorce. Primary record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19389134/
- Esther Perel argues for novelty and curiosity in long relationships in her own words: https://www.estherperel.com/blog/letters-from-esther-53-novelty-is-a-powerful-aphrodisiac-heres-how-to-have-more
- A relationship-science review finds little evidence for the strict love-language matching theory. The list can still be a useful prompt to vary how one shows care. University summary of the research: https://www.utoronto.ca/news/little-evidence-linking-five-love-languages-healthy-relationships-researchers-say
- Research on household mental load supports the distinction between doing a task and carrying the responsibility to notice and coordinate it; avoid treating a particular woman's preference as universal. Primary study: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34177072/

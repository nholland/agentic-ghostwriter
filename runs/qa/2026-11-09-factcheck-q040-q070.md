## Q040: Pillemer's sample (Ch7 L25 vs Ch10 L82) and the 7,000-couple study

### What the OKF records

**Pillemer (Cornell Marriage Advice Project / *30 Lessons for Loving*, 2015).**
These concepts agree: `pillemer-communication-is-paramount`,
`pillemer-dont-keep-score`, `pillemer-five-major-stressors`,
`pillemer-long-marriages-learn-to-fight`, `pillemer-friendship-as-important-as-love`,
and the intake `sources/articles/pillemer-cornell-marriage-advice.md`:
- **700+ individuals in marriages of 30, 40, 50 or more years.** So the
  minimum is thirty years.
- **Average** marriage 44 years, or 43 by author-site and publisher copy
  (defect #12, open, to be settled from the book's introduction).
- **Method:** about 400 Americans aged 65+ answered a random national survey,
  and more than 300 took part in in-person follow-up interviews. So "700"
  counts survey respondents and interviewees together. They were not all
  interviewed.
- Evidence: `search-synthesis` plus publisher/author copy. No page of the book
  was opened. This is fine for a paraphrased figure, but not for a page number.

**The 7,000-couple study.** This is `park-et-al-2025-pay-me-back-exchange-orientation`.
Park, H. G., Johnson, M. D., Gordon, A. M., & Impett, E. A. (2025), "'Pay Me
Back'", *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, DOI
10.1177/01461672251330700, PMID 40326353. It uses 13 years of longitudinal data
from a national sample of couples **in Germany**, N = 7,293.
`verifiable / paraphrase / database-abstract`. Inbox #114 checked Ch7 L25 line
by line on 2026-10-03 (`runs/qa/2026-10-03-inbox-114/claim-3-ch07-7000-couples.md`).
Every clause holds as a paraphrase. "Drift" means lower satisfaction, not
separation. The 2026-09-08 external check asked for the German sample to be
named, and that is still not done in the prose.

### Which chapter is right

**Ch10's "thirty years or more" is right.** That is the sample's floor.

**Ch7's "married more than four decades" is a defect.** It turns an average into
a floor. It was introduced on purpose to sidestep defect #12 (43 vs 44), and the
defect log says so ("true under either figure"). That holds for the average, but
the sentence describes everyone interviewed, and some of them had been married
30-39 years. So the sidestep fixed one problem and created another.

**Ch10's "interviewed seven hundred" overstates the method slightly.** By the
OKF's record, about 300 were interviewed and about 400 were surveyed. Ch7's
"spent years interviewing" has the same problem in a softer form.

**Ch7's unnamed study is sourced, not a placeholder.** It is Park et al. 2025.
The drafter left the name out on purpose to match the voice register
(`chapters/ch07/draft.md` L50).

### Proposed replacements (author's call; Line Editor/Publisher apply)

**Ch7 L25, first sentence.** Replace
> There's a researcher named Karl Pillemer who spent years interviewing people who'd been married more than four decades, asking what actually worked.

with
> There's a researcher named Karl Pillemer who spent years asking more than seven hundred people who'd been married thirty years or more what actually worked.

**Ch7 L25, the study sentence.** Replace
> A separate, much larger study, thirteen years of data on more than 7,000 couples, found something that points in the same direction.

with
> A separate, much larger study, thirteen years of data on more than 7,000 couples in Germany, found something that points in the same direction.

If the Panel's "name the study" option is preferred:
> A separate, much larger study, led by Gideon Park and Emily Impett, followed more than 7,000 couples in Germany for thirteen years and found something that points in the same direction.

No `[PLACEHOLDER]` is needed. Every figure is on file.

**Ch10 L82-83.** Replace
> Karl Pillemer interviewed seven hundred people who'd been married thirty years or more about what puts the most strain on a marriage.

with
> Karl Pillemer asked more than seven hundred people who'd been married thirty years or more what puts the most strain on a marriage.

Leave the next sentence ("In-laws, money, and the way two people speak to each
other all came up.") unchanged. In-laws and money appear in publisher copy.
Communication rests on `pillemer-five-major-stressors` and
`pillemer-communication-is-paramount` (`search-synthesis`). This sentence makes
no counted claim, so it avoids the open five-vs-four problem.

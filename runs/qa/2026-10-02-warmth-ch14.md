# Warmth review: Chapter 14, "She Is Enough" (runs/ch14/refined.md, prose only)

**1. Warmth delivered.** Mostly yes. He'd go home and do three things. Before bed he'd write down one thing he valued about her that day (line 41). He'd tell her "what you saw and why it mattered to you" (line 47). He'd give her "a kinder answer, a sincere compliment, or time spent enjoying her company" (line 53). The chapter treats warmth as something he gives: "Nor does any of this require her to answer your affection on your schedule. If she's tired or distracted when you tell her, what you said is still true." (line 57).

**2. Ending.** The last ~150 words, lines 61-67:
> "Contentment doesn't settle a concern that needs an honest conversation, and gratitude doesn't make recurring hurt harmless. You may wish she asked about your day more, which is worth saying out loud and doesn't stop her from being enough. I don't have a fixed rule for when appreciation is enough and when you need to speak. You can love who she is while taking a problem seriously. / Most of your marriage happens in ordinary hours: a meal, a walk, a question about your day. They don't feel like much while you're in them. They are the life you chose. Slow down enough to taste them, because thankfulness is how you keep wanting what you already have. / Relish the ordinary days with her, because that is where the marriage is lived."

How he feels at the end: grateful, but he gets there through a wary stretch. Lines 57-61 are three paragraphs of caution in a row: hearing no, self-respect, recurring hurt, a problem. The last paragraph recovers the warmth. The close is positive, but it's weaker than the sentence before it. It restates "They are the life you chose" as a moral, which edges toward the two-endings rule. The "taste" sentence pays off the hunger-sauce passage. The close doesn't.
*Suggestion for the author:* end on the line he already wrote, set as its own paragraph: "Thankfulness is how you keep wanting what you already have."

**3. Armor audit.**
- "Your self-respect doesn't depend on her answer. What you decide is how to treat her while you feel it." (line 59). This brings a self-protective frame into the gratitude section. *Repair:* keep only the second sentence, or move the rejection material to Ch15.
- "You may wish she asked about your day more" (line 61). This presents her as the source of a need he isn't getting met, three sentences before the close. It also collides with "a question about your day" in line 65. *Repair:* cut it.
- "gratitude doesn't make recurring hurt harmless" (line 61). It's heavy wording right before the close. *Repair:* "gratitude doesn't replace a needed conversation."
- "When either of you notices a comparison, you can say so, and it can be heard well." (line 23). The skeptic's misreading: "so I tell her I noticed someone else." It's also passive: who hears it, and how? *Repair:* say what saying it looks like, or cut it.
- Not armor: "nails on a chalkboard" and "that bugs me too" (line 11). The author owns the irritation as his own stress, which is what the chapter needs.

**4. Gratitude and the ordinary.** This is the chapter's strength: thrifty, the gray hairs, the garden, the breakfast, "Anything she makes is enough for me," Marcus VI.48, and the nightly note. Controlling appetite gets one section (Seneca, lines 21-27), and it serves the gratitude rather than competing with it. *One sentence to add:* in line 65, make one of the ordinary hours his own detail from earlier, the thing she saved that you needed after all.

**5. Actionable.** Yes: the nightly note and telling her specifically. But he gets no words he could actually say to her. Add one sample line at line 47, built from the chapter's own examples (her patience with a child). Any example line is the author's to approve.

**6. Her as a person.** Strong. She was an attorney and a VP, is on sabbatical, hides her gray hairs, works in the garden, and has her own line about meals. Line 49 ("a person whose life includes more than meeting your needs") says it outright. The one weak spot is the generic "may miss feeling important" (line 47).

**7. Scores.** Warmth delivered 4; Positive ending 3; Actionable warmth 4; Gratitude for the ordinary 5; Respect for her 4; No-armor tone 4.
**Verdict: MOSTLY.** The body gives him real, warm things to do. The three caution paragraphs just before the end cool it, and the close is a restatement instead of the strongest line.

**8. Top five fixes.**
1. Lines 57-61: cut the three caution paragraphs to about two sentences, keeping "what you said is still true" and "You can love who she is while taking a problem seriously."
2. Line 67: close on "Thankfulness is how you keep wanting what you already have." (author's call).
3. Line 61: cut "You may wish she asked about your day more…"
4. Line 23: make "it can be heard well" plain, or cut it.
5. Line 65: name one ordinary thing she does (thrifty callback).

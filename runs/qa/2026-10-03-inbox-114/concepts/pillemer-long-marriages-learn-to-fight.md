# Pillemer — Long Marriages Don't Avoid Fighting, They Learn to Fight

The Cornell Marriage Advice Project — described as the largest in-depth interview
study of people in very long marriages — finds that elders explicitly teach *how*
to fight, not how to avoid conflict. Conflict management is a named category of
lessons; "not going to bed angry" appears as specific advice.

**Usable framing:** "The couples who stay married for fifty years didn't avoid
fighting." This is the book's claim — and 700 long-married elders are its
confirmation.

**Not usable framing (found 2026-10-03, inbox #114):** the sentence Chapter 5
prints next, that these couples "learned, over thousands of small fights, that
most of them didn't need a verdict", is not located in Pillemer. Search
summaries say the elders advise letting some things go and asking whether a
fight is worth it, but none of that was seen on a page. Keep it as the
author's own reading, or soften it; candidate wordings are in
`runs/qa/2026-10-03-inbox-114/claim-2-ch05-fifty-years.md`. The nearest
research is [Gottman — Perpetual Problems](/citations/gottman-perpetual-problems.md),
which is a different claim and unverified.

Table of contents, from the catalogue record (2026-10-03): Evening the odds
(finding a mate); Communication is the key (Talk, talk, talk; No one is a mind
reader; Mind your manners; All in good time; Three danger signs; Five secrets
for great communication); Getting through the hard parts (children, work-family
stress, in-laws, household chores, money); Keeping the spark alive (Think small
(and positive); Become friends; Sexuality; Give up grudges; Get help); Thinking
like an expert (Respect each other; Be a team; Make time; Lighten up; Accept
your partner as is; As long as you both shall live).

Study scale: 700+ individuals in marriages of 30, 40, 50, or more years
(combined ~40,000 years of marriage experience); a random national survey of
roughly 400 Americans aged 65+ plus in-person follow-up interviews with more
than 300 long-married individuals; average marriage length 44 years.

# Attribution warning

Do **NOT** attribute the phrase "don't sweat the small stuff" to Pillemer. That
phrase is Richard Carlson (*Don't Sweat the Small Stuff*, 1997) — different
author, different context.

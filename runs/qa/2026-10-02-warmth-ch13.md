# Warmth review, Chapter 13 "Pursue Her After You Have Her" (2026-10-02)

Read: 01-voice.md, 02-audience.md, part-3-warm-sun.md, ch13/refined.md (lines 1-61; the file has no Editor's Notes section). No other chapters read. Word counts not run.

**1. Warmth delivered.** Partly. The garden scene (l.31) is the warmest thing in the chapter: "she takes you out in the dark with her phone light to show you the corner she's picked." He'd leave wanting to ask her what she's into now and put the phone away tonight. Most of the chapter frames warmth as attention he has been withholding, so it reads as a debt to pay back more than a gift to give. Line 19 makes it a duty: "Duties are universally measured by relations... Noticing her the following spring took effort."

**2. Ending.** The last ~150 words (l.57-61) run from the Tsapelas study through "it tells you nothing about divorce... Guard against going through every shared hour as though there were nothing left to find out," then: "Put the phone down, and give her the evening you came to share."
He feels: resolved but a bit scolded. The last image he gets is a study caveat about divorce. The closing sentence is an instruction to stop doing something, which goes against the spec's "close on the chapter's own image" and ends on the opening's failure (the phone), not on her. *Suggestion for the author, one sentence:* "She's still telling you who she is, a little at a time, and tonight you get to listen."

**3. Armor audit.**
- l.37 "furniture in her own marriage: known, needed, walked past." Strong, but the section stacks guilt. Repair: keep the line and cut one of the three examples in l.39.
- l.39 "You end up with a wife who has gotten used to asking for less." This one shames him. Repair: follow it with a line on what comes back once he starts asking.
- l.41 "She's working out whether this is a change or just an evening." Makes her a judge he's on probation with. Repair: frame it as patience he gives, not a test he's sitting.
- l.57 "Guard against..." is threat language, plus the "divorce" caveat right before the close. Repair: move the caveats up and end the section on "The show you watch every week can be familiar and good."
- l.19 "that's how she knows you chose her" is a little transactional (proof delivered). Minor.

**4. Gratitude and the ordinary.** Present at l.49 ("the moment you looked over and thought she was beautiful. Familiar affection doesn't get less true because you've said it before") and l.57 ("familiar and good"). Elsewhere the chapter leans toward novelty: Perel's "new experience," the lake-town trip, "nothing left to find out." That pulls against the author's "relishing the mundane." Add one sentence at l.45, after "letting an hour belong to the two of you": something that names enjoying the ordinary hour with her as the point, not a step toward something better.

**5. Actionable.** Yes: put the phone in a drawer; ask what she'd be excited about this Saturday; ask about it again days later (l.31); tell her you noticed she handled something hard (l.49). He gets no line he could say out loud. l.49 is the natural place for one.

**6. Her as a person.** Mostly yes. The garden, the nursery and the fence give her a life of her own. "What it looks like from her side" shrinks her back to reactions to his neglect.

**7. Scores.** Warmth delivered 3. Positive ending 2. Actionable warmth 4. Gratitude for the ordinary 2. Respect for her 4. No-armor tone 3.
**Verdict: MOSTLY.** The method is warm and concrete, but the chapter's last 100 words leave him with a caveat and a correction instead of her.

**8. Top five fixes**
1. l.61: replace the closing with a sentence about her, not the phone (suggestion in item 2).
2. l.57: end the section on "familiar and good"; move "Guard against" and the divorce caveat earlier or cut them.
3. l.39-41: soften the guilt stack; recast "change or just an evening" as patience, not probation.
4. l.45: add one sentence of relish for the ordinary hour.
5. l.49: give him one line he could actually say to her.

**Do not touch:** the garden scene (l.27-31); "Asking is useful. Remembering the answer is what she notices." (l.33); "known, needed, walked past" (l.37); "Familiar affection doesn't get less true because you've said it before." (l.49); "You made time for her both nights, and that counts for something." (l.9)

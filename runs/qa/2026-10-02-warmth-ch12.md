# Warmth review: Ch12 "Romance Is a Discipline" (2026-10-02)

Read: 01-voice.md, 02-audience.md, part-3-warm-sun.md, ch12/refined.md (whole file; it has no Editor's Notes section). No other chapters. Line numbers are from refined.md. All counts are uncounted estimates.

**1. Warmth delivered.** Mostly. The middle gives him a real evening: ask about "the meeting she's been dreading, then asking again on Thursday" (L49), get gas in her car (L59), bring home "both" the book and the pastry (L61), leave a note. In that stretch warmth is something he gives. The frame around it slips. It calls romance "trying to win favor" (L37) and treats effort as a budget: "you only have so much in a day... the less is left over for her" (L23). The last section moves to warnings.

**2. Ending.** The last ~150 words are L79-87: "That sounds bleak for about four seconds... You can start again, and you may also owe her an apology. / When you stop making an effort, she may stop expecting it from you. / Marcus Aurelius ran the Roman empire... Do the necessary things instead. That list is harder to write, because it makes you say what actually counts. / She's been waiting to be one of the necessary things, and that muscle is one you can start using tonight."
How he feels: resolved, but with guilt in it. He's urged to act tonight, yet he leaves feeling he has kept her waiting. The final sentence points at tonight, which is right. But "She's been waiting" puts her in the place of the neglected one, and "muscle" pulls back an image the reader last saw at L33. Suggested replacement, for the author's call: *"Put her at the top of that list tonight, and do one small thing in her language before you go to bed."*

**3. Armor audit.**
- L81 "When you stop making an effort, she may stop expecting it from you." This is a warning, and it frames her as giving up on him. Repair: cut it.
- L77 "they come apart because the warmth stops." This is fear-led, and "the research only says they travel together" is a compressed line he has to decode. Repair: keep the fact, then turn quickly to what's still there.
- L79 "you may also owe her an apology." Shame dropped in with no setup. Repair: cut it, or say it as something he gives her, not a debt.
- L37 "Romance is trying to win favor with your wife." This reads as a transaction, and L51 has to walk it back. Repair: frame it as showing her she's still chosen.
- L23 "the less is left over for her." Warmth as a ration. Repair: frame it as where his attention goes, not how much he has left.
- L13 "because your kids will leave you". The reason for putting her first sounds like protecting an investment. L17 softens it well enough. Leave as is.

**4. Gratitude and the ordinary.** Present but thin. Good moments: "An ordinary night with both people in it" (L45), "Write down one memory that still makes you glad you married her" (L71), and "you owe it a real thank you" (L51, though "owe" makes it a duty). Mostly the chapter teaches him to put out more effort, not to enjoy what's already there. The heading "What's still there" (L77) never says what is still there. That is where a sentence fits. Suggestion for the author: after "You can start again," add one line that names her: the head in her lap, the grill, the laughing.

**5. Actionable.** Yes, as actions: the Thursday question, gas, an errand, the pastry and book, a note, breakfast before she's up, a written memory, a week's notice before an anniversary. There are almost no words he could actually say. The one ready line is to ask about her meeting.

**6. Her as a person.** Good. She is busy herself (L25). She asks without accusing (L9). She has a favorite evening, a meeting she dreads, a pastry she never stops for. She also gives: she rubs his head (L43). That scene is the chapter's best gratitude moment and it goes unused.

**7. Scores.** Warmth delivered 3. Positive ending 2. Actionable warmth 4. Gratitude for the ordinary 2. Respect for her 4. No-armor tone 3.
Verdict: **MOSTLY.** The middle gives him concrete warmth he can hand over tonight. The frame ("win favor", effort budget) and the closing stretch (decline, apology, "she may stop expecting") leave him wary rather than glad.

**8. Top five fixes**
1. L81: cut "When you stop making an effort, she may stop expecting it from you."
2. L87: replace the final sentence so it ends on giving, not on her waiting (suggestion above).
3. L79: after "You can start again," add one sentence naming what's still there, using the lap or the grill scene.
4. L37: change "Romance is trying to win favor" to giving language, which also lets L51's walk-back go.
5. L79: cut "and you may also owe her an apology."

**Do not touch:** L9 "She wasn't accusing me of anything. She was asking." / L43-45, the lap and the grilling evening / L49, the Thursday line / L59 "She can ask. She shouldn't have to ask for everything." / L61, the pastry and book / L71, the written memory.

# Chapter 12 defines resentment against Chapter 6's definition, and normalises in her what Chapter 7 condemns in him.

Two collisions with shipped chapters, both argument-level. (1) Ch6 states flatly: 'Resentment almost never fires on the real math. It fires on what's visible right now,' and closes on 'Resentment comes from scorekeeping.' Ch12 says: 'Then resentment. It's the ordinary word for what settles in when someone gives more than she gets.' That is resentment firing on the real math, which Ch6 says it almost never does. A reader who took Ch6 seriously feels the contradiction without being able to name it. (2) Ch7's evidence paragraph condemns tracking: people 'who kept close track of what they were owed, expecting their giving to be matched back, saw their satisfaction erode.' Ch12 says 'If you're a bare minimum partner, you get bare minimum in return. That's not her being petty. That's what anyone does.' Both are defensible on their own; together they read as a double standard the book never acknowledges - the tally is corrosive when he keeps it and natural when she does. That may be exactly what you believe, that reciprocity is a fact about people even though tracking it is corrosive, but the reconciliation currently happens nowhere and the reader is left to do it.

**Recommendation:** Fix (1) with the line the reader desk proposed - attribute the arithmetic to the situation rather than to her perception: 'Then resentment, which is what settles in when someone has been getting the leftovers long enough to stop expecting better.' Leave (2) as it is for now and note it for the whole-book QA pass: it needs one sentence somewhere in the book, and Ch12 at 1,234 words is not where that sentence fits.

The house manual · for the author

The Agentic Ghostwriter

You say what the chapter is, and whether it landed. The house does everything in between, and asks you only what only you can answer.

2touches per chapter: the interview and the verdict
12desks, two of them in the room with you
9gates a chapter passes before you read it
1command to remember: /gw

This is a book production house that runs on Claude Code. You are the expert the house recruited. You talk to the Publisher; the Publisher runs the desks. Every desk is either a person in the room with you, a cold sub-agent that never saw the conversation, or a script that cannot be talked past. This page says which is which, what each one does, and what you say to make it happen.

Section 1

How a chapter moves through the house

Seven stages. Pick one to see who runs it, what it reads, what it writes, and what stops it. The second toggle shows the parallel run used for Chapter 12: the book pipeline researches the chapter with you, and the house drafts from the same brief, cold.

    The brief is the automation boundary. Upstream of a good research brief you must be in the room, because that is where the chapter's ideas come from. Downstream of it, everything can run cold. Chapter 11 proved both halves: its draft ran cold and came back clean on every counted rule; its research could not have, because five of eight exchanges were you correcting the brief.

    Section 2

    Your two touches, and the inbox

    You are needed at the interview and at the verdict. Between them, a cold desk that hits something only you can decide does not guess and does not stop the whole chapter. It finishes everything that does not depend on the answer, marks the gap, and writes a question to the inbox with the context you need to answer it cold, without scrolling back. You answer whenever you next open a session.

    What reaches you, and nothing else

    • A gate that fails twice. Two rounds of cold revision, then the chapter is parked and you are told why.
    • A judgement call on style (the anti-slop categories B to F and J): a flag, never a silent edit.
    • Anything that is yours by definition: a story slot, a change to the outline's commission, a citation moving to verified, a Part rename, a decision to post something.
    • Any question a desk returns.

    A chapter that passes every gate arrives at the verdict with an empty inbox. That is the system working, not the system skipping you.

    There is a second, smaller pause in a normal run: after research, the Publisher shows you each content concept the Researcher wants to add to the book's knowledge ledger, because a concept capturing what you think is a claim only you can confirm. Yes, no, or a correction, and the run continues. Gap markers (a claim that needs a source nobody has found) never pause; the file is the flag.

    Section 3

    Who works here

    Twelve desks. Two run in the session as the same voice you are talking to, because a sub-agent cannot ask you anything. Nine run cold. One, the Archivist, reviews each session and proposes; it never applies. Open a desk to read its mandate: what it owns, what it reads, who checks its work, and what it must never do.

    Section 4

    The gates

    A producer's work is not done until a different reader says so, and that reader is a script or another desk, never the producer's own report. Self-reported counts were wrong on Chapters 9 and 10 and the Prologue, and once hid a live violation. So the counts come from a script, pasted verbatim, and the conformance verdict comes from a checker that is shown only the outline section and the prose, so it cannot be persuaded by the reasons behind a substitution.

    Section 5

    What to say

    You hold one door: /gw. On its own it shows a short menu built from the state oracle, so it can never be stale. With words after it, the Publisher reads what you mean and runs the matching skill. Nothing here needs remembering; when a phrase matters, the menu says it at the moment it matters. This table is the whole vocabulary, filterable.

    You say something likeWhat happensWho

    If nothing matches, the Publisher asks one question with the two or three likely readings and shows the menu. It never guesses into an action that writes, dispatches a desk, or spends your time. A wrong guess on "status" costs nothing; a wrong guess on "draft 12" costs a chapter run.

    Section 6

    A day with the house

    You open a session on the engine repo. The start hook prints the real clock, the book it found, and the board. You type /gw. It shows what is next and what is waiting on you. You type /gw 13, or just say what you want.

    The interview. The Developmental Editor opens with what the outline already commits the chapter to, so you react to something concrete. Then it works the gaps: the memory behind the story slot, where your own experience contradicts the outline's premise, the thing you believe that you have not seen written anywhere, which key point you now think is wrong. It pushes back once on anything that sounds received rather than yours. Your corrections are the product. It writes the record in your words, shows it to you, and hands off.

    The floor. Everything after that runs without you: the brief, its review, the draft, its counts and its conformance check, the refine, the anti-slop read, the distillation, the plate, the PDF. If you say run the floor, every other chapter with a cold stage pending moves at the same time. You can close the laptop.

    The verdict. You come back to a package: the PDF as a reader would meet it, the plate, the counts as the scripts printed them, the conformance rows, and the inbox. You read. You say whether it landed. If you want a line changed, edit 13 walks the chapter with you section by section, keeping your wording, and re-runs the counts afterwards because an edit can break a count that passed.

    When readers write back. Readers said… and paste it. The Publicist logs each response as a signal; the Publisher routes it by what it is: a lost reader to the Line Editor, an argued objection to the Skeptic, a factual challenge to the Fact-Checker, a gift (a story, a counter-example) to the Developmental Editor because it is your material now, new scope to the inbox because only you decide scope. A reaction with no argument is recorded, not routed.

    Closing. Say done. The Publisher tells you in two lines where your work is: which branch, whether it is pushed, whether it is on main, and offers to put it on main. Nothing moves main unless you say so in words. Once a session that touched the work, the Archivist reviews it cold and proposes; you apply or you do not.

    Section 7

    Where things live

    Two repositories, permanently. The book repo is the book. The engine repo is how books get made. In practice you go to neither by hand: you open a session on the engine, and the desks read and write the right places. The rule only matters when you are editing by hand, and then it is one question: is this about this book, or about how books get made?

    Section 8

    Sessions and safety

    None of this is a desk. It is three hooks and a script, because every git failure in the old pipeline's incident record was a model following rule text, and every fix was a check that ran on its own.

    Any write to the ledgertimestamp guardRefuses a hand-written concept or a stamp that is not today. Every wrong date in the record was typed by hand; every date read from the clock was right.

    What the house will never do. Invent a citation, a statistic, or a story. Mark a citation verified; that is yours, against your physical copy. Let a search transcribe a quotation. Post anything. Write the book's constitution, its chapters, or its manifest. Resolve a question that was yours. Edit its own rules; the Archivist proposes and you apply. Claim a check passed that did not run.

    Section 9

    Chapter 12, in parallel

    The design said each desk should be proven on a real chapter. That cannot mean running the interview twice, because a second interview already knows the first one's answers and your time is the most expensive thing in the house. So the two halves are tested separately, and Chapter 12 tests the cold half: the book pipeline ships it as normal, and the house re-runs everything after the brief from the same file. Zero extra time from you, zero risk to the book.

    1. now
      The board says Chapter 12 is at the interview.

      Because no brief exists yet. Over in the book repo, /book-resume says the same thing in its own words: /book-chapter-research 12, with you in the room.

    2. the brief lands
      The board flips on its own.

      The oracle sees chapters/ch12/research.md in the book repo and nothing in runs/ch12/, and its next action becomes /gw 12 --shadow. Nobody has to remember that this was the plan.

    3. you say shadow 12
      The house drafts cold from your brief.

      Ghostwriter, the counts, the Conformance Checker, the Line Editor, the Anti-Slop Reader, the checker again, the distillation, the plate, the PDF. The plan-only review still reads the brief and its gap list is recorded as a finding: if the brief was thin and the book pipeline's chapter shipped fine, you closed those gaps live, and that is exactly the measurement.

    4. meanwhile
      The book pipeline drafts and refines its own Chapter 12 with you.

      Nothing the house does can touch it. The two never write the same file.

    5. both refined
      /gw-bakeoff 12, blind.

      Two neutrally named variants, a counted comparison labelled by variant, the checker run on each without being told which is which, and a sealed mapping. You read cold and answer four questions, the one that matters most being did either say something you did not tell it? The script refuses to unseal until your verdict is written, because you are the judge and also the person who wants the new system to win.

    6. then
      One honest row in the findings, win or lose.

      Chapter 13 tests the warm half: the Developmental Editor leads the interview. The baseline is Chapter 11's five rounds of correction.

    Section 11

    Glossary

    The board
    What the state oracle prints: both pipelines, every chapter's stage, the inbox count, parked questions, what could run cold right now, and the one next action. It is computed from files, never reasoned about, so it cannot be stale.
    The brief
    The research brief, research.md. The automation boundary: everything upstream needs you; everything downstream runs cold. Its only real test is whether someone who never read the interview could write the chapter from it alone.
    Cold
    Running as a sub-agent with clean context. It never saw the conversation, so it cannot be persuaded by the reasons behind a choice, and it cannot ask you anything, which is why anything only you can decide goes to the inbox instead.
    The floor
    Every cold stage that can run right now, across every chapter, dispatched at once. Say run the floor.
    Gap marker
    A citation concept whose whole content is "this claim needs a source and nobody has found one." Written immediately; the file is the flag. Contrast a content concept, which captures what you think and is shown to you first.
    The inbox
    Questions a cold desk could not decide. Each carries the context you need to answer it cold and what your answer unblocks. A parked question is different: something you chose to defer, with a trigger that brings it back.
    The ledger
    The book's knowledge bundle under okf/: frameworks, stories, citations, reader signals, findings. Every citation carries three independent axes: its status, whether it is quoted verbatim, and what evidence was actually looked at.
    Plate
    One diagram per chapter, drawn by the Designer in the book's existing visual style, showing the chapter's mechanism and nothing else. Never blocks a chapter.
    Shadow run
    The house running a chapter's cold stages from a brief the book pipeline produced, into its own runs/, in parallel and without touching the book. The Chapter 12 plan.
    The transcription rule
    Search may locate a source or flag a defect. It may never transcribe a quotation. A verbatim quote is confirmed only by a page someone actually opened. Nine citation defects reached printed prose before this rule existed.
    The two touches
    The interview and the verdict. The design's whole claim, and unproven until a chapter goes through.
    Verdict
    You, reading the chapter as a reader would (the PDF), with the counts, the conformance rows and the inbox beside it, and saying whether it landed.

    Built on the same principle as the pipeline it grows out of: good books come from good systems. The change is where the discipline lives. Rules that can be checked moved into checks. Judgement moved into desks with a mandate. You keep the two things only you can do: say what the chapter is, and say whether it landed.
